## Chapter purpose, theme and central conflict

**Purpose:** Make the first repeatable procedure for hearing contested landing arrangements earn its existence through an injury and a real appeal. The immediate problem is not an abstract desire for government. A crewman may be blamed for following the boat owner's account, a cargo owner may lose a promised delivery, and a second use of the step could damage the bank or interfere with Dala's fishing.

**Central question:** When two witnesses agree on the cuts but disagree on the promise, who may decide what the record means, and who bears the cost while the owner is absent?

**Theme:** A mark can preserve an agreement and conceal its limits. **Subthemes:** witness reliability, absent parties, household consent, memory, labor, practical authority, and the cost of waiting for an appeal.

## Six-scene causal architecture

1. **The Journey That Wasn't Given.** Open at the next rising water. The crewman returns expecting a second trip and has already arranged a load. Raya has not cleared the outer edge after high water. The keeper shows the disputed strip; Ache cannot use his own memory to order the boat in. **Turn:** the crewman refuses to carry the blame for what his employer told him; the cargo owner may lose a buyer if the journey is delayed.

2. **The Man Beyond the Bend.** The boat owner returns with his own account. He remembers touching the two short cuts and hearing that two loads could be brought; the cargo owner insists she paid for one journey carrying two baskets. The keeper reads back what she said; the owner contests whether he heard the limiting words. **Turn:** Ache's first impulse to rule for the keeper is challenged by a witness who was physically working at the bow and could not hear the entire exchange. Neither witness becomes a liar by narrative convenience.

3. **What the Cuts Leave Out.** Ache, the keeper and a skeptical boat worker test ways to distinguish **journey, number of goods, waiting time, payment, and permission to use the path**. Do not introduce alphabetic literacy, a universal written contract, or a professional court without evidence. The new marks are provisional aids to spoken agreement, with each participant required to repeat their own understanding. **Turn:** a seemingly clear record still fails when a person whose space is affected, Dala, was absent.

4. **A Claim Beside the Account.** Dala's old damage claim is found beside the new cargo strip; someone assumes the latest work or a future cord has settled it. Dala rejects the inference. The basket maker's trial fiber is not yet proof of repair, and Raya's household covering remains separately owed. **Turn:** Ache must correct the public account without erasing the evidence of the mistaken reading, and accept a cost in time or trade.

5. **A Rule Someone Can Refuse.** Test a limited procedure on an actual small-vessel request: Raya examines the step, Dala controls access beside her rack, the owner sets transport terms, and the keeper records the result with two witnesses. A worker refuses a service obligation that Ache has not earned the right to impose. **Turn:** the safer procedure delays a shipment and the dissatisfied owner considers using the far bank. Avoid a grand tax code or automatic claim over households.

6. **The Question Left Open.** Resolve the **specific second-journey claim** by hearing the boat owner and affected parties; do not retroactively authorize a dangerous landing. The parties may accept one limited repeat test or choose to trade elsewhere, but any agreement must be explicit about who pays for waiting and who can appeal. Establish a modest repeatable practice: distinct marks for goods and journeys, oral readback in each party's own terms, named witnesses, and a place to keep disputed strips visible. **Ending:** a visitor or neighboring household tests whether Maynila will honor the same process when Ache's own interest is at stake, setting up Chapter 7's political reaction. No permanent council, fleet or dynasty is created.

## Character arcs and independent stakes

**Ache:** begins confident that everyone heard the same words; learns his own apparent agreement was incomplete because he did not check what the boat owner understood. He must choose between speed and a costly hearing. His judgment remains limited to people and access that have actually accepted it.

**Keeper:** protects the distinction between goods and journeys but must admit that a correct tally can be misunderstood. She retains custody of the original strip and refuses to overwrite it merely to vindicate herself.

**Boat owner and crewman:** have distinct interests. The owner needs paid work and may honestly misunderstand the number of journeys; the crewman risks lost wages or blame for carrying out instructions he received. Do not treat either as a plot device for Ache's moral lesson.

**Cargo owner:** wants her goods delivered on the terms she paid for. She has no obligation to finance a second journey or donate her goods to Maynila's hall.

**Dala, Raya, Amaya and the basket maker:** their previously recorded rights and debts remain independent of this cargo dispute. Dala may refuse path use; Raya controls the repair's safety judgment; Amaya's household stores are not a public warehouse; the basket maker's material is hers until the earned exchange and Dala's inspection are complete.

**Sulad:** may appear only if independently returned from paid boat work. His labor, equipment and testimony cannot be requisitioned by Ache. Avoid convenient omniscience or sudden permanent appointment.
