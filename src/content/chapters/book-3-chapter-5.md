## Continuity gate: the acquaintance was never an ally

**Development outline only.** Book I Chapter 7 ends with Mulaw, a young visiting boat leader at an **unnamed first landing**, giving Ache his name and advice to ask the buyer about future boats. Mulaw explicitly says he may never return; he gives **no passage, alliance, title, military aid, political allegiance or promise to Ache**. Nima protects cloth belonging to his absent sister, and Mulaw's boat has obligations independent of Ache. Book I Chapter 8 does not establish a later Mulaw alliance. The Universe character record `lakanmulaw` uses **Mulaw** as the published name; `Lakanmulaw` and `Datu Lakanmulaw` are legacy aliases, not titles or historical claims. Do not place his first meeting in Palawan, Panay, Cebu or Mindoro by retroactive inference.

Book III Chapter 4, proposed *What the Bank Could Keep*, leaves Maynila with a **repaired upper step for a single small vessel**, not a completed lower mooring post, fortress or naval base. Dala's broken net, lost catch and time are unpaid; Raya's household compensation and Ache's cloth debt to Amaya remain open. A large cargo owner has diverted trade across the river. The Chapter 3 no-through-passage-fee agreement still governs. **Before drafting**, verify Chapter 4's public merge and deployment; if publication changes its ending, reconcile this outline.

Book II Chapter 10 establishes Harun's privately owned trading voyage, Tamin's limited first-leg arrangements and Isan's independent conditions. None is a pledged Bruneian fleet or a permanent political ally. Book III Chapters 1–3 establish Ache's limited, negotiated Maynila authority and Tondo's independent claims. Do not invent a universal tributary network or a guarantee of military support.

## Chapter purpose and central conflict

Ache wants to reopen cargo relationships that could help Maynila pay for its unfinished landing work and outstanding claims. A name remembered from exile seems to offer a route into those relationships. Each trader, crew and household, however, has its own obligations and can refuse him. **Central question:** What does Ache owe the people who once helped him, and what can he legitimately ask of them now that he speaks from Maynila's hall?

**Theme:** a remembered favor changes meaning when one party gains authority. **Subthemes:** reciprocity, memory, commercial trust, kinship language, the difference between acquaintance and alliance, and the cost of recording an ambiguous promise.

## Six-scene causal architecture

1. **The Claim Beside the New Step.** Open with Dala's unpaid cord, catch and lost fishing time, Raya's still-unsettled household payment, and Amaya's cloth debt. The repaired upper step works for one small boat at a time; the lower berth and large cargo trade remain elsewhere. Ache wants to use new trade to cover old claims. **Turn:** a buyer challenges whether he may promise landing priority or use a future cargo share that the keeper has not received.

2. **The Name on the Boat.** A visiting buyer or crew member mentions a person who might know Mulaw's route. Ache remembers the Book I Chapter 7 encounter accurately: Mulaw offered a name and practical advice, **not** a boat or future alliance. The person may carry an inquiry but cannot guarantee delivery or return. **Turn:** Ache must pay for the inquiry without spending the keeper's landing share or Nima's sister's property.

3. **Two Accounts of a Favor.** A returning trader, messenger or independently verified old contact describes earlier assistance differently from Ache. One side remembers paid carrying or shelter; the other remembers hospitality or a personal debt. Use a specific object and witness, not a speech about diplomacy. **Turn:** Ache's first proposed repayment would transfer an obligation to someone who never agreed to it; that person refuses.

4. **The Cargo That Could Leave.** A merchant offers trade through Maynila only if the upper step, storage, and priority terms are practical. The cargo owner who diverted his fish has a rational counteroffer. No new charge on mere passage and no unapproved use of Dala's fishing place. **Turn:** a seemingly generous promise would disadvantage another household, and the keeper makes the competing claims visible.

5. **What Mulaw Did Not Promise.** If Mulaw is reached, let him arrive or answer for his own reasons, with Nima and the absent sister's cloth obligation intact; he may decline Ache's request. If no credible contact is earned, the scene may instead resolve Ache's attempt to find him and show the limits of a remembered name. **Turn:** Ache chooses a narrower reciprocal offer that does not claim command over a boat, crew or place.

6. **The Words They Could Carry.** Witness only terms actually accepted: who supplies what, where cargo can land, how a disputed claim is heard, and when the agreement expires. Preserve outstanding Dala/Raya/Amaya obligations if not actually paid. Some trade may return, but no general alliance, treaty or naval force is created. **Ending:** an absent party or a disputed reading of a mark exposes the need for more reliable records and appeals, setting up Chapter 6.

## Character and subplot progression

**Ache:** begins hoping his past journey can solve present shortages; learns that gratitude and recognition do not entitle him to another person's boat, time or property. His choice should cost him a desired shipment or concession. He ends with a smaller, testable relationship.

**Mulaw:** the published character is a fictional boat leader with a named crew and independent route, not an attested datu or a lakan. His return is a plot possibility to earn, never a prerequisite to the chapter's resolution. If present, he can refuse and retain a credible reason to travel elsewhere.

**Nima and the absent sister:** her cloth remains hers. Nima's duty to return it is not a resource Ache can borrow, and his choices should matter beyond Ache's nostalgia.

**Dala, Raya, Amaya and the keeper:** Chapter 4's unpaid obligations must remain legible. The keeper cannot treat expected cargo as received revenue. Amaya's household stores are not a port treasury. Dala can reject an arrangement that blocks her fishing work. Raya's labor and family need explicit terms.

**Commercial subplot:** distinguish cargo ownership, paid carrying, a temporary landing concession, a gift, a household obligation and a promise to inquire. An oral agreement may have different witnesses; do not resolve a disputed claim by making Ache's recollection automatically authoritative.

## Historical and editorial gates

These negotiations and named encounters are fictional. Verify any period-specific claims about maritime ranks, commodity values, language or routes before presenting them as documentary facts. Tomé Pires provides regional commercial context, not Ache's itinerary or Mulaw's identity. Later Spanish Tagalog accounts cannot establish a uniform early-sixteenth-century rule of service. The original Aganduru Spanish passages on Ache remain uncollated; do not use them to supply a marriage, fleet, or guaranteed foreign backing.

Avoid modern treaty or contract jargon in Ache's dialogue, a convenient reunion that cancels past refusals, a newly invented geographical itinerary, and a six-scene series of speeches. Each scene needs an object, a contested decision, a character with a right to refuse, and an irreversible cost or changed option. Preserve first-person Ache POV, his fallibility, and the 30 core + Book III bonus 11–12 architecture.

**Title review:** *Old Alliances, New Debts* overstates Mulaw's historical role in Ache's life. Retain as a functional working title until the prose yields a more concrete, layered reader-facing title. Candidate direction: a name remembered from the first landing, not a title or oath.

## Handoff to Chapter 6

The existing Chapter 6 outline assumes territory, defenses, alliances, a wife and a governing son without establishing their chronology or authority. **Do not inherit those assumptions automatically.** Chapter 6 should begin with the bounded landing and trade arrangements actually achieved, then earn any larger institution, marriage or dynastic succession through dates, characters and source limits. The immediate problem is whose words a tally records and who may contest them, not the launch of a centralized navy or court.
