## Chapter purpose

Pay off the trilogy's travel relationships by showing that allies remember promises and have agendas of their own.

## Opening state

Ache now needs resources, recognition, and regional relationships to support a stronger Maynila.

## Immediate objective

Renew useful alliances without allowing old debts to dictate Maynila's policy.

## Main plot movement

Relationships formed during flight and campaign years return as negotiations over trade, protection, access, or military cooperation.

## Subplots

- **Lakanmulaw:** tests whether Book I trust matured into political alliance.
- **Old promises:** bargains made under desperation now acquire costs.
- **Ache's reputation:** other communities judge the ruler he became, not the exile they once met.

## Conflict and stakes

Ache risks treating friendship as obligation. Allies risk becoming dependencies or rivals if their expectations are ignored.

## Themes and subthemes

**Theme:** favors become politics.

**Subthemes:** reciprocity, memory, debt, friendship, autonomy.

## Character progression

### Ache

**Begins:** assumes shared history creates natural alignment.

**Pressure:** allies insist on independent interests.

**Ends:** learns to distinguish affection, gratitude, treaty, and subordination.

### Lakanmulaw

**Begins:** old ally with history.

**Progression:** demonstrates that friendship survives best when his autonomy is respected.

**Ends:** relationship becomes more political but potentially more durable.

## Turning point

Ache must honor, renegotiate, or refuse an old obligation that conflicts with current priorities.

## Resolution and ending state

The network is strengthened through negotiation rather than absorption.

## Setup and payoff

Pays off Book I Chapters 7–8 and Book II's widening alliance network.

## Handoff

A wider network increases the need for standardized rules, records, and administration in Chapter 6.

## Merchant and craft subplot: favors become obligations

When old allies come collecting on promises, their accounts differ. A gift of cloth, safe anchorage or a crew's assistance may be remembered as kinship, paid service or a debt. An interpreter or locally recognized witness must decide whether a pledge was conditional. **Turn:** Ache chooses to honor a costly old commitment rather than insist his own version controls. **Handoff:** his demand for more consistent records in Chapter 6.
