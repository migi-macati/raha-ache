Ache lives long enough to see the political world he built transformed by Spanish rule.

- The chapter follows the consequences of accommodation after conquest rather than replaying the battle of the previous bonus chapter.
- Ache's baptism is treated as a documented event while motive and interior belief remain matters for careful fictional treatment.
- Succession becomes immediate as Ache's health and age close the space for delay.
- The 1572 evidence says his property passed to a legitimate nephew; the archive does not silently treat that sentence alone as proof that the nephew was Sulayman.
- Ache dies in 1572.

The ending returns to the trilogy's central question of legacy: what survives when the ruler, the political order, and even the meaning of Maynila have changed?

**Bonus function:** close Ache's historical life without making his death the resolution of Book III's main invented plot.
