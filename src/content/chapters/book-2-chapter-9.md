## Chapter purpose

Show that Ache's deepest Book II growth is not the ability to win, but the ability to recover after failing.

## Opening state

Ache is free again but his force is damaged and his authority has been shaken.

## Immediate objective

Regather survivors, restore operational order, and decide whether the homeward campaign should continue.

## Main plot movement

Ache reconstructs enough command capacity to proceed toward Luzon.

## Subplots

- **Officer trust:** followers assess whether Ache still deserves command.
- **Brunei versus home:** retreat is a rational option.
- **Earlier mentors/rivals:** their lessons and judgments should echo even if they are offstage.
- **European encounter:** becomes memory and warning rather than immediate antagonist.

## Conflict and stakes

Ache can preserve pride by pretending the defeat changed nothing, or preserve command by openly learning from it.

## Themes and subthemes

**Theme:** leadership after failure.

**Subthemes:** accountability, resilience, trust, shame, recovery.

## Character progression

### Ache

**Begins:** humiliated and tempted to restore authority through performance.

**Pressure:** followers need explanation, order, and credible decisions.

**Ends:** earns renewed trust by accepting failure and reorganizing around reality.

### Followers/officers

**Progression:** loyalty becomes conditional on Ache's response to defeat rather than on his earlier victories.

## Turning point

Ache chooses accountability and practical recovery over blame or reckless immediate revenge.

## Resolution and ending state

The force can continue. Ache is a more mature commander than he was before capture.

## Setup and payoff

Pays off the lesson of Chapter 4: this time, defeat produces disciplined adaptation.

## Handoff

Chapter 10 becomes a free choice rather than forced motion: return to Brunei or continue to Luzon.
