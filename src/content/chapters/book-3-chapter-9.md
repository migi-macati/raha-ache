### Exact Chapter 8 inheritance

Raya's upper landing step remains unsafe, with rotten wood marked for cutting; the old stones are tide-dependent, and the sounding stake is not a mooring post. Dala controls the path above her net. Nali permits no general use of her upper-root path and has not opened the rack path. Her rack was already broken before Ranu's journey, with no identified culprit. Bina independently reported Nali's correction and declined a second commission; Kito controls whether he accepts new pole work. Iya can report what she hears but will not speak for her sister or promise a return journey. The keeper retains two separate memory strips, one for the witnessed crossing and one for Bina's report of Nali's correction. They are **not** transferable licenses.

The cord owner advertised an available crossing too broadly; a pot owner paid carriers and a boatman for an unused morning and has a possibly cracked vessel. Another shipment used a longer channel. The cord owner openly recommends another landing to preserve his own trading relationships. He still favors voluntary paid watchers but cannot impose a passing charge. Ache publicly accepted the correction, losing traffic. Amaya's one-cloth claim is unpaid; Tesa sold the suitable length to another customer and offered only possible carrying work. Ache holds Amaya's old measure, not a replacement cloth. Raya's timber, Dala's frame/lost catch, and other household obligations remain separate.

## Chapter purpose and conflict

A commercial alliance forms around **predictable access to trade**, not abstract resistance to an invented state. Several boat owners, carriers and merchants decide to direct business to a rival landing or bargain collectively for a reliable schedule. Their refusal threatens Ache's practical standing because his landing cannot promise the use of privately controlled paths and unsafe wood. Some members want honest terms; another sees an opportunity to impose a fee under a borrowed claim of authority. Their interests diverge when the cost of an alternative channel becomes clear.

Ache's temptation is to recover traffic by giving a promise beyond his own power. His alternative is slower: earn one specific agreement, supply actual labor, and accept that a merchant may still leave. Chapter 9 should **escalate** the conflict into a coalition with leverage; Chapter 10 must resolve the book's main crisis with a concrete, earned outcome, not a newly invented battle or empire.

**Theme:** Trust can be withdrawn collectively. **Subthemes:** information that travels farther than its witnesses; market losses; responsibility for repairs; consent and wage costs; competing landings; rival accounts of what an agreement means; political legitimacy without guaranteed obedience.

## Six-scene causal architecture

1. **The boat that passes.** Ache sees a cargo vessel use the other channel while Raya tests the rotten step. A carrier explains why waiting for four separate permissions is costly, and why the alternative landing's owner can offer a firm berth. Ache cannot contest a boat owner's right to choose. **Turn:** the lost traffic has become an organized offer elsewhere, not a single offended trader.

2. **The owners' meeting.** The cord owner brings two traders and a paid boat worker to state conditions under which they might return: inspected wood, an agreed waiting place, known wage liability and honest notice before a boat leaves. The group does not speak for all merchants or Tondo. One trader asks for a universal fee; another objects because the watchers would be paid from cargo he does not own. **Turn:** Ache cannot buy unanimity with one convenient mark.

3. **What a repair takes.** Raya identifies a specific timber and labor requirement, but its owner has another use for it. Dala refuses to have her net path converted into a waiting area. Ache must seek permission for materials and offer his own time, while Amaya presses him to keep the carrying work he needs to pay her cloth debt. **Turn:** a promised public repair competes with a private obligation he has already failed to meet.

4. **A story reaches Tondo.** Salin or another independently established visitor reports how the proposed watcher fee is being retold. Limit the speaker's authority to the household they actually represent. Ache discovers that a merchant has used the keeper's marks as evidence of a right she never granted. The keeper insists on preserving the disputed report instead of cutting it away. **Turn:** the coalition's public case contains a genuine loss and an opportunistic false claim.

5. **The withheld boat.** A time-sensitive load reaches the river; the boat owner and pole-handler separately decline a journey without pay or a safe landing. Ache can either pretend the step is open or let the load go elsewhere. He refuses the false promise and makes a bounded offer of work that he can personally deliver. **Turn:** the alliance has leverage, and Ache's loss is visible to households whose support he needs.

6. **The terms on the bank.** The merchants present a joint demand, but their terms split over who bears the waiting cost and whether Nali's and Dala's paths can be used. Ache publicly identifies which terms he can accept, which require others' consent, and what work remains undone. A dissatisfied owner departs with a real grievance and a practical plan for the next market. **Ending:** the coalition is sufficiently organized to force a Chapter 10 reckoning; no new tax, office, military command, treaty or completed repair is conjured at the close.

## Character and subplot progression

**Ache:** begins fearing the loss of traffic more than the loss of a truthful account. He learns that acknowledging a merchant's cost does not authorize him to charge other households for it. His promised work and unpaid cloth become tests of whether anyone should trust his next offer.

**Raya:** can judge and perform the repair, but may refuse unsafe shortcuts or unpaid work. **Dala:** protects her net and its access, even when a repaired landing would benefit the market. **Keeper:** can remember and distinguish claims but cannot issue licenses for other people's property. **Amaya:** demands a usable replacement cloth on her own terms and refuses to become the unconsulted lender of public repair materials.

**Cord owner and traders:** distinguish real losses, personal reputation and opportunism. Give at least one merchant a credible case for moving trade and another a reason to distrust a universal charge. A paid crew member must retain the ability to refuse new labor.

**Nali, Iya, Bina, Kito:** if referenced, preserve their earlier limits. Bina is no standing messenger; Iya cannot bind Nali; no second crossing becomes authorized merely because Chapter 8 had a corrected account.
