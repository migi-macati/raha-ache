## Continuity reset and purpose

**Development outline only.** Book III Chapter 3, *Where the Wood Stood*, ends with a witnessed replacement of a displaced channel marker, an actual crossing, unsettled restitution and a Maynila owner openly opposing Ache's limits on tolls. Tondo's households retain independent boats and claims. The landing still lacks a dependable lower mooring post, regular watchers, secure storage and an agreed way to pay for repairs. Amaya controls the hall's stores, the keeper accounts for landing shares, and Sulad remains an independent paid worker. Harun departed on his own trading vessel. Ache has no army, permanent fleet, Bruneian military guarantee or right to requisition every craftsperson.

**Chapter purpose:** turn a temporary passage arrangement into something that works through ordinary tides, absences and refusals. Ache discovers that a protected landing requires labor, timber, food, maintenance and the right of workers and owners to challenge how these are obtained. The working title *The Making of the Kota* describes an ambition; the chapter need not finish a fortress or establish historical artillery.

**Central question:** Can Ache make Maynila more secure without turning yesterday's voluntary agreement into compulsory labor and a new charge that its users never accepted?

## Six-scene architecture

1. **The Post That Would Not Hold.** A loaded vessel arrives before the promised mooring post is braced. The new sounding stake cannot be used as a tie point. A cargo owner demands immediate unloading; the keeper and a boat worker refuse to repeat the mistake of Chapter 3. Ache must find a temporary berth and admit the landing's practical weakness. **Turn:** an owner threatens to move business to another shore unless repairs have a date and accountable suppliers.

2. **Wood Belonging to Someone.** A proposed palisade or protected storage yard needs timber. The obvious stockpile is already pledged to hall repairs, households and boat owners. Amaya refuses to surrender it, while Dala's family still has claims on landing labor. Ache must negotiate a small, separately supplied first section. **Turn:** a supplier asks whether a ruler's request can be refused without losing access to the steps.

3. **The Maker's Price.** A skilled carpenter or metalworker examines the proposed bracing and storage protections. The maker rejects a design that assumes free labor, unseasoned timber or unavailable copper. Sulad can advise on water and boats but does not become Ache's officer by default. **Turn:** a promised delivery fails because the person who made it was never consulted. If Panday Pira is used, establish chronology and sources first; otherwise retain an independently characterized fictional maker.

4. **The Water Tests It.** A high tide or sudden heavy load strains the unfinished works. A support fails; a worker is injured or a household's path is obstructed. The danger arises from a concrete choice about material, timing or wages, not a generic storm spectacle. **Turn:** Ache must stop construction or accept responsibility for continuing it.

5. **The Work No One Owed.** The owner who opposed Chapter 3's passage settlement proposes collecting from every passing boat or calling on household service obligations. The keeper, workers and far-bank users contest both. Ache chooses a smaller undertaking funded by identifiable consenting owners and paid labor, with a witnessed right to challenge disputed claims. **Turn:** some influential owners withdraw support, making the protection less extensive but its terms credible.

6. **What the Bank Could Keep.** A limited mooring and protected storage arrangement survives an actual working test. The hall's supplies remain under Amaya; the keeper retains a separate count; a craftsperson receives payment or an enforceable witnessed obligation. A dissatisfied owner can still depart, Tondo remains independent, and the unfinished defenses require future maintenance. **Ending:** Ache inherits a practical system and its debts rather than a completed kota. Chapter 5 can press earlier alliances and promises against these new demands.

## Character arcs and subplots

**Ache:** begins treating construction as a command he can issue after the marker settlement. Learns that a plan is only real when materials, makers and payers agree, and that stopping an unsafe job may be his most consequential decision. Ends with less visible construction than he wanted, but a functioning first section.

**Amaya and the keeper:** hold their separate authority over household stores and landing shares. They can challenge Ache in front of workers and owners. Neither becomes an automatic treasury or administrative subordinate.

**Sulad:** offers technical help only on agreed terms, retains his own livelihood and may refuse a role that would make him responsible for every vessel's safety.

**Makers and dependents:** their skill and obligations affect the design. A craftsperson's consent, household food and injury compensation matter as much as defensive ambition. Do not use a worker's suffering merely to prove Ache's wisdom.

**Trade and status subplot:** different owners want safe storage and predictable access, but disagree on who should pay. A powerful household may attempt to convert a temporary favor into permanent service. Keep the dispute grounded in specific people, objects and words, not modern policy language.

## Historical evidence gates

The **1570 relation** describes guns, metals and unfinished molds at Maynila; this does not establish an early-sixteenth-century workshop run by Panday Pira or prove the date of Ache's first fortification. The named Panday Pira subplot is **conditional** on a defensible chronology and direct source review. Do not portray him as a verified participant in this earlier chapter merely because a later tradition names him. A fictional craft expert is the default if that gate remains unmet.

Treat timber barriers, boat repairs, storage, tally strips, work agreements and possible defensive construction as **fictional reconstruction**, checked against the setting rather than presented as documented events. The presence of `blair-robertson` in outline metadata is a research lead, not proof of the proposed scenes. Do not silently import later Spanish siege conditions, cannon types, permanent navies or a centralized taxation office.

**Title review:** *The Making of the Kota* may promise a completed fortification absent from the final prose. Revisit after the six scenes; prefer a concrete detail whose meaning changes with the chapter.
