## Chapter purpose

Force Ache to form an alliance outside his inherited networks and establish Lakanmulaw or the equivalent Visayan ally as a recurring relationship rather than a one-chapter encounter.

## Opening state

Ache and Sulad have conditional shelter for one night, little silver, and no confirmed onward passage. Ache is too quick to treat his own willingness to help as authority to settle other people's arrangements.

## Immediate objective

Survive the dispute at an unnamed first landing and earn only a conditional chance to seek onward passage.

## Main plot movement

Ache pays for an extra hand to steady a visiting boat, but cannot authorize unloading Nima's sister's cloth onto occupied ground. His payment buys the agreed labor, not landing rights. Any cooperation must be negotiated afterward.

## Subplots

- **Ache and the visiting boat leader (provisionally Mulaw):** a possible alliance must respect the leader's obligations to his crew and the absent cloth owner.
- **Ache and Sulad:** Sulad already challenges Ache's unilateral decisions; neither his labor nor his consent can be promised for him.
- **Widening world:** political categories outside Maynila prove more complex than Ache expects.

## Conflict and stakes

Ache has spent scarce silver on useful work without resolving the dispute. The cloth remains aboard, the landing space remains contested, and Sulad has reason to question Ache's judgment.

## Themes and subthemes

**Theme:** stranger, enemy, ally.

**Subthemes:** prejudice, reciprocity, reputation, practical trust.

## Character progression

### Ache

**Begins:** assumes money and initiative can organize a dispute before securing the relevant people's permission.

**Pressure:** must evaluate another leader as an equal actor with independent goals.

**Ends:** accepts cooperation that he cannot fully control.

### Lakanmulaw

**Begins:** suspicious outsider with no reason to serve Ache.

**Progression:** discovers enough shared interest or respect to cooperate.

**Ends:** potential ally whose loyalty remains conditional.

### Sulad

**Progression:** tests Ache's judgment rather than merely supporting his decisions.

## Turning point

Ache must acknowledge in front of the affected people that his payment did not buy permission to unload; Sulad and the cargo owner retain the right to refuse further obligations.

## Resolution and ending state

Ache gains a conditional introduction or chance to negotiate onward passage, not a guaranteed boat, route, or fare. Mulaw's independent obligations and Sulad's objections survive the immediate agreement.

## Setup and payoff

Sets up Lakanmulaw's later return in Books II–III.

## Handoff

The alliance creates opportunity, but Chapter 8 still has to solve the material problem of supplies and passage.

## Suspense and emotional stakes revision

**Scene mandate:** the first encounter with a Visayan/other community is told through a specific dispute about a vessel, safe anchorage or shared threat. **Reversal:** the apparent adversary has a moral objection to Ache's position; Ache does not win simply by identifying as Brunei's grandson. **Choice:** make an unflattering concession or lose a potentially lifesaving ally. **Cost:** Sulad sees a kind of leader Ache has not yet learned to be.
