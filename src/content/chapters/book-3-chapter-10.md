## Canon and source gate

**This replaces the obsolete kingdom/fleet/succession outline, which is not published canon.** Published Book III Chapter 9, *Three Knots*, ends with Ache carrying Amaya's empty jar basket uphill while the cord owner arranges the next market at a rival landing. The merchant group has a real common grievance but no agreed universal charge or joint destination. The last scene establishes that Raya's step remains unsafe, Madan's uncut timber must be inspected only after his prior house obligation, Dala has granted no standing basket area, and Amaya has received a small measure of rice for Ache's carrying but **still lacks her replacement cloth**. Sela lost two fish sales; Laman's boat made an empty return; Hiran refused an unpaid changed journey; Salin heard a rumor on behalf of Naru's Tondo household and does not represent Tondo. The keeper recorded the rumor separately from the witnesses' statements and did not create a transferable license.

Book III Chapter 10 must **resolve the core commercial and legitimacy crisis on the page**, with an earned physical and social outcome, before the separate Spanish-arrival bonus Chapter 11. Do not introduce a standing army, royal fleet, kingdom-wide levy, council bureaucracy, hereditary Sulayman command, subordinated Lakandula, southern-shore governor, marriage or son, or a formal delegated office. Sulayman, Lakandula and Sulad remain in `related` for research navigation only, not as established Chapter 10 appearances. Ache speaks in first person; every other participant retains separate control of their work, land, boat, cargo and testimony.

Tomé Pires's *Suma Oriental* (composed c.1512–1515) supports broad regional trading context, **not** the specific fictional step, boats, fish, labor contracts, market schedules or Tondo-Maynila coalition. The Laguna Copperplate Inscription (900 CE) does not establish this keeper's bamboo records; Plasencia's 1589 account is later and cannot certify these wages or rights. Do not import modern port authority, police, union, tariff, property registry, representative assembly or public service rules as historical institutions. Original-language Portuguese and Spanish source passages remain uncollated. All scene-level bargaining and physical geography are reconstructed fiction.

## Chapter conflict and earned resolution

The cord owner's rival-landing arrangement is working for some traders. Ache cannot restore traffic by denying their loss or ordering boats back. The decisive question is whether any **specific** group can offer a usable, honestly described landing under conditions each necessary person has actually accepted. A paid watcher cannot make rotten timber safe. A repaired step cannot open Dala's net path or Nali's opposite-bank rack path. A truthful warning can send customers elsewhere, and that may be the most valuable service the bank can provide.

The chapter resolves when a physically inspected landing, a particular waiting arrangement, clearly paid work and a way to communicate changing conditions **are tested with real cargo**, without creating a blanket right or permanent levy. The coalition loses its single leverage point because different owners now have different viable, voluntarily chosen arrangements. The cord owner may keep business at the rival landing; he must not be humiliated into obedience. A successful landing may close again with tide or damage. Ache earns trust by keeping an obligation he can complete and by declining one he cannot.

**Theme:** A promise can endure only as far as the work and consent behind it. **Subthemes:** market time, foregone wages, weather, ownership, competing truthful accounts, material repair, unpaid household labor, lost business that cannot be recovered.

## Six-scene causal architecture

### Scene 1: The empty basket comes back full

Open immediately after Chapter 9's last line: Ache follows Amaya uphill to collect her jars, learning that carrying them without breakage is the promise in front of him. The cord owner's next-market offer at the rival landing becomes real: buyers and boat owners move there, while one merchant complains that its dry place was already full. Do not stage the rival landing as uniformly superior. Ache hears a complaint he cannot dismiss, and Amaya refuses to have her cloth claim converted into unpaid public repair labor. **Turn:** the coalition's bargaining power is a series of individual choices, not a single enemy to defeat.

### Scene 2: Wood already promised

Madan finishes or secures the earlier roof support for the house above the bend before showing Raya the uncut length. His niece checks the old cord promises. Raya examines the actual timber and its bearing points; she may reject a defective section. The cutting and carrying crew name their own wages and food, and Madan names a price that cannot be met by Ache's good intentions. Sela, Laman or the clay-weight trader may offer **voluntary, explicitly bounded contributions** in exchange for a particular service, but no universal toll or use of Dala's ground. **Turn:** repair becomes possible at a visible cost and time, and at least one trader refuses to fund it.

### Scene 3: The work that belongs to Amaya

Ache takes actual work offered by Tesa or another seller, keeps the agreed morning and brings a measurable payment home. Tesa cannot retroactively restore the cloth length already sold. Amaya decides what payment counts and whether a replacement is adequate. The story may pass several days; show labor, food, rest, delayed earnings and trade-offs rather than conjuring a cloth after one trip. Ache must choose between a paid obligation and an urgent request to attend the landing. **Turn:** he keeps his prior commitment, even though the landing loses an opportunity without him. If the cloth is paid in full, show the negotiated price and Amaya's explicit acceptance; otherwise keep the unpaid balance visible at the end.

### Scene 4: The place for one market

Dala examines an actual proposed waiting patch and may allow **one identified load at one identified tide**, reserving her right to move the net and refuse later requests. Raya inspects the newly set timber and rejects any premature opening. Hiran, or another independently available worker, agrees to a defined paid watch or pole shift, with hours, food, wage payer and right to stop made explicit. A messenger or carrier agrees voluntarily to carry the bank's true condition before boats depart, without becoming a standing official or being presumed truthful forever. The keeper distinguishes who witnessed each statement; her marks do not become permits. **Turn:** a specific workable arrangement exists but can still fail when tested.

### Scene 5: The water tests the promise

A real load arrives while the water is changing. Give the boat owner and pole-handler independent decisions; no one may demand unsafe passage. The new timber, stone bearing, net path and paid waiting time are tested physically. A tide shift, slipping support or unexpected cargo weight should force a costly pause or revised plan. Ache must choose between falsely announcing the landing open and allowing a buyer to leave. Raya's authority over the repair is practical and limited, and she can close the step even after a successful test. **Turn:** at least one paid journey succeeds or is responsibly cancelled, with visible consequences, because the agreed limits are followed rather than hidden.

### Scene 6: Different boats, different answers

The merchants reconvene after seeing what the bank can and cannot do. Sela, Laman, the clay-weight trader, Hiran, Dala, Raya and the cord owner need not all attend; each relevant person must independently confirm any term attributed to them. The keeper can preserve the accepted and refused claims separately. One owner returns for a specific agreed service; another uses the rival channel; the cord owner retains a credible competing business. The proposed blanket watcher charge is abandoned as a joint demand, not magically outlawed for everyone. The coalition **ceases to be a unified threat**, with no military victory, submission, council decree or new state. Ache may still owe Amaya and Nali, but the central port-access crisis is resolved by functioning, bounded agreements. **Ending:** the ordinary work of a changing bank continues. Bonus Chapter 11 must begin the separate arrival of Spaniards, not rescue an unresolved Book III climax.

## Character progression and dramatic QA

**Ache:** stops offering other people's labor and land in the hope that his name can hold an agreement. He must keep a paid job, own a failed promise and accept that an owner may still leave after being told the truth.

**Amaya:** decides whether rice, labor or an actual replacement cloth satisfies her household; she cannot be conscripted into the landing project. **Raya:** decides what wood and bearing will support a load, and may close the step when conditions change. **Dala:** grants or refuses particular use of the path without yielding her fishing work. **Madan and niece:** keep earlier promises and independently price material and labor. **Sela:** protects her sellers and buyers after a real lost fish market. **Laman and Hiran:** separately choose boat and pole work. **Cord owner:** can win business at another landing, make an attractive offer, and remain unconvinced. **Keeper:** preserves claims and uncertainty, not enforceable orders. **Salin:** speaks only for Naru's household if he appears. **Nali and Iya:** no general crossing rights and no permanent messenger duty.
