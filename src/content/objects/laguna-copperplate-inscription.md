**Dated 900 CE.** A copper-plate legal/debt-clearance inscription from the broader region of Laguna, composed principally in **Old Malay** in an **Indic/Kawi-related script** and reflecting maritime Southeast Asian cultural networks. Place names and political interpretations are debated in scholarship.

**Not:** a baybayin document, an Ache-era artifact, proof that Maynila in 1500 used the same inscription forms, or evidence of uninterrupted control by the polity remembered later as Tondo.

Its *theme* matters for the novel: earlier societies used writing, records, named obligations and transregional languages centuries before Europeans reached Manila. The *novel* may reflect this deep past through conversation or later scholarship without claiming Ache read the actual plate.

Sources: [Clavé & Griffiths (2022)](https://archium.ateneo.edu/phstudies/vol70/iss2/2/); [National Museum discussion](https://www.nationalmuseum.gov.ph/2022/09/20/the-9th-to-10th-century-archaeological-evidence-of-maritime-relations-between-the-philippines-and-the-islands-of-southeast-asia/).
