## Historical context

Islamic connections between Brunei and Manila-area elites are historically significant, but conversion should not be imagined as a single date when an entire population adopted uniform beliefs and practices. Religious learning, marriage, trade, and political affiliation could spread ideas unevenly. Evidence for a specific young Ache's teachers, daily prayers, memorized texts or personal convictions is lacking.

Brunei's Muslim court was established by the early sixteenth century; the religious composition of Maynila and Tondo was more complex and cannot be deduced from rulers' names alone. Avoid equating the word **Moro** in Spanish documents with a precise description of each person's practice, ethnicity or language.

**Story use:** Ache encounters differences between court instruction, maritime pragmatism and family obligations; learning should emerge through relationships and choices rather than a lecture. **Unverified:** precise curriculum, local mosque organization, literacy of each character, and religious views of individual households.

**Research base:** Cesar Adib Majul, *Muslims in the Philippines* (1973); William Henry Scott, *Barangay* (1994); [Oxford Islamic Studies overview of Islam in Southeast Asia](https://www.oxfordreference.com/display/10.1093/oi/authority.20110803100013774).
