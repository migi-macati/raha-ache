## Evidence

Archaeology across multiple islands attests **gold and other ornaments, ceramic manufacture, imported wares, textile arts and specialized watercraft construction** at varying dates. Written records and later scholarship describe oral **songs, riddles, proverbs, ritual performance and poetic forms**; exact lyrics, named instruments and performance customs in Ache's household around 1500 are mostly unknown.

The [National Museum](https://www.nationalmuseum.gov.ph/our-collections/archaeology/ornaments/) associates ornaments with artisanal skill, stratified display and other roles. The [Banton Cloth](/raha-ache/objects/banton-cloth/) from Romblon is an **earlier** comparative textile, not an Ache-era Tagalog sample. The [NCCA's literature account](https://ncca.gov.ph/about-ncca-3/subcommissions/subcommission-on-the-arts-sca/literary-arts/early-philippine-literature/) surveys later-attested oral genres, and Scott's Tagalog-focused chapters warrant closer textual comparison.

## Dramatic use

A craftsperson seeks payment; a dyer protests a court's commandeering of cloth; a vessel's ornamental work or inherited gold tells a family story; an elder's recited compact conflicts with an official written interpretation. A song can synchronize a boat crew, mark mourning, express satire or preserve genealogy.

**Do not:** claim each ancient artifact came from Maynila, use unsupported 1500 song lyrics, or describe all Philippine communities as sharing identical art and ritual.
