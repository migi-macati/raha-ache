## Documented episode

Pigafetta reports a large flotilla in several squadrons near Brunei in July 1521, followed by European attacks on and capture of vessels. The reported Luzon prince was described as captain-general for Brunei. **This does not prove the organization or strength of his previous Loue expedition.**

Aganduru Moriz later assigns Ache nearly two hundred vessels and up to six thousand soldiers. These numbers are attributed to a seventeenth-century retrospective source, not confirmed by a surviving muster.

## Historically plausible operational problems

- **Information:** scouts, lookouts, pilots, signals and the risk of misunderstood orders.
- **Movement:** winds, tides, river shallows, speed differences, night approaches and weather.
- **Supply:** potable water, food preservation, firewood, replacement gear, medical needs and suitable anchorages.
- **Force:** missile fire, maneuver, boarding, shore landings, and the political costs of destroying useful vessels.
- **Authority:** allied crews and captains may owe loyalty to their own patrons, not exclusively to the fleet commander.

These are **scenario-building inferences** based on maritime logistics, not a surviving Brunei naval manual. Avoid precise standardized formations or command ranks without dated evidence.

## Ache's progression

Let Ache earn competence by solving a specific bottleneck, losing something meaningful, and learning from experienced mariners. An effective tactic should have a cost or expose a new vulnerability. He cannot simply command an immense fleet by birthright.
