## Historical context

Pigafetta's **1521** Brunei description includes elaborate court reception, ceremonial movement, elite material display and hierarchical mediation. The account is that of a visiting European observer and cannot establish every private rule or how ordinary residents experienced the court.

Court audiences could be moments of controlled access and political theater: who speaks, interprets, waits, carries gifts, or guarantees a promise can matter as much as formal titles. Gifts may create expectations, but no specific Bruneian gift tariff or compulsory formula for Ache's arrival has been documented here.

**Story use:** Ache makes a diplomatic mistake by addressing the wrong intermediary; a patron's gift tests whether he understands its implied obligation. **Unverified:** exact etiquette for Ache's grandfather, room arrangement, prescribed gestures, court uniforms and translation protocol.

**Research base:** Antonio Pigafetta, 1521 Brunei narrative; [Pigafetta's account in *The First Voyage Round the World*](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/The_First_Voyage_Round_the_World/Pigafetta%27s_Account_of_Magellan%27s_Voyage).
