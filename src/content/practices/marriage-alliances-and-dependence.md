## Historical context

Elite marriages could strengthen kinship and diplomatic relationships, but no one alliance should be assumed to create automatic political submission. Aganduru Moriz retrospectively describes a marriage arranged for Ache with a Bruneian relative. The precise chronology, negotiations and personal consent are not independently documented.

Marriage arrangements and household alliances affected women, children and dependents as political actors, not merely symbols exchanged by rulers. The novel should give women their own choices and constraints without pretending to know the historical private thoughts of Ache's relatives.

**Story use:** a marriage negotiation can complicate succession, personal loyalty and military commitments. **Unverified:** Ache's exact betrothal date, ceremonial details, private correspondence and the legal terms of the alliance.

**Research base:** Rodrigo de Aganduru Moriz, *Historia general* (retrospective); William Henry Scott, *Barangay*; Cesar Adib Majul, *Muslims in the Philippines*.
