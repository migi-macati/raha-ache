## Historical context

The maritime world connected speakers of different Philippine, Malayic and Bornean languages. Malay served as a major language of exchange and diplomacy in many Southeast Asian ports, but that does not prove every courtier was fluent or that all conversations occurred in one language. Sixteenth-century Spanish spellings of names and titles are not standardized phonetic records.

Early Philippine writing traditions existed, but their distribution, functions and Ache's personal literacy are not established by surviving texts. The 900 CE Laguna Copperplate Inscription demonstrates much earlier literate administration in a regional network; it cannot be used as proof that Ache's household kept identical records six centuries later.

**Story use:** interpreters exercise agency; a mistranslated promise can have political consequences; Ache earns linguistic competence through imperfect practice. **Unverified:** exact language of any invented conversation, Ache's fluency at each age, private written correspondence.

**Research base:** William Henry Scott, *Barangay*; [Clavé and Griffiths (2022), Laguna Copperplate Inscription](https://archium.ateneo.edu/phstudies/vol70/iss2/2/).
