## Regional exchange

Sixteenth-century maritime Southeast Asia connected Chinese manufactures, regional forest and marine products, metals, textiles, foodstuffs, captives, and goods passing through many intermediary ports. The commodity mix varied by port and season. **Do not infer that every item was traded at Maynila in Ache's childhood.**

Ptak's reconstruction distinguishes Borneo- and Luzon-linked corridors toward the Moluccas; this does not establish a single royal monopoly or one fixed customs regime. [Ptak (1992)](https://www.persee.fr/doc/arch_0044-8613_1992_num_43_1_2804).

## Diplomacy in practice

Gift exchange, kinship claims, interpreter choice, guarantees of safe passage, debt, hostage-taking and patronage are useful dramatic mechanisms. Some are documented regionally, but the novel's particular documents, tolls and ceremonies must remain fictional unless supported by a source.

Avoid treating commercial relationships as consensual by default: raiding, forced labor and enslavement were part of the wider historical setting and should be represented without romanticizing them.

## Scene prompts

A merchant refuses payment in an unfamiliar measure; a harbor elder disputes who can promise protection; an interpreter's ambiguous phrase threatens an alliance; a diplomatic gift creates an obligation the recipient did not anticipate.
