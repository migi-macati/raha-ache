## Distinct evidence

**900 CE:** [Laguna Copperplate Inscription](/raha-ache/objects/laguna-copperplate-inscription/) uses a **Kawi-related Indic writing system and Old Malay**. It is **not baybayin** and does not prove unbroken later bureaucratic practice.

**1593:** the [*Doctrina Christiana*](/raha-ache/sources/loc-doctrina-christiana-1593/) in Spanish and Tagalog securely displays Tagalog baybayin and Roman-script text **after** Ache's death. Older Baybayin use is supported by later records and scholarly reconstruction, but who in Ache's household could write and the exact style/medium they used c. 1500 remain uncertain.

**Novel invention:** Ache's Court of Scribes and Translators, its ledger and appeals are imaginative state-building institutions. The story should contrast the authority of **oral witnesses, physical tokens, written marks, multilingual translation and named patrons**; none is automatically neutral.
