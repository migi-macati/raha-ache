## Seasonal logic

The **northeast monsoon** generally influences much of the South China Sea during boreal winter, while the **southwest monsoon** is associated with boreal summer. Winds and currents vary substantially by coast, strait and local weather. These are broad patterns, **not** a calendar that guarantees safe travel or a fixed sailing duration.

Navigators could use coastal landmarks, depths, reefs, tides, stars, prevailing winds and accumulated pilot knowledge. A passage that was practical in one direction or season could become expensive or dangerous in another. [NOAA, monsoon overview](https://www.noaa.gov/education/resource-collections/weather-atmosphere/monsoons); [Ptak, northern Spice Islands trade routes](https://www.persee.fr/doc/arch_0044-8613_1992_num_43_1_2804).

## Political implications

Access to a pilot, freshwater stop, sheltered anchorage, or favorable departure window can be bargaining power. A commander cannot assume a fleet will move as fast as its quickest ship.

## Limits for the novel

Exact voyage durations, courses, named pilotage marks, and a specific 1500s sailing calendar require further research. Do not substitute modern engine-powered travel times or contemporary harbor charts for the historical coastline.
