Sultanate and royal court central to Ache's maternal kinship, political education, and early military career.

Aganduru Moriz describes Ache arriving at his grandfather Siripada's court, being received as a royal relative, and later departing with a large Bruneian force.

In the novel, Brunei is the principal setting of Ache's transition from displaced prince to trained commander.

## Historical setting: a maritime sultanate

Brunei was a major Muslim court and maritime trading power on the northwestern coast of Borneo. Pigafetta's **1521** account describes a populous settlement built on piles above tidal water, river-borne movement, elaborate court audiences, armed guards, merchants, and substantial material wealth. His figures, descriptions, and judgments are observations by a visiting outsider and should not be treated as an exact census or universal portrait.

Brunei's political reach was built through trade, royal marriage, diplomacy, naval action, and relationships with other coastal communities. Scholarship commonly places Sultan Bolkiah's reign in the late fifteenth and early sixteenth centuries and describes a period of expanding influence. This should **not** become a map of uniformly administered territory or a claim that Brunei directly governed every linked Luzon port.

### Brunei and Ache

Aganduru Moriz's retrospective narrative says Ache went to his maternal grandfather's court for help amid the Tondo dispute and was entrusted with command. The 1521 voyage testimony independently points to a son of the Luzon ruler commanding forces associated with Brunei; Ginés de Mafra's account also associates a Luzon prince with marriage negotiations involving Brunei. The exact relationship among these passages, the prince's identity in each, and the ruler's name require explicit source comparison.

**Siripada** appears as a royal designation/name in early accounts. Equating every occurrence with **Sultan Bolkiah** is an interpretive step; the novel's character pages must keep that distinction visible.

### Novel-use texture and limits

- **Documented for 1521:** A stilt-built water settlement, court protocol and ranked intermediaries, naval forces, trading networks, Muslim kingship, and encounters with the Magellan expedition.
- **Reasonable dramatic reconstruction:** Training aboard war vessels, multiethnic crews, court competition, ship provisioning, negotiations, and Ache's divided loyalties.
- **Unknown:** The precise organization of Ache's command, his years at court, day-to-day court language, specific tutors, detailed fleet strengths, and the extent of Brunei's jurisdiction in Maynila.
- **Chronology caution:** Pigafetta's 1521 observations cannot simply be projected unchanged backward into Ache's childhood.
