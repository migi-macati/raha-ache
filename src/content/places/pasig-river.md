River connecting Manila Bay with inland communities and Laguna de Bay.

Book I uses the river for Ache's early sailing, training, household movement, trade, and political encounters.

It is one of the novel's recurring geographic anchors: home, route, boundary, marketplace, and approach to Maynila.

## Navigation evidence

The 1572 *Conquest of Luzon* narrative says pilots advised the 1570 expedition to wait for high tide before entering Manila's river, whose entrance had a bar. Soundings and local knowledge mattered. This describes 1570, not Ache's earlier childhood. [Blair and Robertson, volume III, pp. 72–75](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/13616/13616-h/13616-h.htm).

Modern Pasig studies confirm that tide and season can change flow direction, but cannot reconstruct a particular sixteenth-century tide or shoreline. [Paronda, David and Apodaca (2019)](https://doi.org/10.1088/1755-1315/344/1/012049).
