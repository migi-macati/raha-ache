Plasencia's account gathers information from Tagalog informants **after the 1571 conquest** and amid ongoing Spanish adjudication. It is extremely useful for Tagalog social categories, land tenure, leadership, fishing and marketplace rights, debt and dependency—but its scope includes different Tagalog districts, not solely Maynila, and some customs could have changed since c. 1500.

**Accessible historical document:** [Project Gutenberg, vol. VII](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/13701/13701-h/13701-h.htm) and [NCCA, *Philippine History Source Book*](https://ncca.gov.ph/wp-content/uploads/2021/09/PHILIPPINE-HISTORY-SOURCE-BOOK-FINAL-SEP022021.pdf).

### Details for scene construction

- Chiefs governed differentiated followings, sometimes a few dozen households, with personal alliances among chiefdoms. His *barangay* origin story is his inference, not an excavated account of prehistoric migration.
- Some chiefs claimed **specific fisheries** and **river-market stretches**, where a nonmember could owe payment for fishing or trade. This supports plausible competing rights to landings/markets, not a uniform port-wide customs bureau.
- **Maharlika** were freeborn retainers with military and maritime duties; **aliping namamahay** could own houses and property yet owe service; **aliping sa gigilid** faced more severe household dependency and potential sale. The terms are historically variable and should not be translated into fixed modern legal castes.
- Labor, feasts, boat service, inherited duties, war captives and obligations are concrete sources of disputes that affect foreign trade and princely action.

### Caution

Do not project every rule backward to 1500, transfer Plasencia's Pila/Laguna example to Maynila as documentary fact, or sanitize slavery/debt into benign cooperative labor. Plasencia's own discussion acknowledges Spanish officials confusing the categories and worsening abuses.
