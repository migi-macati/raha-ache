The National Museum documents imported **Chinese, Vietnamese and Thai** high-fired ceramics excavated at Philippine archaeological sites, including **Santa Ana, Manila**. Trade wares exist for multiple centuries, so dating by site and ceramic type is crucial. Objects establish contact and circulation—not the individual ethnicity of their last user, the voyage's crew, or a particular king's sovereignty.

The Museum also considers Srivijayan traders' contacts in the 8th–11th centuries **possible** rather than a proven annexation of Philippine territory. Its Mt. Kamhantik material attests the reach of Tang Changsha wares.
