The [National Museum ornament collection](https://www.nationalmuseum.gov.ph/our-collections/archaeology/ornaments/) documents ornaments of gold, bronze, glass, shell, jade, bone and other materials from many regions and periods, associated with artistry, rank and funerary practices. [Burial goods](https://www.nationalmuseum.gov.ph/2021/11/23/burial-goods/) demonstrate variability in mortuary wealth; **Santa Ana 11th–14th-century** graves are earlier and upriver from Ache's Maynila.

The [Banton death-shroud textile](https://www.nationalmuseum.gov.ph/wp-content/uploads/2021/09/Hibla-ng-Lahing-Filipino-The-Artistry-of-Philippine-Textiles.pdf) shows sophisticated warp tie-dyed weaving, approximately **13th–early 14th century** in Romblon. It **cannot** be used as a precise copy of Ache's Tagalog court clothing in 1500.

[Butuan boats](https://www.nationalmuseum.gov.ph/2022/10/24/preserving-our-heritage-towards-the-future-of-our-museums/) show edge-joined, lashed-lug maritime construction in an older and geographically distinct setting. Maritime technical exchange is plausible, but the novel should not claim an excavated Butuan hull represents Ache's actual boat design.

For scene construction: artisans and owners negotiate time, materials, technical knowledge and status. Avoid treating all objects as pure decoration or automatically imported.
