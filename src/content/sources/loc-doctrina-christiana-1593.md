The **1593** printed catechism shows Tagalog in **baybayin and Roman letters** with Spanish. It is the earliest surviving book printed in the Philippines and a secure **late-sixteenth-century** script witness. It is **not a surviving Ache-era original**, does not prove any particular person's literacy in 1500, and does not prove that every court record was written.

The **900 CE Laguna Copperplate Inscription** instead uses **Old Malay and Indic/Kawi-related writing**, a **different, much older tradition**. The distinction matters: neither object directly proves continuous documentation practices at Ache's court.

Source: [Library of Congress item](https://www.loc.gov/item/48031307/); [National Museum Baybayin Gallery](https://www.nationalmuseum.gov.ph/2022/10/21/baybayin-ancient-and-traditional-scripts-of-the-philippines-gallery/); [Clavé and Griffiths (2022)](https://archium.ateneo.edu/phstudies/vol70/iss2/2/).

Use: Book III's proposed **Court of Scribes and Translators** must be visibly *invented as Ache's reform*, not described as documentary fact.
