## Verified English edition and name boundary

The English translation of the anonymous <a href="https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/13616/pg13616-images.html">*Conquest of the Island of Luzón*</a>, dated April 20, 1572, is printed in Blair and Robertson, vol. 3, especially printed pp. 128 and 145. The editors' bibliographical note identifies Retana's *Archivo del Bibliófilo filipino*, vol. 4 (1898), as their source for this document. **The English edition is separately locatable. Retana’s printed Spanish wording has now been checked against a digitized text transcription; the underlying page images and manuscript have not been visually collated.** The narrator is a conquest-side writer, not a neutral witness.

On printed p. 128 of this relation, the writer identifies a recently deceased Christian Manila chief as **Laya**, who entered into terms with the Spaniards alongside **Raxa Solimán**. In the same document, printed **p. 145**, the author returns to Laya's death when describing inheritance practices: he claims that Laya died Christian, had no inheriting children by his one 'legitimate' wife, and that his property passed to a 'legitimate nephew.' The nephew is **not named** in this passage. The author's descriptions of wives, legitimacy and inheritance are Spanish observations and categories, not an independently verified account of each household's choices.

**Identification gate:** The document says **Laya**, not **Ache** or **Matanda**, in these passages. Connecting Laya to the **Raha Ache the Old** named in the separate Riquel record is a historical identification requiring explicit explanation and cross-document support. Do not present the Laya passage as if it literally names Ache, or assume the unnamed nephew is Sulayman. The Spanish author's earlier account of negotiations with Laya and Raxa Solimán supplies contextual evidence for identification, but does not eliminate all uncertainty.

## Chronology and original-edition locator

**Conditional terminus ante quem:** The English edition dates this relation **20 April 1572** (Blair and Robertson, vol. 3, contents and document heading) and describes **Laya as already deceased** (printed p. 128; reiterated on p. 145). If the document's date and retrospective account are accurate, Laya died **no later than 20 April 1572**. This is a constraint on the source's **Laya**, not independent proof of the death date of **Ache**: the Laya–Ache identification remains a separate inference. Avoid repeating a later exact death month as documented without reconciling this contemporary account.

## Retana Spanish-text collation (29 October 2026)

A searchable digitized transcription of Retana's *Archivo del Bibliófilo filipino*, **tomo IV** (Madrid, 1898), [is accessible here](https://es.scribd.com/document/584225691/Archivo-Del-Bibliofilo-Filipino-Recopilacion-de-Documentos-Historicos-Cientificos-Literarios-y-Politicos-y-Estudios-Bibliograficos-Por-We-Retana-Tomo). The same volume is catalogued by the [Biblioteca Virtual Miguel de Cervantes](https://www.letrasmexicanas.mx/portales/fernandez_lizardi/obra/archivo-del-bibliofilo-filipino--recopilacion-de-documentos-historicos-cientificos-literarios-y-politicos-y-estudios-bibliograficos--por-we-retana--tomo-4/) with a [PDF scan](https://www.letrasmexicanas.mx/descargaPdf/archivo-del-bibliofilo-filipino--recopilacion-de-documentos-historicos-cientificos-literarios-y-politicos-y-estudios-bibliograficos--por-we-retana--tomo-4/). The transcription includes the Spanish account under *Relación de la conquista de la isla de Luzón*, dated at its conclusion in Manila **20 April 1572** (Retana printed p. 37).

- **Retana printed p. 11:** In its account of the 1570 negotiations the Spanish writer names **Laya** and **Raxa Solimán** as distinct principals and says of Laya, **“que oy día es difunto y murió cristiano.”** The date of the document places the statement after Laya's death, but supplies no death month.
- **Retana printed p. 30:** In a generalizing, strongly prejudicial discussion of marriage and inheritance, the writer says Laya had children by other wives, none inheriting by the wife he calls legitimate, and **“y ansí heredó un ligítimo sobrino suyo.”** The nephew is **unnamed**. This describes property inheritance in the author's terms, not a documented transfer of office or rule.
- **English comparison:** Blair–Robertson vol. III printed **pp. 128 and 145** correspond to these passages. The name, Christian-death claim, other children, and unnamed nephew are consistent at the level of the accessible Spanish transcription and English translation. Neither passage names **Ache**, **Matanda**, or **Sulayman as heir**.

**Verification level:** Original-language **published-edition text transcription inspected**, including the printed page locators. The Scribd text may contain OCR/transcription errors; **page-image and manuscript collation remain UNSAVED/unverified**, and exact spellings should be checked visually before diplomatic quotation or philological conclusions. This is a partial resolution of the Spanish-source gate, not verification of the Laya–Ache identity. The source is partisan, including dismissive judgments about local people and their institutions.

## Fiction and historical QA

- Documented in this English translation: a Christian chief called Laya is described as deceased by the 1572 writing, and the author claims a legitimate nephew inherited property.
- Inferred: identification of Laya with Ache the Old, and any link between that nephew and another named principal.
- Unverified: Spanish manuscript and page-image fidelity, specific baptism date or private motive, actual disposition of each item of property, and any assertion that the source establishes a deathbed request for Sulayman's appointment.
- Fictional scene design: preserve Ache's personal agency and faith uncertainty; do not turn colonial categories of 'legitimacy' into objective judgments about his relationships or descendants. Distinguish the time of the writer's retrospective narration from the date of the events he describes.
