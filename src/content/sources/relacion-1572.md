## Verified English edition and name boundary

The English translation of the anonymous <a href="https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/13616/pg13616-images.html">*Conquest of the Island of Luzón*</a>, dated April 20, 1572, is printed in Blair and Robertson, vol. 3, pp. 141–172. The editors' bibliographical note identifies Retana's *Archivo del Bibliófilo filipino*, vol. 4 (1898), as their source for this document. **This verifies the English-edition locator, not the original Spanish wording.** The narrator is a conquest-side writer, not a neutral witness.

On printed p. 128 of the larger volume (within the 1571–72 narrative, before the document's title pagination is complete in this compilation), the writer identifies a recently deceased Christian Manila chief as **Laya**, who entered into terms with the Spaniards alongside **Raxa Solimán**. In the same document, printed **p. 145**, the author returns to Laya's death when describing inheritance practices: he claims that Laya died Christian, had no inheriting children by his one 'legitimate' wife, and that his property passed to a 'legitimate nephew.' The nephew is **not named** in this passage. The author's descriptions of wives, legitimacy and inheritance are Spanish observations and categories, not an independently verified account of each household's choices.

**Identification gate:** The document says **Laya**, not **Ache** or **Matanda**, in these passages. Connecting Laya to the **Raha Ache the Old** named in the separate Riquel record is a historical identification requiring explicit explanation and cross-document support. Do not present the Laya passage as if it literally names Ache, or assume the unnamed nephew is Sulayman. The Spanish author's earlier account of negotiations with Laya and Raxa Solimán supplies contextual evidence for identification, but does not eliminate all uncertainty.

## Fiction and historical QA

- Documented in this English translation: a Christian chief called Laya is described as deceased by the 1572 writing, and the author claims a legitimate nephew inherited property.
- Inferred: identification of Laya with Ache the Old, and any link between that nephew and another named principal.
- Unverified: original-language exact text, specific baptism date or private motive, actual disposition of each item of property, and any assertion that the source establishes a deathbed request for Sulayman's appointment.
- Fictional scene design: preserve Ache's personal agency and faith uncertainty; do not turn colonial categories of 'legitimacy' into objective judgments about his relationships or descendants. Distinguish the time of the writer's retrospective narration from the date of the events he describes.

## Bibliographical follow-up

Collate the Spanish passage in Retana, *Archivo del Bibliófilo filipino*, vol. 4 (Madrid, 1898), against the Blair–Robertson English translation, especially the Laya identification, Christian death, inheritance and nephew. Cite original Spanish page/folio only after inspection.
