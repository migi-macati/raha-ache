Archaeology excavated in **Santa Ana, Manila** documents riverine habitation and **eleventh–fourteenth-century graves** containing Chinese Song/Yuan ceramics, local earthenware, coins, beads and metal objects. The evidence is earlier than Ache and **upriver from the bay-mouth settlement**; it must not be portrayed as a plan of his court or as proof that these people called themselves the later reconstructed polity of Namayan.

In addition to burials, NCCA describes midden layers with **shells, animal bones, pottery sherds and iron-working debris**. The National Museum's earthenware catalog notes a **twelfth–fourteenth-century cooking stove** from Santa Ana. These support lived practices and local manufacture, not simply luxury imports.

**Dating caution:** these sites do not prove what burial or cooking practice prevailed among every south-bank Maynila household circa 1500. Neither does a ceramic's Chinese origin prove a Chinese visitor or resident at a particular Santa Ana grave.
