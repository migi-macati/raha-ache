## What this source establishes and does not

Brewer reviews early Spanish-authored descriptions of Philippine religious specialists, gender presentation, and sexuality. The missionaries' observations are **partial, selective and shaped by colonial assumptions**; even the use of words such as *asog*, *bayog* and *baylan* varies by regional context and source.

The article can help the novelist avoid treating the early Philippines as culturally uniform and avoid assuming today's sexuality and gender categories were the self-labels of people in early Maynila. It **cannot** establish Raha Ache's private attraction or validate invented romances, nor does a historically recorded gender-variant religious role automatically imply bisexuality or same-sex intimacy.

A bisexual Ache is a **deliberate modern literary characterization of a thinly documented historical person**, not an attribution found in the sources.
