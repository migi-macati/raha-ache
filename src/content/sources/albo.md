Project source notes preserve Albo's description of Borneo and the capture of a junk carrying a son of the king of Luzon.

## 1521 Brunei sailing and junk capture: corroborating route witness

Francisco Albo's navigational `derrotero`, in the English translation published by the Hakluyt Society (1874), describes the Brunei expedition meeting many armed canoes, **sailing in haste and going outside the harbor**, then encountering and capturing a junk carrying a son of the ruler of Luzon. It next states that the ships sailed away from Borneo, returning by the route they had used. [Albo logbook, Borneo passage](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/The_First_Voyage_Round_the_World/Log-Book_of_Francisco_Alvo_or_Alvaro) (paragraphs beginning `So we remained here several days` and `We sailed from Borney`). The [Philippine Diary Project chronology](https://philippinediaryproject.com/category/logbook-of-francisco-albo/page/5/) groups the approach and encounter under **21 June–29 July 1521**.

**Evidence boundary (updated 2 November 2026):** The [Sevilla first-circumnavigation documentary project's Spanish transliteration of Albo's *Derrotero*](https://sevilla.2019-2022.org/wp-content/uploads/2016/03/8.ICSevilla2019_Derrotero-de-Francisco-Albo-f15.pdf#page=15), printed pp. 15–16, has now been read and checked against a rendered printed page. It identifies the archival witness as **Archivo General de Indias, PATRONATO,34,R.5**. This is a modern transcription of the archival text, **not a direct inspection of the sixteenth-century folio image**. Its square-bracket explanations identifying Carvalho and suggesting a ransom are editorial annotations, not statements by Albo. The passage itself gives no calendar date, precise capture-to-departure interval, named captain, or ransom amount.

Albo reports withdrawal **outside Brunei harbor** before seizing the junk with a Luzon prince and then later sailing away from Borneo. Departure from the harbor is not necessarily departure beyond sight of the wider approaches. The **29 July** date comes from Pigafetta's dated confrontation, not an independent date in Albo's Brunei paragraph. The Genoese pilot's *Roteiro* (edited Portuguese in NHCP, *Historia Obscura*, vol. I, 2025, Document 13, pp. 76–79) describes a different re-anchoring and multi-day sequence; its calendar conflicts with Pigafetta. Albo's archival folio images and textual variants remain uninspected. None of these accounts establishes which fictional captives returned, how long Ache was aboard, or the hour of final departure.
