Van der Wall reads Joaquin's national history through Oswald Spengler's civilizational philosophy. His critique identifies the historical force of Joaquin's anti-purist conception while challenging Eurocentric hierarchies, the erasure of uncolonized peoples' agency and the claim that colonial transformation determined an unchangeable national soul.
