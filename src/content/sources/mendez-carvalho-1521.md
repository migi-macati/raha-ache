## What the document records

At Tidore on **13 November 1521**, expedition accountant **Martín Méndez** recorded a statement by pilot **Juan López de Carvalho** to the ruler of Tidore. Carvalho described an alarm near Brunei, the seizure of a junk and a prao, and the capture **the following day** of another junk carrying a son of the ruler of Luzon. The 1920 printed transcription uses the phrase *e luego otro dia tomamos otro junco* (p. 176). This is a participant's **November recollection of July events**, not a daily log made at the time of the captures.

The same statement says three women taken from a Luzon ruler's junk near Brunei remained on the expedition's ships. The entry for **14 November 1521** records their transfer to Tidore's ruler (pp. 177–178). The document does **not** establish their names, consent, release, return home or eventual fate. It does not identify them with any of the novel's fictional captives.

## Historical limits and competing accounts

This is **contemporaneous documentary testimony**, accessible here through a **1920 published archival transcription**, not through an inspected original sixteenth-century folio. The collection identifies Document 105 with the Archivo de Indias materials and warns of dating mistakes elsewhere in the record. The November statement gives neither a calendar day for the Brunei captures nor a final departure date for the European fleet. Its next-day capture sequence differs from a strictly same-day reading of Pigafetta's 29 July narrative. Albo describes the withdrawal from Brunei harbor and a Luzon prince's capture but gives no exact date. The Genoese pilot supplies another sequence. These texts must not be counted as independent confirmations of one precise itinerary without checking their transmission and possible dependence.

**Evidence inspected:** [1920 collection, vol. III, Document 105, pp. 175–178](https://es.scribd.com/document/399553318/Coleccion-General-de-Documentos-Relativos-a-Las-Islas-Filipinas-Existentes-en-El-Archivo-de-Indias-de-Sevilla-III), indexed printed transcription. **Still uninspected:** original archival folio, facsimile of the printed pages, and a critical collation of variant readings. The document records what Carvalho said; it does not independently verify every claim he made.

## Fiction boundary

Book II's Ache, his confinement, the names and choices of captives, negotiations and homeward arrangements remain fictional reconstructions. Do not turn the three women's documented transfer into an invented rescue or reunion, or silently rewrite the novel's one-day staging as established history. Any future chronology correction requires a witness-by-witness comparison and a narrow, tested manuscript change.
