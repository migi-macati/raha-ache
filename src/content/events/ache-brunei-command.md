Pigafetta's account of the 1521 encounter describes the captured Luzon prince as captain-general of the king of Brunei. This is contemporary evidence for the command title, although the identification of that prince with Ache requires comparison with later sources.

Aganduru Moriz, writing in the seventeenth century, says Siripada received Ache at court and later placed nearly two hundred vessels and about six thousand Bruneian soldiers under his command. The court reception and numerical muster are not independently established by Pigafetta's account; preserve them as attributed retrospective claims, not a verified fleet inventory.
