## Evidence and identification

The anonymous Spanish-side relation dated **20 April 1572** describes a recently deceased Christian Manila chief called **Laya** (Blair and Robertson, vol. III, printed p. 128). It says Laya had children by other wives, no inheriting children by the wife it calls "legitimate," and that his property passed to an **unnamed legitimate nephew** (printed p. 145).

Identifying **Laya with Ache the Old / Rajah Matanda** requires comparison with other records, including Riquel's 1571 account. The 1572 passage itself does not name Ache, Sulayman as heir, or an exact baptism or death date. If Laya and Ache are the same person and the relation is correctly dated, the account places his death **by 20 April 1572**; an August 1572 death month needs independent reconciliation. The year-only timeline sort value is a placeholder, **not an asserted month or day**.

## Fiction and source limits

Bonus Chapter 12 reconstructs Ache's final household decisions and baptism from limited testimony. It does not document his private beliefs, an inheritance transfer to Sulayman, or a deathbed political appointment. The source's labels of legitimacy are colonial descriptions, not neutral findings about local kinship. See the [succession research](/raha-ache/research/succession-1572/) and [1572 source dossier](/raha-ache/sources/relacion-1572/). Original Spanish collation remains outstanding.
