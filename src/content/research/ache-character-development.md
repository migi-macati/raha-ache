## Early notes

Curious, adventurous, inward-looking, and more attracted to study, invention, navigation, and exploration than routine government.

Strong expectations from his ruling family.

Develops from a young heir avoiding responsibility into a commander and ruler.

## Name

An early development note connected Ache's name to Aceh and used that connection as a possible "name is destiny" motif.

The historical etymology remains unproven.

## Rank and identity

Early notes experimented with "Ache Lusong" as an invented surname and with "Prince/Panginoon" as English-facing shorthand.

"Lusong" is not treated as a historical surname.

## Book I, Chapter 3 — first prose pass

Ache uses the river-tally investigation opened in Chapter 2 to win provisional passage for a Bruneian captain without armed confrontation. He learns to obtain independent testimony from Ula, a Namayan elder, and to distinguish his Muslim household formation from political loyalty or authority over people of other practices. His mother confirms Siripada as his maternal grandfather within the novel's reconstruction, but warns that kinship with Brunei is no automatic promise of ships. Hamid offers instruction in trade language and ship accounts. The blue-cord poles planted on Maynila's shore set up Chapter 4's concrete encroachment.

The specific river dispute, Hamid, cloth token, and red-thread custom are fictional; Aganduru's account supports the maternal Brunei connection, not these scenes.
