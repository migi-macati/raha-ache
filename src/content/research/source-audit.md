## Primary-source chain

Current primary-source pages include Albo, Pigafetta, Mafra, the Legazpi expedition relations, Riquel's 1571 record, and the anonymous 1572 Relación.

Aganduru Moriz, Chirino, Morga, and Gaspar de San Agustín are kept as near-primary or early colonial chronicles rather than contemporaneous eyewitness evidence for every event they describe.

## Secondary scholarship

William Henry Scott, Cesar Adib Majul, Luis Camara Dery, Félix de Huerta, Rizal's annotations, and other later works are kept separate from primary testimony.

## Documentary collections

Blair and Robertson is treated as a reference/documentary collection. Where possible, claims should cite the underlying document rather than the collection as though Blair and Robertson were the historical witness.

## Foreign contacts research packet

The [foreign presence dossier](/raha-ache/research/foreign-contacts-ache-era/) expands the source hierarchy:

- **Contemporary European commercial intelligence:** Tomé Pires's *Suma Oriental* (1512–15), a witness to Malaccan commercial knowledge rather than a Luzon traveler.
- **Contemporary Chinese and Southeast Asian layers:** Song/Yuan/Ming records and the 900 CE copperplate, requiring accurate translation, dates, and contested place identifications.
- **Archaeology:** imported Chinese, Vietnamese, Thai and West Asian objects; contact and provenance do not automatically prove settlement or foreign government.
- **Later archival source as mediated by scholarship:** Iaccarino reports Japanese merchants at Maynila in May 1570 from AGI Patronato 24 r. 17, which merits direct transcription.
- **Earlier empire hypotheses:** Srivijaya/Majapahit influence requires distinguishing prestige, trade, and literary claims from occupation or territorial rule.
