**Historical attribution, unverified in the original:** Later scholarship, including Majul, attributes to Aganduru Móriz a story in which Ache's cousin in Tondo encroached on Maynila while Ache's mother governed. The cited Spanish passage (CODOIN 78, pp. 59–60) has not been inspected; see the [source dossier](/raha-ache/sources/aganduru-moriz/). This is a provisional retrospective account, not independently established eyewitness evidence.

**Development status:** This political-and-religious coalition concept was superseded by the [published Book III](/raha-ache/books/book-3/), whose core shows specific contested river-landing rights, not a general Tondo-led religious alliance or Bruneian military reconquest.

Earlier novel expansion: Tondo becomes the leading political counterweight to a Brunei-linked, Islamizing Maynila and can rally other non-Muslim interests around the fear of Bruneian expansion.

The wider Luzon/Visayan religious coalition was a speculative proposal, neither an established historical alliance nor an event in the published trilogy.

The earlier character-design principle was that the Tondo rival should remain a competent political actor with a defensible strategic rationale rather than a simple villain.
