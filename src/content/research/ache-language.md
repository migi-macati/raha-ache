Old Tagalog is the expected local language of Ache's Maynila environment.

Malay was the major diplomatic and trading language of the Brunei-centered maritime world.

Ache's long residence at the Brunei court and naval command make fluency in court/trade Malay a strong inference, but the project does not currently have a primary sentence explicitly stating his language proficiency.

## Revised source controls and character consequences

See [Maynila language, writing and social-order research](/raha-ache/research/maynila-language-arts-social-order/) and [the Book III merchant-language subplot](/raha-ache/research/book-3-merchant-and-status-conflict/).

**Three written contexts must stay separate:** the **900 CE** Old Malay/Indic/Kawi copperplate, the **1593** Tagalog/baybayin *Doctrina Christiana*, and Ache's **fictional** written harbor rules. Their existence does **not** prove Ache's literacy, a sixteenth-century script archive or universal Tagalog literacy in 1500.

**Spoken arc:** Ache learns to follow Bruneian trading speech through actual practice; no source records his exact language proficiency. Interpreters have their own agendas and can turn “friendship” into “submission” or a conditional oral pledge into permanent service. **Mehomete** is attested in 1570 as Goiti's Maynila-born interpreter but is not documented as Ache's earlier courtier.
