*Editorial development material; includes spoilers for all three books. Private motives, actions, relationships and habits below are fictional character design, not historical testimony.*

## The core principle: flaws are adaptations, not checklists

A believable character is **the same person in victory, boredom, danger, embarrassment and affection**, even when the circumstances make that person act inconsistently. What looks like a virtue in one setting becomes a liability in another; what another character calls a flaw may genuinely have helped this person survive.

For each recurring character, establish:
1. **Self-story:** the account the character gives of why they act that way, which contains truth and omissions.
2. **Private want and fear:** not simply what the plot requires or what Ache requires.
3. **Useful strategy and harmful overuse:** the mechanism that once worked and the cost when circumstances change.
4. **Contradiction:** a value they sincerely hold but betray in a specific situation, with understandable pressures.
5. **Shame or vulnerability:** a fact they would rather hide, not a staged backstory confession.
6. **Voice and attention:** what they notice, what they avoid saying, who makes them funny, and how they behave without Ache present.
7. **Costs and limits:** at least one decision not forgiven, one consequence not repaired, and one conviction they retain despite the story.

**No clinical diagnosis is necessary.** Fear, habits, grief, loyalty, ambition, faith, love, dependency and pride are not diagnoses. Avoid making cruelty interesting merely by attaching trauma to it, or describing every wrongdoing as secretly altruistic.

**Source boundary:** Historical figures have thinly documented private lives. Everything here beyond the existing archival record is **proposed novel psychology**. Fictional characters are completely imagined. Do not project twenty-first-century individualism, caste, nationalism, sexuality categories or ideas of bureaucratic equity uncritically onto 1500–1572 maritime Southeast Asia.

## Central figures, written as contradictory people

### Ache — generous fixer, intrusive ruler

**He tells himself:** If I can see the danger and prevent it, I would be cowardly not to act.

**Useful trait:** rapid pattern recognition; the ability to notice what a boat, treaty or institution might do next. He sometimes risks himself to rescue someone who has nothing to offer him.

**Harmful overuse:** he mistakes **solving a problem** for **owning the right to decide what other people must risk**. In embarrassment he begins speaking as a ruler instead of a boy. When afraid of failing someone, he becomes quicker and less willing to consult them.

**Contradiction:** fiercely believes followers should be protected, yet casually volunteers their labor; cherishes friendship, yet expects Sulad to always be available; hates Tondo's recordkeeping as intrusion, yet later builds more efficient registers himself.

**Quiet human details (working scene choices):** likes the sensation of a well-repaired paddle, not royal jewelry; remembers hull failures in absurd detail but misses a person's fear; laughs too early after a close call; keeps his father's cloth in a place where he can touch it but resents being observed doing so. He can be petty about winning a trivial wager.

**Shame:** people may admire his claim instead of him. His mother held Maynila together without him. He would rather be disliked for an act of courage than pitied for being powerless.

**Hard choice:** let Sulad or Sulayman reject one of his well-reasoned orders and still remain a person he trusts. In Book III Ch10, the institutional correction must be public and costly; no sudden moral enlightenment erases those harmed.

**Never:** write him as a secret modern democrat or infallible strategic prodigy. He can be wrong twice for different reasons, and can choose badly after giving good advice.

### Sulad — caretaker whose loyalty becomes possessive

**He tells himself:** I don't need a crown; someone has to keep idiots alive.

**Useful trait:** practical anticipation; sees weather, fatigue, danger and the emotional state of ordinary sailors before a prince does. Cares through food, repairs and standing beside someone rather than speeches.

**Harmful overuse:** he can decide what others are allowed to risk **without asking them**; his protectiveness shades into control. He keeps private tallies of unreciprocated sacrifice and says “it's fine” until resentment escapes in an unfair remark.

**Contradiction:** insists on candid speech with Ache but hides his own aspirations and family loyalties; mocks status while enjoying the informal power of being the prince's indispensable companion.

**Texture:** answers fear with a dry practical complaint; gets unusually quiet when a mother's object is handled carelessly; can enjoy fine craft and domestic routine rather than living entirely for combat. He is sometimes deliberately, not accidentally, unkind in arguments.

**Shame:** his labor may be assumed rather than chosen; part of him wants recognition he despises himself for wanting.

**Hard choice:** refuse Ache at an important cost and accept that some people may call it disloyalty. Their friendship does not become magically stronger simply because they reconcile.

**Never:** make him the omniscient moral conscience or an endlessly patient bodyguard.

### Ache's mother — competent negotiator who uses silence as control

**She tells herself:** A living settlement is worth more than a satisfying declaration.

**Useful trait:** knows whose children eat when a trade boat is delayed; can read a merchant's incentives and prevent needless escalation.

**Harmful overuse:** she withholds reasoning until others feel trapped, then tells them they should have understood. She can sacrifice a small household's claim because a larger trade arrangement protects many others—and find reasons not to revisit that sacrifice.

**Contradiction:** loves Ache intensely yet humbles him publicly; defends stability but tolerates people profiting from uncertainty; demands honesty but offers only portions of the truth.

**Texture:** checks a provisioning detail herself when nervous, folds and unfolds an old cloth while considering a decision, has a genuine and occasionally mischievous sense of humor away from the court. Sometimes speaks too sharply and does not apologize until later.

**Shame:** she cannot guarantee safety even to her child; much of her political authority depends on people who only provisionally support a woman regent.

**Hard choice:** a compromise helps a merchant and injures an ordinary family. She must acknowledge that she chose it, not turn the injury into another lesson for Ache.

**Never:** use her as an infallible dispenser of wise sayings or a secretly evil strategist.

### Lakandula — protective ruler who bargains with other people's autonomy

**He tells himself:** Peace without secure rights for Tondo's households is merely submission to another shore.

**Useful trait:** understands kin networks, patient bargains and the need to keep a trading world functioning. He may rescue or pay for a person without expecting his cousin to know.

**Harmful overuse:** measures another ruler's intentions through leverage and precedent, sometimes treating a relative's grief as an opportunity. He can be meticulous about old insults long after a sensible bargain has been struck.

**Contradiction:** champions customary autonomy when Tondo benefits, but wants deference where his own men control the bank. A genuine liking for Ache coexists with willingness to undermine him.

**Texture:** enjoys specific harmless moments of family familiarity and then turns calculating without signaling a change; corrects an inaccurate memory rather than a policy argument. He can be politically patient and privately thin-skinned.

**Shame:** he fears being remembered as a lesser ruler who was outplayed by a younger kinsman, while knowing that pride itself threatens his people.

**Hard choice:** protect a vulnerable Maynila person when his own officers want to exploit them, or accept a bargain that humiliates him in the eyes of Tondo followers.

**Never:** a theatrical usurper who engineered every injury Ache discovers. His youth-connection to Ache is a historical **reconstruction** rather than direct identification by an eyewitness.

### Ula — community guardian who enjoys her authority

**She tells herself:** People who sail far still need people who remember the shore.

**Useful trait:** has earned local trust over years through care, ritual, memory and attention to how families actually cope; permits people to disagree without expelling them.

**Harmful overuse:** when her status is threatened, she can mistake a younger person's doubt for disrespect. She uses reputation and a carefully timed silence to gain influence. Her suspicion of Bruneian religion may come from real experience but can make her unfair to sincere believers.

**Contradiction:** insists on humility from chiefs yet likes being the most respected person at a gathering. Defends other people's customs but occasionally treats Ache's own faith as mere politics.

**Texture:** knows when to stop talking; has small favorites among children, poor memory for names she thinks she should know, and can misread someone and privately feel foolish. Avoid aphorisms in every second line.

**Shame:** one of her remedies, consolations or predictions might fail to protect someone. The failure does not prove she is fraud; it makes her human.

**Hard choice:** publicly admit uncertainty when followers need her confidence, or speak against Ache when she knows that will cost her community access.

### Bulkan — seasoned captain with pride in an obsolete answer

**Self-story:** I've buried enough sailors to know what works.

**Virtue/flaw:** drills crews to prevent death; stubbornly insists on familiar drills even when new tides or enemy tactics make them dangerous. He dismisses a young person's insight before testing it, partly out of fear of losing authority.

**Contradiction:** teaches obedience but once broke an order to save a particular sailor. Keeps the story private because it weakens his lecture.

**Texture:** fusses over rope inspection and food before battle; is impatient with jokes in public, occasionally an eager participant in them in private.

**Choice:** admit a junior sailor was right in front of his men—or pay for his pride with an avoidable loss. **Continuity:** his exact role during the flight remains unresolved. Do not silently put him on Ache's boat without reconciliation.

### Siripada — grandfather and patron, affectionate and transactional

**Self-story:** The court exists so many families may survive.

**Virtue/flaw:** understands how to support relatives without bankrupting the court; withholds affection or relief at moments he believes it would weaken a political lesson. He fears favoring Ache will alienate captains whose men must pay the cost.

**Contradiction:** wants Ache to prove independence while making him dependent on Bruneian permission. Can be delighted by his grandson's wit and annoyed when the boy shows it publicly.

**Texture:** remembers a mundane domestic detail Ache's mother would have known, but forgets a small promise he considered trivial.

**Choice:** refuse a grandson's request when the whole court watches; later make an unpublicized personal kindness that does not undo the refusal. Historical private psychology invented.

### Jamil — protective admiral whose caution becomes an ego

**Self-story:** A returned crew matters more than a celebrated maneuver.

**Virtue/flaw:** masters morale, tides and provisions; can punish an innovative officer for exposing weaknesses in his own tried method. He may protect an old subordinate at the expense of an unknown younger sailor.

**Contradiction:** condemns arrogance but cannot easily admit his own tactical error. Might praise Ache in private while withholding public credit.

**Texture:** checks something mundane after stressful combat, laughs only when truly relieved, recognizes crew members' families more readily than their titles.

**Choice:** defend Ache against unfair court blame while admitting that Ache and Jamil both made different errors. If a crewman is hurt, no perfect explanation saves their relationship.

### Hasan al-Fansuri — teacher who can misuse moral certainty

**Self-story:** A correctly understood word can prevent a needless wrong.

**Virtue/flaw:** patient with a clumsy learner, intellectually curious; in an actual crisis he can spend too long distinguishing beliefs or finding proper language when someone needs help. He may scorn a local practice he hasn't listened to deeply.

**Contradiction:** preaches humility yet enjoys being asked to settle arguments by pure learning. Can refuse to translate a knowingly dishonest pledge and still remain dependent on court patronage.

**Texture:** impatient about mistranslated words, unexpectedly tender with a frightened pupil, privately laughs at his own poor practical seamanship. Avoid using him only to recite doctrine.

**Choice:** withhold scholarly prestige from a royal policy he personally believes wrong, possibly losing safety and livelihood.

### Safi — skilled rival who resents unearned access

**Self-story:** My crews should not die to restore another prince's home.

**Virtue/flaw:** serious about merit and competent at command; envies Ache's effortless access to patronage and sometimes discounts real achievements as nepotism. His resentment makes him harsh toward other foreigners who actually have earned respect.

**Contradiction:** condemns favoritism, then privately asks a family ally to protect his own appointment. Offers real help to Ache without suddenly becoming his devotee.

**Texture:** competitive about tiny games as much as great battles; secretly appreciates the ridiculousness of court ritual; rarely forgets a public slight.

**Choice:** risk his standing to tell Ache an unwelcome truth, or hold to a tactically defensible course that causes grief. Respect need not become intimacy.

### Laila Nur — ambitious partner who confuses control with care

**Self-story:** People who think before a crisis can prevent most cruelty.

**Virtue/flaw:** alert to dynastic obligations and women's constrained room to influence court decisions; she plans well, preserves correspondence and refuses to be a romantic prize. But planning can become an attempt to make loved ones choose the future *she* thinks is safe.

**Contradiction:** values individual choice but tries to secure Ache's by arranging circumstances without telling him. Capable of tenderness and a cold political bargain on the same day.

**Texture:** notices who is seated outside important discussions; can be openly silly with trusted company, impatient when interrupted, and deeply hurt when Ache praises her intelligence without hearing her objection.

**Choice:** give up a desired relationship rather than abandon a commitment to Brunei, without being depicted as punished for independence. **Continuity:** she is *not yet identified* with Ache's attested cousin-wife.

### Jalal — charming ally who sometimes prefers the exciting answer

**Self-story:** If you cannot laugh with your allies, you cannot trust them in a storm.

**Virtue/flaw:** openhanded, brave, effective at informal diplomacy; rashly pledges help because he wants to be loved and admired. His debts to Sulu kin can overturn an assurance he made to Ache.

**Contradiction:** praises honest bargains, avoids confessing when his promise outruns his resources; can be generous to peers and thoughtless toward servants.

**Texture:** tells a joke badly when frightened, remembers a guest's favorite food but forgets a formal obligation, speaks more carefully with family than with fellow princes.

**Choice:** withdraw promised aid to protect his own people, and accept Ache's anger without pretending either side is wholly wrong.

### Lakanmulaw — free-minded ally who has prejudices of his own

**Self-story:** I know my own coast; outside princes learn it or leave.

**Virtue/flaw:** understands local seafaring and the right to refuse unwanted patrons; pride in those capacities turns into premature distrust of Malay/Bruneian court people and their faiths.

**Contradiction:** resents outsiders stereotyping his people while indulging in his own dismissive jokes about visitors. May save a rival crew even while refusing their political plan.

**Texture:** affection expressed in repairs, direct insults and communal food; privately embarrassed by not knowing a court formula others treat as obvious.

**Choice:** defend Ache in a specific battle but withhold his own people from a politically costly campaign. He is not a generic “non-Muslim friend” symbol.

### Sulayman — independent younger ruler, not an apprentice copy

**Self-story:** Peace that cannot be defended is permission for the strong to take what they want.

**Virtue/flaw:** vigorous protection of people exposed to danger, political intuition for when threats are immediate; impatient with costly deliberation and quick to interpret caution as indifference to the vulnerable.

**Contradiction:** demands Ache trust him with command while denying lesser officers a comparable freedom. Sincerely admires his elder and resents constantly living inside Ache's legacy.

**Texture:** notices a frightened guard before Ache does, dislikes ceremonial praise when a practical job is unfinished, may be surprisingly shy or ill at ease around intimate family grief.

**Choice:** in fictional Ch9–10, exercise delegated authority differently than Ache would and own the consequences. The record of the later Spanish-era Sulayman is fixed where documented; particular youthful habits and disagreements are invented.

### Ache's Bruneian wife — a person, not a marriage instrument

**Self-story (to invent carefully):** I will not cross a sea to become a symbol in somebody else's family.

**Virtue/flaw:** capable of assessing dynastic kin obligations and maintaining an independent household; may invest so heavily in protecting status that she judges local Maynila women and their claims unfairly before learning who they are.

**Contradiction:** asks to be treated as a partner, while withholding information she fears Ache will use to overrule her.

**Texture and choice:** give her food she misses, relatives she worries about, a practical talent she enjoys, an occasional tedious task she resents, a private joke, and a public decision where she openly disagrees. The **historical woman's name and individual psychology are not recorded in the archive**. Do not silently make her Laila, who remains separate in development.

### Mehomete — interpreter who believes he can remain useful to everyone

**Historical fact:** in 1570 the voyage relation identifies this Maynila-born provisioner and intermediary associated with Cebu and Goiti; wife and son converted with him. The text does not provide his private motives, younger employment or inner life.

**Proposed fictional character:** values exact words, family provision and movement between communities; is quick to flatter each side with the version it can hear, and begins thinking that his compromises are harmless because they keep everyone talking.

**Contradiction:** wants to prevent violence by interpreting, sometimes withholds a politically dangerous nuance; demands that others trust his words though he mistrusts their candor.

**Texture:** remembers quantities and promises precisely, fears being caught having translated differently, loves some everyday domestic thing his public role cannot save. Avoid making baptism either automatic proof of treachery or moral perfection.

**Choice:** in Bonus 11 an apparently slight translation choice could alter what one side believes it promised. What he actually thought remains unknowable historically. Any earlier place at Ache's court is a **conditional, entirely invented** backstory requiring chronology review.

### Pablo — a resident who resents being reduced to an origin or faith

**Historical fact:** a Japanese man identifying himself as Christian and calling himself Pablo appears in the Spanish 1570 report. A rumored role among Maynila's gunners is not confirmed; life and fate unknown.

**Proposed fictional character:** wants a household/community to be secure, rejects the assumption that anyone who knows his religion knows his loyalties. May be status-conscious, easily slighted by merchants, excessively private, or jealous of people whose membership in Maynila is never questioned.

**Contradiction:** asks not to be judged by a category, yet judges newcomers for failing the cultural tests he has learned. Could recognize Christian symbols with relief, then recoil from what soldiers demand in the same breath.

**Texture:** a particular tool or domestic routine matters more to him than being a historical “symbol of Japan.” Don't automatically portray him as samurai, master gunner, Jesuit, Portuguese emissary or universally multilingual.

**Choice:** whether to aid a specific individual when danger reaches his own house; cannot manufacture a verified historical outcome after 1570.

### Panday Pira — master of a craft, imperfect with people

**Historical boundary:** later accounts associate his name with Manila artillery production; exact biography and source chain remain under audit.

**Proposed fictional character:** refuses an unsafe commission because he cares about workmanship and the lives affected by failure; his pride may cause him to disparage apprentices and conceal the help needed for a deadline.

**Contradiction:** demands respect for artisans but treats juniors as replaceable when they make errors. He may enjoy royal patronage and resent royal interference simultaneously.

**Texture:** notices a metal flaw before a ruler's greeting; considers the household consequences of lost copper or fuel; capable of affection without a sentimental workshop speech.

**Choice:** delay vital guns until work is safe, or deliver flawed weapons under royal pressure. Do not invent proof he personally designed every cannon known to Spanish observers.

## Smaller recurring people must also live offscreen

- **Injured rower:** does not exist merely to teach Ache empathy. A skilled worker may be proud, demanding, funny, protective of an apprentice, embarrassed to accept help, and capable of blaming the wrong person.
- **Rower's sister:** has a claim that remains open; she may overstate a demand from fear for her household and still be right about the prince's responsibility. Give her a source of pleasure and an opinion about something unrelated to Ache.
- **Tondo clerk:** records crews partly because one missing sailor could not be traced. The same practice can help coercive claimants. He is neither innocent saint nor planted villain; he may fear being fired and act out of shame or professional pride.
- **Saved child and father:** gratitude is real and incomplete. The child may regard the day as terrifying rather than heroic; the father must still feed a crew and compensate losses.
- **Ordinary boat workers:** some support Ache's reforms, some dislike them, many are focused on their households. No crowd should respond with identical applause or outrage.

## Behavioral differentiation: who notices what in the same room?

**At a broken quay:** Ache sees a movable load and solution; Sulad sees men who are too tired to pull; Ache's mother knows whose cargo will miss the tide; the Tondo clerk asks whose names must be recorded; Ula asks who has been left unvisited; Lakandula sees a precedent; Panday Pira notices the failing fittings; Sulayman looks for the next threat. These are **tendencies, not permanent character gimmicks**.

**When accused:** Ache argues facts; Sulad withdraws then explodes; mother asks a question that conceals emotion; Safi brings witnesses; Laila lays out consequences; Jamil says little and checks the crew; Hasan questions the key word; Jalal jokes too quickly; Ula insists on a relationship; Mehomete tries to find wording acceptable to both sides. Give each of them a scene where they abandon that habit under pressure.

**When affection is safe:** show characters eating, celebrating, grieving, teasing, flirting, attending households, making objects, worrying about aging parents and enjoying useless games. A villain needs an ordinary noon, and a trusted companion needs a bad one.

## Tests for draft reviews

A character passes only if:
- Their choice would remain intelligible in a chapter written from **their own first-person POV**, even if morally objectionable.
- They can **hurt someone they love without intending to**, and can deliberately hurt someone for a reason they regret.
- They have one **correct insight the protagonist resists** and one **false belief they defend**.
- Their flaw is shown through **concrete behavior and consequences**, not author labels or a confession about “my fatal flaw.”
- They have at least two relationships that show **different facets** of them; not everyone experiences a character in the same way.
- Their habits are **not repeated mechanical tics** (everyone sighing, smirking, offering aphorisms, or exchanging clever insults).
- They do not become automatically “fixed” at a book climax. Sometimes a real loss teaches nothing quickly.
- Important people can disappear from Ache's scene and still have goals and activities underway.
- History's thin record is **not filled with invented certainty**. The story bible labels psychology as fiction; prose realizes it without explaining scholarship mid-scene.

## Revision priorities

1. **Book I current drafts:** make Ache's mother's humor less consistently corrective; allow Sulad to withhold an easy response; let Ula be briefly unfair or vulnerable and correct herself in action. Don't stuff the opening with secret traumas.
2. **Book I Chapters 4–5:** give the injured rower, sister and Tondo clerk agency before the flight; no puppet-master conspiracy.
3. **Book II outlines:** give Safi a victory deserved, Jamil a genuine error, Hasan a failure of practical empathy, Laila a hard independent choice, and Jalal/Lakanmulaw conflicting obligations.
4. **Book III outlines:** force Ache, Sulad and Sulayman to disagree at costs, give the wife her own household stakes, and make Mehomete/Pablo ordinary people before historical symbols.
5. **Prose standard:** in every 1,500–2,000 words, at least one small unplanned-looking human action should arise from character (a misheard joke, sour mood, interrupted meal, protective lie, embarrassment) rather than advance the main plot on command. This is a **drafting guideline**, not a quota requiring formulaic writing.
