This is an **archived institution-building proposal**, superseded by the published Book III. The [published book](/raha-ache/books/book-3/) depicts bounded river-landing repairs, witnesses and disputed records. It establishes no permanent Harbour Council, Naval Order or Court of Scribes. The proposals below are retained as development history and must not be read as events or offices that appear in the finished narrative.

## Harbour Council of Maynila

Customs, commercial disputes, port rules, and standardized weights.

## Naval Order of the Bay

Permanent corps of river and sea captains.

## Court of Scribes and Translators

Malay and Tagalog records, treaties, correspondence, and laws.

## Religious settlement

Part III development notes preserve a deliberate religious duality rather than immediate replacement of older practices by Islam.

These institutions were **proposed fictional developments**. They are neither published novel canon nor independently established historical institutions.

## Earlier design controls (superseded proposal)

The [new research dossier](/raha-ache/research/maynila-language-arts-social-order/) distinguishes 900 CE Kawi-related inscription practice, **1593 baybayin evidence**, and invented Ache-era writing. The Court of Scribes and Translators is **a story invention**, not a proven standardized state chancery. Define witnesses, interpreters, appeal procedures and the costs of false written records.

**Harbour Council:** mediate local and visiting traders' rights without erasing Tondo's independent claims, fishery owners or non-elite households. A “neutral” rule can enable seizure or predatory exclusive access.

**Naval Order:** service and supply obligations vary by household status; later Plasencia describes maharlika rowers and differentiated alipin service. Avoid importing one fixed Tagalog labor code from 1589.

**Court of Scribes:** the earlier plan proposed introducing [Mehomete](/raha-ache/characters/mehomete-maynila/) as a **proposed** late-period interpreter-broker only when dated consistently. The role must exist independently of the person to withstand both his absence and the 1570 Spanish encounter.

**Artisans and ritual:** textile makers, metal casters, boat builders, singers and religious specialists have distinct interests and limits; decorative art and songs should perform a scene function.

The [Book III merchant-and-status development packet](/raha-ache/research/book-3-merchant-and-status-conflict/) proposed tests of these systems in Chapters 6–10; the published chapters instead center on local claimants, records, labor and refusal without establishing these institutions.
