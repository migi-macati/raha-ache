## Maynila line

**Ache's father** — directly present but unnamed in Aganduru Moriz. Later reconstruction identifies him as Salalila.

**Ache's mother** — directly present but unnamed in Aganduru. She governs Maynila after the father's death and is described as the daughter of the Brunei ruler Siripada.

**Ache** — the central historical figure.

## Brunei line

**Siripada** — Ache's maternal grandfather and ruler of Brunei in Aganduru Moriz.

**Sultan Bolkiah** — identified with Ache's maternal grandfather in later secondary reconstruction. The story bible does not silently merge Bolkiah and Siripada.

**Ache's Bruneian wife** — described by Aganduru as Siripada's granddaughter and Ache's first cousin. Her personal name is not established in the current archive.

## Tondo relationship

Aganduru describes the ruler of Tondo during Ache's youth as Ache's cousin.

Later reconstruction identifies this unnamed cousin with **Lakandula**, who is directly named as Tondo's principal in the 1571 Riquel record.

## Sulayman and succession

Riquel directly places **Sulayman** beside Ache as the younger ruler of Manila.

The project follows the historical reconstruction that Sulayman was Ache's nephew, but the 1572 succession passage preserved in the archive refers only to an unnamed **legitimate nephew** inheriting Ache's property.

Until the exact genealogy and wording are fully reconciled, the story bible does not use the 1572 passage by itself as proof that the inheriting nephew was Sulayman.

## Working rule

When writing the novel, documented kinship, named historical identification, and fictional elaboration should remain separable.

A scene may use the project's strongest reconstruction as novel canon while the story bible still records where the historical evidence becomes inferential.
