## Evidence status (2 November 2026)

The **1882 Spanish printed text** of Rodrigo de Aganduru Móriz, *Historia general de las Islas Occidentales*, CODOIN vol. LXXVIII, **pp. 60–61**, has now been read through the indexed [Ghent scan](https://www.heuristiek.ugent.be/wp-content/uploads/2018/08/codoin.78.pdf). Aganduru says Siripada sent a granddaughter, previously promised in Brunei, **after** he learned the prince had recovered secure possession in Maynila. He calls her the prince's **first cousin** and asserts the marriage had no offspring. The printed wording is now checked at text level, replacing the earlier paraphrase-only status. Scan images, OCR-sensitive spellings, the seventeenth-century manuscript and independent corroboration remain unverified.

**Chronology boundary:** Aganduru's narrative orders the marriage **after** his claimed restoration, but supplies **no exact date** or contemporary marriage record. Some later summaries attach the story to 1521; that is not a verified wedding date. The event page's `dateSort` is a provisional ordering key, not evidence of a dated ceremony. Aganduru's claim of a successful armed restoration and his providential explanation for the absence of offspring are contested retrospective assertions, not established facts.

## Novel continuity

Published Book III Chapter 2 ends with a bounded local restoration and **does not depict a marriage**. Its earlier link to the marriage event was removed. Do not infer a wedding from a source backlink or write a ceremony, spouse relationship or offspring into canon without an independent narrative decision and clear fact/reconstruction/fiction boundary.
