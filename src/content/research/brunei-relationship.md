Documented: Ache is closely connected to Brunei, is described as grandson of its ruler in Aganduru Moriz, and serves as captain-general or naval commander for the king of Brunei in the 1521 evidence.

Reconstructed: Maynila is often described as lying within Brunei's political and Islamic sphere.

Unresolved: the degree of formal vassalage, tribute, and day-to-day control Brunei exercised over Maynila.

## Evidence audit: Maynila–Brunei–Tondo, circa 1500–1521

### Stronger evidence

- **1521 eyewitness layer:** Pigafetta describes Brunei's court, waterborne settlement, and regional maritime activity. The expedition encountered a royal son from Luzon linked to Brunei's naval command. Other expedition testimony, especially Ginés de Mafra, should be checked independently for the reported marriage mission.
- **Retrospective narrative:** Aganduru Moriz says Ache appealed to his grandfather at Brunei after conflict with his cousin in Tondo. It supplies a coherent political story but was written after the events.
- **Later political context:** 1570–1571 sources establish Maynila and Tondo as important, separate ruling centers, and later evidence demonstrates connections among Manila-area and Bruneian elites.

### Interpretive distinctions

**Kinship is not sovereignty.** A ruler's marriage, fosterage, military service, or diplomatic reliance does not alone prove that Brunei collected regular tribute from Maynila or appointed its rulers.

**Islamic influence is not uniform conversion.** Muslim courtly connections and the presence of Islamic practice do not establish the faith or observance of every household in the river settlements.

**Naval command is not a complete biography.** The expedition sources support a commander in Brunei's orbit in 1521, but not the novel's named mentors, specific missions, training regimen, or private motives.

**Siripada is not automatically Bolkiah.** Keep the original source's designation distinct from later scholarly identifications; do not silently equate royal titles and personal names.

### Fiction continuity guidance

The novel can dramatize Ache's arrival at Brunei, military apprenticeship, and loyalty conflicts, but these scenes must be understood as inventions grounded in the broader context. Preserve his family dispute as a motivating claim without pretending that surviving documents establish exactly what land was taken or how. Treat Tondo and Brunei as sophisticated political actors with internal interests.
