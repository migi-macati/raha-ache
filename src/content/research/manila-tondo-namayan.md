The trilogy treats the Pasig corridor as a multi-polity environment rather than a single centralized kingdom.

Tondo is the older major port at the river mouth's north side; Maynila is the younger south-bank polity with strong Brunei/Malay/Islamic connections; Namayan belongs to the older upriver and Laguna de Bay political landscape.

Later genealogical traditions linking their dynasties are kept separate from contemporaneous evidence.

## Comparative historical framework for Book I

**Time focus:** Ache's youth through 1521, with later 1570–1590 sources used retrospectively and labeled as such.

| Question | What the evidence supports | What remains open |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Geography | Tondo north and Maynila south of the Pasig mouth; both linked to Manila Bay and upriver routes | Exact boundaries, channels, harbor installations and disputed settlements at a particular date |
| Regional order | Multiple kin-connected centers and negotiated authority rather than a single modern-style state | Fixed hierarchy, regular tribute rates, and a common legal system |
| Tondo's earlier history | The 900 CE Laguna Copperplate Inscription attests a literate debt/authority network in the region | Direct institutional continuity to sixteenth-century Tondo; identification of every place name |
| Ache's family conflict | Aganduru Moriz describes an unnamed Tondo cousin encroaching during Ache's mother's rule | Cousin's identity; precise territory, motive, and sequence of incidents |
| Maynila's Islam | Rulers and networks encountered in the sixteenth century show strong Muslim and Bruneian connections | When and how deeply particular families and communities adopted Islamic practices |
| Namayan | Later traditions associate an upriver polity with the Pasig corridor | Whether its remembered genealogy or institutions can be projected into Ache's lifetime |

### How to write the river corridor

The Pasig should function as a route of exchange and political leverage. River traffic makes witnesses, pilots, landing places, safe-conducts, debts, marriage ties, and access to inland supplies useful plot mechanisms. These are **plausible fictional mechanisms**, not specifically attested Ache-era regulations.

Do not convert a dynastic quarrel into a neat Tondo-versus-Manila national war. Each polity can contain competing families, merchants, crews, and households. A settlement can owe obligations to different patrons; authority may vary by season, passage, marriage, and personal allegiance.

### Chronological source layers

1. **900 CE:** Laguna Copperplate Inscription; a much earlier regional source, not a snapshot of Ache's time.
2. **1521:** Pigafetta and other voyage narratives; direct observations of Brunei and reports of a Luzon royal commander.
3. **1570–1572:** Spanish expedition accounts of Maynila and Tondo's ruling generation, decades after Ache's youth.
4. **Late sixteenth century:** *Boxer Codex* and other colonial descriptions; useful but retrospective.
5. **Later historiography:** Aganduru Moriz, Scott, Dery and modern scholarship; assess source chains and conflicting reconstructions.

### Research priorities

- Check Aganduru Moriz's original-language passage and date against Scott's paraphrase, especially the words translated as cousin, mother, and encroachment.
- Verify the 1521 Luzon-prince passages separately in Pigafetta, Mafra, and later retellings.
- Review the Clavé–Griffiths reassessment before asserting that a specific inscription toponym is unequivocally Tondo.
- Separate documentary evidence for Namayan from much later family genealogies.
