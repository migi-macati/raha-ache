Spanish and expeditionary sources use "King of Luzon" or "Prince of Luzon" for figures connected to Maynila.

This does not establish centralized sovereignty over the whole modern island of Luzon.

The working interpretation is that the title reflects foreign shorthand for the ruler of a major Luzon/Maynila polity and its maritime sphere.
