Aganduru Moriz calls Ache's maternal grandfather and Brunei ruler **Siripada**.

William Henry Scott's synthesis used in the project identifies Ache's maternal grandfather with Sultan Bulkeiah/Bolkiah.

The story bible therefore keeps three propositions separate:

1. Ache had a maternal grandfather who ruled Brunei in Aganduru's narrative.
2. Aganduru names that ruler Siripada.
3. Later scholarship identifies this ruler with Sultan Bolkiah.

The first two belong to Aganduru's account. The third is a historical identification that remains explicit rather than silently merging the two names.

## Name, title, and chronological caution

Early European accounts use the designation **Siripada** for a Brunei ruler. The recurrence of a designation does not automatically prove it is a unique personal name or establish an unbroken identity across all narratives. The identification of Ache's grandfather with **Sultan Bolkiah** is a scholarly reconstruction dependent on Brunei's disputed dynastic chronology.

Keep the three distinct assertions in the main text separate: (1) Aganduru's grandfather narrative, (2) the name/designation he uses, and (3) Scott's Bolkiah identification. The novel may dramatize a single grandfather, but the Studio research should continue to flag the identification as uncertain.
