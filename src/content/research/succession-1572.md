## What the 1572 account actually says

The anonymous *Conquest of the Island of Luzon*, dated 20 April 1572, calls a Manila chief **Laya**, describes him as recently deceased and Christian (Blair and Robertson, vol. III, printed p. 128), and says he had no inheriting children by the woman whom the Spanish writer calls his "legitimate wife." The writer nevertheless says Laya had children by other wives and that his property passed to an **unnamed legitimate nephew** (printed p. 145). These are claims in a conquest-side account, filtered through its author's categories of marriage, legitimacy and property.

Read the [English translation](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/13616/pg13616-images.html) and the [source dossier](/raha-ache/sources/relacion-1572/). The Spanish original printed by Retana, *Archivo del Bibliófilo filipino*, vol. IV (1898), has **not** been collated.

## Identity and chronology gates

The document **does not call Laya Ache or Rajah Matanda**. Identifying him with the **Raha Ache the Old** named in another 1571 record requires cross-document historical argument. It is therefore too strong to say that this passage *directly documents* Ache's wives, children, baptism, inheritance or precise death date.

If the document's date and retrospective wording are accurate, **Laya** was dead by **20 April 1572**. That terminus ante quem applies to Ache only if the Laya–Ache identification is established. An exact August 1572 death date cannot be treated as proven by this document.

The inheriting nephew is **not named**. The text cannot establish that he was Sulayman or that succession to property meant succession to political authority. It also does not establish that other children were absent: it expressly alleges children by other wives, while excluding them under the inheritance rule described by the writer.

## Fictional treatment

The novel may reconstruct Ache's household choices and death, but should not present a Spanish observer's legal categories as neutral local law, give the unnamed nephew Sulayman's identity without evidence, or invent a documented deathbed transfer of office. Separate property, household obligations and political authority. Preserve the distinction between witnessed events, later testimony and Ache's private motivations.
