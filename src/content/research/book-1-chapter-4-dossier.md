## Continuity gate (9 October 2026)

The **published** Chapter 3 ends at sunset, when Ache returns with Ula and Sulad and inspects **two clerks with two tally boards**. Under his mother's **one-season** agreement, Tondo's clerk may compare records but may not collect on Maynila's shore. Seven boats passed since midday without double collection. This arrangement actually works. Chapter 4 must start from its success, not invent a violent seizure. Chapter 2 established the father's cloth token, the unresolved compensation for damaged fishing stakes, and Ache's discovery of ambiguous river dues. Chapter 3 established Ache's partial Malay, his Muslim faith, Sulad's maternal charm and Brunei as possible but uncertain refuge.

**Publication warning:** draft PR #15 contains an alternate Chapter 3 manuscript, including Nakhoda Hamid and blue-marked poles. Those events are **not** canonical in the published Chapter 3. Do not merge or presuppose them without deliberate reconciliation.

## Historical evidence and limits

**Primary comparative evidence:** Juan de Plasencia, *Customs of the Tagalogs* (1589), in Blair & Robertson, *The Philippine Islands*, vol. VII (1903), printed pp. 164–170. The account reports that chiefs in **some** Tagalog villages held particular fishery and river-market rights; outsiders could owe payments while affiliates were exempt (p. 166). It also discusses rowing obligations, different household statuses, transfers of affiliation and arbitration. [Full transcription](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/13701/13701-h/13701-h.htm). This is **later**, geographically variable evidence, not proof of a standardized c.1500 Maynila customs bureau or migration fine.

**Retrospective narrative:** Rodrigo de Aganduru Moriz, *Historia general*, Book I, chapter XIII (seventeenth century; printed 1882), describes Ache's unnamed Tondo cousin encroaching during his mother's regency. The original Spanish passage, edition pagination and informants still need checking. Identifying this cousin with the Lakandula attested in 1571 is **inference**.

**Later port observation:** anonymous 1570 *Relation of the Voyage to Luzon*, Blair & Robertson, vol. III, pp. 72–83, records shipping and contested access decades later. Its Chinese merchants' allegations do not prove a particular earlier port law. [Text](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/13616/pg13616-images.html).

**Novel inventions:** the two clerks, tally boards, exact landing arrangements, escort disputes, family affiliation, household conversations and any political ultimatum. Do not assign a modern shoreline, exact tide hour, named tax rate or documented royal command to c.1500.

## Chapter 4 dramatic plan

**Central test:** Ache wants to prove Tondo's presence illegitimate but discovers it makes passage easier. Authority is changing through labor, protection and consent, not merely a marked boundary. His mother must offer a concrete countermeasure, and Tondo's agents must have an intelligible case.

1. **Morning count (Ache POV):** no duplicated payments. Ache shifts from counting boats to asking who guides them, feeds the rowers and controls landing priority.
2. **Worker's testimony:** a fisherman or porter connected to Chapter 1 says Tondo's escort prevented a costly delay. Ache cannot dismiss the benefit without dismissing the worker.
3. **Cargo test:** a trader needs timely unloading; Ache's proposed crew has other obligations. Tondo's crew is available. Show the price of a missed passage through baskets, food and labor, not an abstract tariff.
4. **Affiliation dispute:** a family or boat crew voluntarily accepts Tondo's patronage; Ache disputes whether permission for one voyage becomes a permanent claim. A woman or elder speaks for the household.
5. **Mother's counteroffer:** she proposes witnesses and a narrower Maynila-financed escort, and makes Ache reckon with who pays and rows. She is an active ruler, not passive or infallible.
6. **Partial victory:** Ache persuades one trader to use Maynila's service, but cannot compel all households to return. Sulad asks who will row tomorrow.
7. **Chapter-ending pressure:** an unresolved claim or specific political deadline makes Chapter 5's fight/stay/leave decision urgent. Do not invent a convenient ultimatum before deciding who can credibly issue it.

**Characters:** Ache learns that useful service wins recognition faster than birthright; mother balances protection against cost; Sulad protects his autonomy; Lakandula is a rival whose exact childhood identity is uncertain. Ula's standing can make a witness heard without making her a historically documented ritual officer. **Themes:** survival versus surrender; kinship, consent, authority, obligations. **Book payoff:** Brunei offers possible outside backing but no automatic rescue.

## 9 October 2026: source-checked dispute mechanics and scene architecture

### Source check: what a later Tagalog account actually permits

**Directly inspected translation:** Juan de Plasencia, *Customs of the Tagalogs*, dated Nagcarlán, 21 October 1589, in Blair and Robertson, *The Philippine Islands*, vol. VII (Cleveland, 1903), printed pp. **164–176**; [Project Gutenberg full text](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/13701/13701-h/13701-h.htm) (page markers embedded in text). Specific passages:

- **p. 165:** freeborn *maharlica* could be summoned to row for a chief. This does not make every rower dependent.
- **pp. 166–167:** some chiefs claimed defined fisheries and sections of rivers used as markets, with payments or membership exemptions; *aliping namamahay* also rowed but retained houses and property.
- **pp. 167–168:** the distinction between *namamahay* and *sa guiguilir* matters; Plasencia says Spanish officials' misclassification enabled abuses. This is specifically a **colonial** problem and cannot be copied into c.1500 as a known event.
- **pp. 170–171:** disputes could be heard before a chief's community, and disputants from different communities or chiefs could seek outside arbiters to avert fighting. The wording supports **a possible witnessed mediation**, not a mandatory standardized court.
- **pp. 171–172:** debt and fines could create severe service obligations. Do not infer that a single escort trip or a crew mark automatically created such a debt.

**Source criticism:** Plasencia collected testimony in different Tagalog districts about eighty years after the novel's proposed opening. He was a colonial missionary writing to inform Spanish government and church action, not an observer of Ache's household. His blanket claims and moral categories require scrutiny. Use the account as a **comparative constraint** on plausible social practices, not an eyewitness reconstruction of Maynila around 1500.

**Aganduru Móriz:** *Historia general de las islas occidentales*, first printed in the 1882 Madrid *Colección de documentos inéditos para la historia de España*, vols. LXXVIII–LXXIX; [vol. I bibliographic record](https://books.google.com/books/about/Historia_general_de_la_Islas_Occidentale.html?id=jiViHQAACAAJ). The passage about Ache's mother and Tondo cousin **has not been read in the Spanish original**. Its alleged specific wording, page and eyewitness basis remain unverified. Do not invent a quote or identify the cousin with the later Lakandula as an established fact.

### Exact narrative mechanism: a mark is not an agreement

**Fictional case selected for development:** One Maynila boat household has taken a **single paid Tondo escort** to move cargo safely through a difficult departure. The Tondo clerk's crew strokes, already introduced at the end of Chapter 3, establish who was aboard. A Tondo-associated escort organizer asserts that the crew agreed to return for a **second departure** because he provisioned and reserved rowers for both. The household's representative says only the first passage was agreed; the rowers were fed, but no second service was promised. Neither the crew count nor the cargo tally records **duration** or the spoken terms.

Do **not** call this automatic slavery, formal transfer of allegiance, an indenture contract, or a documented historical custom. It is an invented dispute about **what was actually promised**. The organizer has a real grievance if he has fed idle rowers or turned away another boat; the household has a real grievance if an ambiguous record becomes a demand it never accepted. The Tondo clerk is reluctant to expose the crew list indiscriminately, because it was collected partly to help a woman find a missing sailor. The woman in Chapter 3 is a **separate unresolved case**; do not conveniently identify her husband with the new crew.

**Household's independent preference:** Keep Tondo's reliable escort option and the crew's earned pay, while refusing the disputed second voyage. The household does not seek Ache's blanket protection, a compulsory return to Maynila patronage, or a public insult to Tondo. Its adult representative speaks in the hearing and can reject the prince's proposed solution.

**Organizer's independent preference:** Maintain predictable crews for scheduled departures and avoid an unpaid food-and-labor commitment. He can accept compensation or a witnessed future arrangement but cannot credibly use the tally board alone to prove a lifelong claim.

**Mother's affordable remedy:** She does not abolish the one-season joint tally that has prevented double collection. She convenes witnesses from both shores and seeks an acceptable person outside the immediate dispute to hear what was promised (an **invented use** of a practice described much later by Plasencia). She offers Maynila food and a small temporary escort only if named boatmen **consent** and their other obligations can be covered. The exact number of rowers, food quantity and price are left unspecified until a source-supported or internally consistent calculation exists. Both clerks can annotate their boards to distinguish **crew present** from **service owed**; the symbols and procedure are invented, not an attested port registry.

**Ache's damaging intervention:** He publicly treats the organizer's claim as Tondo's deliberate seizure of Maynila people. His charge is premature: the organizer may have witnesses to provisioning, and the household fears losing future escort service. Ache must retract the accusation without retracting the narrower principle that crew counts cannot by themselves establish further service. This is a small political success and a painful loss of confidence.

### Seven scenes, with choices and consequences

1. **Next morning, the paired boards (Ache first person).** Objective: discover the harm behind the Tondo clerk's hesitation. Obstacle: a clerk can explain who sailed but cannot answer who promised what. Ache notices the smell of wet wood, a thumb covering a row of strokes, the difference between cargo marks and human names. **Turn:** the record is useful and dangerously incomplete. Transition: a household comes to contest an entry.
2. **The household speaks (Ache).** Objective: persuade its representative to reject Tondo. Obstacle: she wants to retain Tondo's escort and rejects being made a symbol of Maynila's dignity. The disputed promise concerns **one voyage versus two**. **Turn:** Ache realizes she is not asking him to rescue her from every Tondo arrangement. The family needs provisions before a planned departure; do not invent a precise clock or seasonal wind.
3. **The organizer's accounts (Ache).** Objective: catch a lie. Obstacle: Tondo actually provisioned men and reserved a crew; witnesses disagree about whether that was for one trip or two. Show baskets of food, repaired lines, a pilot's skill, and the cost of a boat waiting for water deep enough to depart. **Turn:** there is a genuine accounting dispute, not a smoking-gun plot. Keep modern bureaucratic language out of dialogue.
4. **The public mistake (Ache).** Objective: force a ruling. Ache accuses the organizer in front of traders; the household's representative protests that he has jeopardized the escort she still needs. **Turn:** the vulnerable party refuses his protection on his terms. His mother's authority is also publicly strained.
5. **Mother's mediated bargain (Ache).** Objective: limit coercion without destroying useful exchange. An outside witness or mutually accepted arbiter hears both sides; a specific second-voyage claim is suspended unless corroborated. Maynila offers a **voluntary, provisioned** alternative, but the available boatmen have fishing and household work. **Turn:** a fair safeguard costs labor and food and cannot be generalized without resources.
6. **Sulad's refusal (Ache).** Ache volunteers Sulad to help with the alternative escort; Sulad refuses until he knows whose family loses his labor. His mother's protective cord from Chapter 3 remains his own, not a cue for another theology lesson. **Turn:** Ache must ask rather than assign. One trader accepts Maynila's service; others stay with Tondo. No instant political recovery.
7. **After the boats depart (Ache).** Ache sees a tally revised to show the difference between *present* and *promised*, yet another boat leaves under Tondo escort. His mother will have to decide whether the arrangement continues when the **one-season** trial ends; Ache sees no way to sustain a competing service at scale. **End pressure:** Chapter 5 inherits a real choice: tolerate expanding Tondo influence, provoke a fight Maynila cannot afford, or seek outside backing. Do not introduce a surprise ultimatum or invent a specific date.

### Thematic and continuity ledger

- **Main plot:** Tondo influence grows through reliable services and the interpretation of obligations, despite an agreement that works on its own stated terms.
- **Mother–son subplot:** he detects a genuine risk, but she understands that his public accusation can destroy the household's choices. Her counteroffer has a material cost.
- **Sulad subplot:** refusal and negotiated consent, building toward his independent decision about exile in Chapters 5–6.
- **Faith/identity subplot:** keep Ula and Sulad's private attachments from Chapter 3 meaningful without reducing them to demonstrations of Ache's tolerance.
- **Theme:** survival versus surrender; **subthemes:** evidence and power, patronage and consent, useful authority, family obligation, pride.
- **Setup/payoff:** Chapter 2's one-season tally, Chapter 3's seven boats and crew strokes, and the missing-sailor motive all acquire consequences. The father's cloth remains Ache's, not a second Bruneian token. The historical cousin/Lakandula identification stays uncertain in Studio.
- **What reader knows that Ache resists:** both Maynila and Tondo can help households; his claim to defend them does not authorize him to decide for them.
- **Historical matrix:** attested later: differentiated boat service, river-market privileges, outside arbitration (Plasencia 1589). Inferred: comparable negotiated patronage may have existed earlier. Invented: named people, board marks, second-voyage quarrel, mediation, alternative escort, household choice, exact political effects.
- **Geographic restraint:** Maynila shore, Tondo across the Pasig, bayward departure and changing river depth; no invented mapped landing, tide hour or modern street.
- **Lived details to earn in prose:** food for rowers, damp cords, woven shade, fishers losing labor, loading baskets, paddling cadence, a mother's insistence on being heard, uncertain spoken promises. Avoid anachronistic paper forms, privacy statutes, standardized tariff books, uniforms and mechanical clocks.
