## Continuity gate (9 October 2026)

The **published** Chapter 3 ends at sunset, when Ache returns with Ula and Sulad and inspects **two clerks with two tally boards**. Under his mother's **one-season** agreement, Tondo's clerk may compare records but may not collect on Maynila's shore. Seven boats passed since midday without double collection. This arrangement actually works. Chapter 4 must start from its success, not invent a violent seizure. Chapter 2 established the father's cloth token, the unresolved compensation for damaged fishing stakes, and Ache's discovery of ambiguous river dues. Chapter 3 established Ache's partial Malay, his Muslim faith, Sulad's maternal charm and Brunei as possible but uncertain refuge.

**Publication warning:** draft PR #15 contains an alternate Chapter 3 manuscript, including Nakhoda Hamid and blue-marked poles. Those events are **not** canonical in the published Chapter 3. Do not merge or presuppose them without deliberate reconciliation.

## Historical evidence and limits

**Primary comparative evidence:** Juan de Plasencia, *Customs of the Tagalogs* (1589), in Blair & Robertson, *The Philippine Islands*, vol. VII (1903), printed pp. 164–170. The account reports that chiefs in **some** Tagalog villages held particular fishery and river-market rights; outsiders could owe payments while affiliates were exempt (p. 166). It also discusses rowing obligations, different household statuses, transfers of affiliation and arbitration. [Full transcription](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/13701/13701-h/13701-h.htm). This is **later**, geographically variable evidence, not proof of a standardized c.1500 Maynila customs bureau or migration fine.

**Retrospective narrative:** Rodrigo de Aganduru Moriz, *Historia general*, Book I, chapter XIII (seventeenth century; printed 1882), describes Ache's unnamed Tondo cousin encroaching during his mother's regency. The original Spanish passage, edition pagination and informants still need checking. Identifying this cousin with the Lakandula attested in 1571 is **inference**.

**Later port observation:** anonymous 1570 *Relation of the Voyage to Luzon*, Blair & Robertson, vol. III, pp. 72–83, records shipping and contested access decades later. Its Chinese merchants' allegations do not prove a particular earlier port law. [Text](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/13616/pg13616-images.html).

**Novel inventions:** the two clerks, tally boards, exact landing arrangements, escort disputes, family affiliation, household conversations and any political ultimatum. Do not assign a modern shoreline, exact tide hour, named tax rate or documented royal command to c.1500.

## Chapter 4 dramatic plan

**Central test:** Ache wants to prove Tondo's presence illegitimate but discovers it makes passage easier. Authority is changing through labor, protection and consent, not merely a marked boundary. His mother must offer a concrete countermeasure, and Tondo's agents must have an intelligible case.

1. **Morning count (Ache POV):** no duplicated payments. Ache shifts from counting boats to asking who guides them, feeds the rowers and controls landing priority.
2. **Worker's testimony:** a fisherman or porter connected to Chapter 1 says Tondo's escort prevented a costly delay. Ache cannot dismiss the benefit without dismissing the worker.
3. **Cargo test:** a trader needs timely unloading; Ache's proposed crew has other obligations. Tondo's crew is available. Show the price of a missed passage through baskets, food and labor, not an abstract tariff.
4. **Affiliation dispute:** a family or boat crew voluntarily accepts Tondo's patronage; Ache disputes whether permission for one voyage becomes a permanent claim. A woman or elder speaks for the household.
5. **Mother's counteroffer:** she proposes witnesses and a narrower Maynila-financed escort, and makes Ache reckon with who pays and rows. She is an active ruler, not passive or infallible.
6. **Partial victory:** Ache persuades one trader to use Maynila's service, but cannot compel all households to return. Sulad asks who will row tomorrow.
7. **Chapter-ending pressure:** an unresolved claim or specific political deadline makes Chapter 5's fight/stay/leave decision urgent. Do not invent a convenient ultimatum before deciding who can credibly issue it.

**Characters:** Ache learns that useful service wins recognition faster than birthright; mother balances protection against cost; Sulad protects his autonomy; Lakandula is a rival whose exact childhood identity is uncertain. Ula's standing can make a witness heard without making her a historically documented ritual officer. **Themes:** survival versus surrender; kinship, consent, authority, obligations. **Book payoff:** Brunei offers possible outside backing but no automatic rescue.
