Ruma operates a working boat on the Brunei river in Book II, Chapter 3, *When the Water Turns*. She carries water jars and rope to a repair site beyond a bend, on terms agreed with Jamil. She chooses where her cargo and passengers may sit, refuses to let Ache handle a line without understanding its load, and defers the boat's handling to Tahir when he knows the current better.

During the supervised return, an unseen mooring line forces a delay. Ruma breaks her own spare pole while holding the bow clear. She loses a separately promised paying load to another carrier. Jamil agrees to replace the pole and account for the delay, but she insists on speaking for her own work and compensation. Her obligations extend beyond Ache's exercise.

**Continuity limits:** Ruma's private customers and future employment are not established. Her agreement to carry Ache as an observer does not give him authority over her vessel. Ruma, her work and the incident are fictional, not attested historical persons or events.
