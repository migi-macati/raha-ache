## Documented record

The anonymous Spanish *Relation of the Voyage to Luzon* (1570) describes a **Maynila-born merchant/supplier** whose name is transliterated **Mehomete** (also Mahomete in the text). He had previously brought provisions to Spaniards in **Cebu**. Before the 1570 return voyage to Maynila, he and his **wife and a son** had converted to Christianity. He joined Goiti's party as **interpreter**, accompanied by a **brother also from Maynila**. That brother was said to be married in town, had a house there, and approached relatives and intermediaries during the negotiations. The writer attributes several diplomatic messages to Mehomete.

**Source:** [*Relation of the Voyage to Luzon* (1570), in Blair and Robertson, vol. III, pp. 72–79](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/13616/pg13616-images.html).

## Unknown

The name before conversion (if different), age, occupation beyond supplying goods, motives for conversion, personal relationship to Ache or Sulayman, precise political allegiances, family fate and later biography are **not established**. He should not be depicted as a traitor or saint by the story-bible narrator.

## Novel use

A candidate for a deliberately researched **brief secondary first-person perspective** in Book III Bonus Chapter 11. His role embodies the ambiguity of merchant brokerage and bilingual service during conquest, but using him requires **invented inner life clearly marked as fiction**. No appearance in Book I/II: he is documented only near **1570**, not c. 1500.
