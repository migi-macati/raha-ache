## Documented record

The anonymous Spanish *Relation of the Voyage to Luzon* (1570) describes a **Maynila-born merchant/supplier** whose name is transliterated **Mehomete** (also Mahomete in the text). He had previously brought provisions to Spaniards in **Cebu**. Before the 1570 return voyage to Maynila, he and his **wife and a son** had converted to Christianity. He joined Goiti's party as **interpreter**, accompanied by a **brother also from Maynila**. That brother was said to be married in town, had a house there, and approached relatives and intermediaries during the negotiations. The writer attributes several diplomatic messages to Mehomete.

**Source:** [*Relation of the Voyage to Luzon* (1570), in Blair and Robertson, vol. III, pp. 72–79](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/13616/pg13616-images.html).

## Unknown

The name before conversion (if different), age, occupation beyond supplying goods, motives for conversion, personal relationship to Ache or Sulayman, precise political allegiances, family fate and later biography are **not established**. He should not be depicted as a traitor or saint by the story-bible narrator.

## Novel use

A candidate for a deliberately researched **brief secondary first-person perspective** in Book III Bonus Chapter 11. His role embodies the ambiguity of merchant brokerage and bilingual service during conquest, but using him requires **invented inner life clearly marked as fiction**. No appearance in Book I/II: he is documented only near **1570**, not c. 1500.

## Expanded role in the trilogy (fictional development, not biography)

**Narrative role: major supporting figure in the late Book III political arc and its Spanish-contact coda.** [The Broken Compact subplot](/raha-ache/research/book-3-merchant-and-status-conflict/) explores how a merchant-interpreter can mediate obligations among rulers, foreign merchants, and dependent households. **Any pre-1570 service to Ache is invented.** It may be used only if the project fixes Book III Chapters 6–10 late enough for credible chronology. If the chapter dates are too early, the earlier broker must instead be an independent fictional character.

**Proposed arc:** a broker initially trusts Ache's new official records to protect merchants, then discovers that mistranslation can help seize dependent labor or deny property. He becomes an intellectual foil to Ache on whether a neutral written rule can exist when powerful patrons decide what words mean. His subsequent documented work in Cebu and return with Goiti in 1570 provides a dangerous reversal. Do not settle his motives for baptism or his relationship with Spain as historical fact.

**Bonus 11:** give him an important choice, and optionally a short first-person section emphasizing family, translation and risk. **Bonus 12** appearances require additional fiction and cannot assert documented survival beyond the known record.

**Cast decision:** Mehomete can take over the as-yet unnamed interpreter/merchant-broker **function** in Book III, but does not replace Ache's established personal allies, dynastic rivals or mentors.

## Human contradictions (fictional characterization)

Historical fact establishes a Maynila-born trader and interpreter in 1570, not his personal motives. A fictional portrayal could make him proud of exact words yet willing to soften a dangerous translation to keep families safe and merchants talking. The compromise may cause harm. He resents being treated as useful rather than trustworthy. Earlier service to Ache is a speculative backstory requiring dating.
