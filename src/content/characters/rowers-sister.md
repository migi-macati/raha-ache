A recurring ordinary person from Book I's revised harbor rescue. **A fictional role, still unnamed pending character naming work.** Do not present her as a representative of all dependent households.

**What she wants:** food and work for her household without becoming a lifetime supplicant at the prince's door. She came to the hearing with her brother's paddle because no other object made the loss plain enough to the court.

**Useful trait:** direct, tenacious, perceptive about excuses and false assurances. **Fault:** frightened about the future, she can assume the worst of those with power and refuse genuinely helpful arrangements out of pride. She sometimes pressures her brother to make a claim he would rather handle himself.

**Contradiction:** resents rulers treating people as numbers, but may speak over the injured man as though she alone knows what's best. She can sympathize with the father of the child Ache saved yet resent the attention that rescue receives.

**Non-plot life:** she has a daily schedule to keep, close relationships, skills and pleasures unrelated to Ache. She should never exist only to deliver a righteous rebuke.

**Book I arc:** Chapter 2 leaves the injury's consequences open. Chapter 4 may show that harbor recordkeeping can help her trace claims yet also expose family members to compulsory service. She may distrust Maynila and Tondo alike, and need not thank Ache for his help.

All details are **novel canon**, not evidence of a particular historic family.
