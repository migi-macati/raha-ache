## Documented record

The Spanish *Relation of the Voyage to Luzon* (1570) reports **twenty Japanese living in Maynila**, and says one man approached the Spaniards, wore headgear the author associated with Christianity, **identified himself as a Christian**, and gave his name as **Pablo** (Paul). It adds that **some people said he was among Maynila's artillerymen**; that final claim is **hearsay**, not established fact.

**Sources:** [1570 primary account, vol. III, p. 82](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/13616/pg13616-images.html); [Ubaldo Iaccarino, Kyūshū–Luzon trade](https://www.ostasien-verlag.de/zeitschriften/crossroads/cr/pdf/CR_10_2013_155-194_Iaccarino.pdf).

## Unknown

Age, Japanese home region, native language, conversion circumstances, occupation, relationship with Sulayman, and fate. A historical **Japanese Christian resident** does **not** automatically imply a samurai, Spanish ally, Jesuit, Portuguese client or permanent Japanese quarter.

## Novel use

A possible supporting figure or brief POV in Book III Bonus Chapter 11, provided the authored details beyond the primary record are labeled fictional. No retroactive insertion in Ache's childhood simply because this person is attested in 1570.

## Expanded role in the trilogy (fictional development, not biography)

**Narrative role: major supporting character during Bonus Chapter 11's Maynila crisis.** His presence as a Japanese man living in Maynila who professed Christianity is attested in the Spanish relation. His age, language skills, livelihood, participation in the artillery defense and fate are unverified; an alleged gunner role is explicitly hearsay. The novel must keep the distinction visible.

[The Broken Compact subplot](/raha-ache/research/book-3-merchant-and-status-conflict/) culminates in the way foreign residents react to 1570: whose homes and cargo are defended, how people understand shared faith, and whether allegiance follows religion, protection or place of residence. **Pablo need not be a samurai, a Christian missionary or a collaborator.**

**Working dramatic function:** Pablo's encounter with Spaniards may reveal that a person can profess the same religion as outsiders without welcoming their military demands. If involved with Maynila's guns, this is a deliberately fictional reading of contemporary hearsay. Give him a significant **decision in Bonus 11** and, subject to evidence, an invented aftermath in Bonus 12 rather than extending his life backward into undocumented decades.

**Cast decision:** replace an anonymous Japanese visitor/supporting interlocutor in the 1570 sequence. Do **not** replace Panday Pira or assume Pablo designed Maynila's cannon. His narrative first-person section, if used, should be short and distinct from Ache and Mehomete.
