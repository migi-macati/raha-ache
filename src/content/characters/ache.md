## Historical record

Later secondary accounts identify Ache as the son of Maynila's previous ruler, often reconstructed as Salalila. A retrospective account attributed to Aganduru Móriz says his father died when Ache was newly born and his mother governed afterward. The original Spanish passage remains uncollated; neither the proposed father's name nor the details of the regency are independently established.

A later account attributed to Aganduru Móriz describes Ache's early Tondo rival as a cousin, but the original passage and its provenance remain unverified. The ruler's identity is uncertain; identifying him as the Lakandula documented in 1571 would overstate the evidence. In the novel's reconstruction, Tondo pressures Maynila during Ache's mother's regency, contributing to Ache's eventual departure for Brunei.

The identification of Siripada as Ache's maternal grandfather and of the unnamed regent as Siripada's daughter rests on Aganduru's later narrative; contemporary genealogical corroboration has not been established. A seventeenth-century account attributed to Aganduru says Siripada received Ache at court and later sent him with nearly two hundred vessels and about six thousand Bruneian soldiers; its original Spanish passage and numerical muster remain unverified. Pigafetta's contemporary 1521 account describes a captured Luzon prince as captain-general of the king of Brunei but does not name him Ache in that passage. Identifying the commander with Ache requires comparison with later accounts.

A later account attributed to Aganduru says Ache returned to Maynila, recovered his lands, forced Tondo back, and married a cousin sent by his grandfather. The original Spanish passage for the marriage has not been collated, and its date and kinship details remain uncertain.

Riquel's 1571 account names "Raja Ache the Old" and "Raja Soliman the Young" among Maynila's rulers. Their commonly reconstructed uncle–nephew relationship is not established by that naming alone.

A Spanish relation dated 20 April 1572 calls a recently deceased Christian Manila chief **Laya** and says his property passed to an **unnamed nephew**. Identifying Laya with Ache the Old requires comparison with other records; the relation does not name Ache, identify the nephew as Sulayman, or establish an exact baptism or death date. Its statements about wives, children and inheritance reflect the Spanish writer's categories.

## Published novel canon

Book I follows Ache as a young Maynila heir who leaves with Sulad and reaches Brunei by negotiating passage and work, without a guaranteed vessel or court reception. His age of roughly sixteen to seventeen is a development estimate, not an independently documented birth chronology. He is perceptive about boats and trade, proud of his inherited position, and often slow to recognize who bears the cost of his decisions.

Book II follows his work and contested responsibilities in Brunei, the Loue campaign and the 1521 encounter. He leaves aboard Harun's private trading vessel with limited permission and companions who choose to travel. Book III returns him to Maynila, where authority is negotiated through specific landing repairs, testimony, debts and permissions. The core finale resolves a local dispute, not a military reconquest or kingdom-wide settlement. Bonus Chapters 11–12 separately treat the Spanish arrival and Ache's death through explicitly qualified historical evidence and fictional perspectives.

Sulad is Ache's principal young companion in Book I, works separately for much of Book II, and returns by choice. The published story does not make him Ache's permanently available subordinate.

## Earlier development

An earlier outline used Pasay as Ache's same-age companion. That version is preserved on Pasay's page and in development notes but is not treated as the current companion assignment.

## Uncertainty

The alleged early Tondo cousin is not securely identified with Lakandula; the Aganduru passage and its source chain require verification. The exact birth year is unresolved. The identity of Siripada with Sultan Bolkiah is a secondary identification. The marriage account is retrospective and its original Spanish passage uncollated; the woman's kinship and the wedding date remain unverified. The Laya–Ache identification, the unnamed 1572 inheriting nephew, and Ache's exact death month remain separate evidence questions. The relationship between Rajah Sulayman and the leader killed at Bangkusay remains unresolved.

## Novel character flaw and long-range suspense

The [psychological development framework](/raha-ache/research/trilogy-character-stakes-and-reversals/) distinguishes **historically reported public events** from invented private behavior. Ache recognizes navigational and administrative patterns quickly and tends to believe understanding a system permits him to command it. That instinct helps save people and can also inflict harm on those he never consulted.

**Book I:** a rescued child and injured rower are simultaneously true; Ache can be perceptive about Tondo's trade records and still dismiss the human value of his mother's bargain. **Book II:** responsibility for crews and missing people exposes the limits of command. **Book III:** the published landing records and disputed charges create benefits and harms for particular claimants; Chapters 9–10 settle a local conflict conditionally. The older Court of Scribes, permanent navy and centralized coalition-crisis plan are development concepts, not institutions established in the published novel. **1570–72:** Ache cannot possess foreknowledge of later Philippine colonial history; the bonus chapters distinguish documented testimony from fictional reconstruction.

**Character surprise rule:** avoid treating each failure as a scripted moral lesson followed by a clean improvement. Ache can grow more effective before he grows more just.

## Characterization: the useful flaw and its human cost

**Fictional behavior, not recorded biography.** Ache often acts before asking who will bear the cost. His rescue at the quay saves a child and injures a rower. He wants gratitude and resents that another family still needs an answer. He can repair a boat expertly yet forget that Sulad's time and labor are not his to assign. When ashamed, he becomes formal; when relieved, he jokes too soon. He guards his father's cloth while mocking other people's protective objects. He can love Maynila and try to control it.

**Scene stress test:** have Ache apologize without giving an explanation of why he was right. When the apology fails, make him tempted to command forgiveness. His final progress is accepting a refusal without treating it as insubordination. [Human-character guide](/raha-ache/research/psychologically-credible-characters/).

## Attraction and intimate life (fictional development and published limits)

The development framework allows Ache romantic and sexual attraction **toward women and men**. This is **literary invention**, not a claim that the historical Raja Ache's sexuality is documented. Do not mistake an intended character dimension for a relationship already established on-page, or give his circa-1500 narration a modern identity declaration.

**Book I (~16–17):** tentative crushes, awkward jealousy, embarrassment, admiration and entirely **nonsexual** affection among peers. His deep attachment to Sulad can have ambiguous first-crush subtext but cannot require Sulad to reciprocate.

**Book II, only after Ache and the other person are adults:** the development plan proposes chemistry with Laila and potentially an independent, age-appropriate male peer such as Jalal; the latter is an *option*, not a confirmed published pairing. Laila appears in the published book, but no wedding is depicted. Consent and practical ability to refuse matter, especially around courtly rank.

**Book III:** the published core depicts no marriage or marital domestic life. Marital closeness, discord and later attractions belong to possible future or retrospective development, and must not be described as existing chapter canon. Respect the distinct identity of Ache's historically reported wife, whose name and intimate biography remain unverified.
