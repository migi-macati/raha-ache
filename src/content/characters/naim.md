Naim owns a small cargo boat operating between Brunei's river and outer settlements. Ruma introduces him as an owner willing to speak with Ache about passage. In Book II, Chapter 10, Naim is repairing a scraped bow and refuses to turn his local trading route into a voyage to Luzon.

Naim's refusal forces Ache to distinguish a passage that is actually offered from a vessel he wishes he could command. He helps Ache assess the physical repair and points him toward the practical risk Harun must accept.

**Continuity limits:** Naim is fictional. His private trade obligations and later movements are unestablished; his refusal is not evidence of a documented historical dispute.
