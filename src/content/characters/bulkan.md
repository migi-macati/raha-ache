Bulkan is a veteran captain and practical adviser in Ache's Maynila household. He is mentioned in Book I Chapter 1 and appears directly in Chapters 5–6. He knows the damaged outrigger cannot be made ready by wishful thinking, and he challenges Ache's proposal to station men at the landing: whom exactly would they stop, and who would decide whether a hired crew had been paid fairly?

In Chapter 6 Ache considers taking Bulkan along, but his mother needs the captain to remain in Maynila. Bulkan does **not** accompany Ache and Sulad on their departure. Instead he asks questions about the first landing, watches, passengers, cargo, and who decides when a vessel is in difficulty. He refuses to answer those questions for Ache. His role is to test the young man's readiness without offering a private force or a guaranteed rescue.

**Character limits:** Bulkan can be strict and skeptical of improvised commands, but the published chapters do not establish an independent military expedition or a secret plan to follow Ache. Earlier exploratory material about accompanying Ache and Pasay belongs to a superseded version and must not be presented as current canon.

**Evidence boundary:** Bulkan is a fictional character. His advice is part of the novel's reconstruction of maritime work, not testimony about an identifiable historical captain.
