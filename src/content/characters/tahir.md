Tahir is the experienced poleman handling Ruma's boat on the Brunei river in Book II, Chapter 3, *When the Water Turns*. He refuses Ache's tempting inside passage because he cannot safely hold the loaded stern near the piles. On the return journey he agrees to follow Safi's timber boat only after its crew has cleared the outer post and a visible signal has been negotiated.

When a submerged mooring line rises across their course, Tahir stops rather than gamble on its depth or fastening. He directs the boat away with Ruma's help, waits for a safer current and retains control of the maneuver. Ache's contribution is to accept and communicate that decision, not to substitute for Tahir's seamanship.

**Continuity limits:** Tahir's family, rank, other voyages and future allegiance are unestablished. His river judgments are fictional narrative constructions, not evidence of a documented Bruneian seamanship procedure.
