Ula is an older woman trusted by river households in the **published Book I narrative**. In Chapter 3, *The Muslim Way*, she asks Ache's mother to send him with her to a gathering upriver, where families prepare boats, make promises, and observe different practices before travel. She does not require Ache to join in rites he does not share. Her authority comes partly from relationships and knowledge of people along the river; the story does not assign her a formal office or make her the voice of every local tradition.

Ula sees through Ache's impatience, but she is not a source of guaranteed answers. In Chapter 5 she agrees only to inquire through a woman whose brother sometimes carries goods upriver. In Chapter 6 she makes Ache clarify the unnamed captain he seeks, the message to be carried, and what he will pay for an inquiry even if it fails. She cannot promise a captain, passage to Brunei, or an answer on a particular day. Her network consists of people who may refuse.

**Character limits:** She enjoys being consulted and can be sharp or impatient; her advice should emerge from a particular circumstance rather than turn every scene into a lesson. Sulad's inherited protective cord belongs to his own family, and Ula does not claim to speak for him. Ula remains in the Maynila river world when Ache and Sulad depart in Chapter 6.

**Evidence boundary:** Ula and her actions are fictional. Earlier exploratory material linked her to a different Ache–Pasay version; that planning does not supersede the published Ache–Sulad chapters. Avoid treating her practices as a documented reconstruction of a named historical religious leader.
