A recurring **fictional** clerk introduced at the end of Book I Chapter 3. He compares boat and crew counts under the temporary joint arrangement between Tondo and Maynila.

**What he wants:** a traceable account that will help prevent double collections and, genuinely, help a family find a missing sailor. He also wants to keep his position and not be blamed when powerful patrons demand information.

**Useful trait:** conscientious about numbers and small acts of human distress. **Fault:** when challenged, he hides behind “I only record” despite understanding that a list of rowers can be used to enforce debts or obligations. He likes being treated as indispensable and is embarrassed by not being able to answer who may inspect his board.

**Contradiction:** mistrusts corrupt collectors, yet may give a well-connected claimant information he would withhold from a stranger. He is proud of serving Tondo even when he disagrees with a Tondo superior.

**Non-plot life:** family obligations, fatigue, a need to be paid, irritation with sloppy colleagues, and a fondness for neat tallies. In private he may make a joke that Ache would never hear.

**Published Book I arc:** Chapter 3 reveals his reason for counting crews and his uncertainty about who may inspect the board. In Chapter 4 he hesitates when Ache tests whether he would disclose a rower's whereabouts to a powerful claimant. He later states that the marks prove who traveled, not who owes another voyage, and approaches the workers to ask before recording their names. The Chapter 2 proposal for a Tondo clerk is not an appearance by this individual.

**Development boundary:** His private motives, family obligations and possible future disclosure choices above are characterization plans, not events established by the published chapters. He can act imperfectly without secretly plotting against Ache.

The joint tally office and the clerk are *invented narrative devices*, not a documentary reconstruction of a named 1500 port official.
