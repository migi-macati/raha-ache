Raya is a **fictional maker and repairer of landing steps and timberwork** in the published Book III narrative. She enters in Chapter 4, *After the Passage*, when Ache asks about a post strong enough to take a boat's pull. She distinguishes a step that can bear a person's weight from a mooring post that must withstand a vessel. She inspects Madan's timber, the soft bank and the route used by Dala before proposing what work can safely be attempted.

Her sister's son assists her at the landing. She tests wood and ground herself and refuses to call a sounding stake a mooring post. In Chapter 5 she permits only limited use of the repaired step and insists on inspecting it again after high water. Her work cannot be expanded by Ache's hopes, an undelivered timber offer or another household's ungranted access.

Raya continues to assess the landing's physical limits through Chapters 6–10. Her judgment depends on the condition of materials, available labor and the consent of people whose paths or goods would be affected. She does not command all river traffic or guarantee that a repaired step can take a loaded boat.

**Evidence boundary:** Raya, her family and the repairs are novel fiction. Her practical observations are part of the story's reconstruction, not a documented early-sixteenth-century engineer, public works office or measured Pasig River survey.
