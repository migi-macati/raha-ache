Sima is a **fictional woman responsible for moving her household's goods through the Maynila landing** in Book I, Chapter 4, *The Second Crossing*. She has paid an organizer for a first transfer by hired Tondo rowers. He claims she promised a second; she says she only agreed to discuss it if she needed the crew again. The rowers' later complaint that the organizer has not paid their wages is a separate debt. Sima's payment does not establish that the workers received theirs.

Sima knows the practical cost of idle boats, waiting cargo, and a bargain recorded inaccurately. She can move baskets with her own people but cannot simply send all of them away from their other work. When Ache publicly accuses the organizer, she interrupts him because he has spoken for her without permission. She will pay for work done, insists on knowing the price before hiring again, and accepts a crew from either shore if the men consent.

By the end of Chapter 4 she has negotiated directly with another boat owner. Chapters 5 and 6 mention her earlier payment while the rowers' wages remain unresolved. She does not need Ache to settle every part of her business, and she is neither the injured rower's sister nor the organizer's relative.

**Evidence boundary:** Sima, her household, and these negotiations are invented for the novel. The story does not establish her family history, exact status beyond managing the cargo, or subsequent fate. Do not infer historical testimony about an identifiable sixteenth-century merchant from her scenes.
