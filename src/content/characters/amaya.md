Amaya is a **fictional woman who manages household goods, supplies and obligations** in the published Book III narrative. In Chapter 1, *The Bay That Forgot Him*, she recognizes Ache from his boyhood, when she brought baskets of river greens to the kitchen. She remembers his behavior more clearly than the status he hopes to recover.

In Chapters 2–6 she participates in the work and testimony around the landing while keeping track of what people can actually spare. Ache comes to depend on supplies and labor that are not his to promise. By Chapter 7 he owes Amaya replacement cloth. She refuses to let a proposed repair or another claimant's opinion erase the debt, and she decides for herself whether to lend more rice or cord. She invites a witness to inspect any replacement she eventually accepts.

In Chapter 10 she shows Ache how to pack borrowed jars so they do not knock together on the path. Her instruction arises from responsibility for the goods, not from a duty to teach Ache a moral lesson. Her work and other household obligations continue regardless of his political concerns.

**Evidence boundary:** Amaya, her household, the cloth debt and these transactions are fictional. The story does not establish her exact legal status, an official administrative role or a historical record of the exchange.
