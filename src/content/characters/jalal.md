Jalal is a local noble encountered by Ache at a fictional eastern landing in Book II, Chapter 5, *The Price of Passage*. He is witty and socially connected but cannot promise the labor of Saliha's carriers, the use of his aunt's store, or passage on independent boats. His exact dynastic position remains developmental; the chapter does not establish a wider Sulu alliance.

He questions Ache's version of the disputed cargo settlement and refuses to pledge other people's vessels. Ache's restraint interests him, but Karim's public humiliation has created a cost for Jalal among his own kin.

## Human contradictions (fictional characterization)

His generosity and wit bring people together, but he makes promises faster than his Sulu kin can afford. He jokes when frightened and overlooks workers while treating fellow nobles warmly. He may withdraw aid from Ache to meet his own obligations. This hurts a friend without proving either man a villain.

## Possible adult chemistry (candidate only)

Jalal is a promising **possible adult male romantic peer** for Ache, not automatically his lover. First verify and fix his adult age, lack of close kinship to Ache, and independence from Ache's command; all must be established before any adult-romance scene.

If chosen, let attraction emerge from shared humor, meaningful disagreement and respect for each other's separate maritime obligations. Jalal must remain free to choose Sulu's interests over Ache, and neither man's attraction automatically determines the other's identity. If the role works better as platonic, keep the friendship instead.
