An unnamed Bruneian royal woman described in a later account attributed to Aganduru Móriz as Ache's wife after his return to Maynila. The account reportedly calls her Siripada's granddaughter and Ache's first cousin, with a marriage arrangement made while Ache was in Brunei. The original Spanish passage cited at CODOIN 78, printed pp. 59–60, has not been independently collated. Her exact kinship, the marriage's chronology and the account's wording remain unverified; see the [marriage evidence record](/raha-ache/events/ache-marriage/).

## Novel development

Putri Laila Nur is a separate working fictional love interest. The project has not decided that Laila is the historical cousin/wife.

## Human contradictions (novel development)

**The woman described in the retrospective account remains unnamed and her private life undocumented.** In fiction she should not exist as a reward for Ache's return. She may want her own Bruneian kin and household protected and sometimes judge Maynila customs unfairly; she must have a specific skill, disliked chore, intimacy, and political disagreement. Her relationship to the separate fictional Laila Nur remains undecided. All emotions here are invented.

## A marriage with its own emotional life

The novel should make the wife an adult woman with desires, preferences, political responsibilities, affectionate habits and frustrations of her own. Marriage may involve affection and companionship alongside political duty and sharp disagreement. The historical source does **not** establish what either spouse privately desired.

Ache's attraction to women and men should not automatically make him an unfaithful husband or render her marriage a false front. The adult emotional lives must be developed with both partners' autonomy. Fictional Laila's relation to this unnamed historical wife remains unresolved.
