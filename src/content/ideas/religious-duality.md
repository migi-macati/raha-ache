A current Book III development principle: Ache’s Maynila does not immediately replace older religious practices with a single uniform Islamic settlement.

The project preserves religious plurality and tension as part of institution-building. This is novel development unless and until specific practices, communities, and historical arrangements are separately sourced.

The narrative purpose is to make religion part of lived politics, kinship, ritual, legitimacy, and personal belief rather than a simple label applied to the polity.

## Foreign contacts and religious heterogeneity

The [foreign-presence dossier](/raha-ache/research/foreign-contacts-ache-era/) confirms that Islamic and Indic influences could be carried through different Malay, Javanese, Arab/Persian-associated and Chinese Muslim networks across generations. An individual's ancestry, learned law, commercial identity, ritual practice and allegiance cannot be read from one religious label.

For the novel, Ache's Muslim formation, Ula's older traditions and Bruneian court practice may coexist or disagree. Avoid assigning one uniform faith to all residents of Maynila, or claiming that an Arab person personally converted Ache's dynasty without direct evidence.
