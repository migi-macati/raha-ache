The project treats Maynila as a port-centered polity whose effective power depended on relationships and flows rather than modern territorial borders.

Kinship, trade, tribute, alliances, military force, river and bay access, and control of exchange all matter to how rule works in the story world.

This is a framing model for writing and reconstruction, not a claim that sixteenth-century Maynila possessed the fixed administrative territory of a modern state.

## Historical operating model

A port's authority can be measured by control over **passage, brokers, storage, debts, crews, protection, prestige gifts and access to overseas commercial routes**. The [foreign-presence dossier](/raha-ache/research/foreign-contacts-ache-era/) distinguishes direct Chinese/Japanese/Malay contacts from goods reaching Maynila through intermediaries. A Bruneian trader can convey Indian-made cloth without an Indian resident; a Javanese prestige claim is not proof of territorial occupation.

As Ache gradually formalizes control in Book III, harbor rules can stabilize trade while limiting the autonomy of local traders, coastal communities and visiting merchants. This supplies real costs and constituencies for his fictional coalition crisis.
