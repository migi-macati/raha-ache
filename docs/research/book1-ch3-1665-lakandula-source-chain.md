# Source chain: Lakandula anchorage-fee claim (9 October 2026)

## Research finding

Luciano P. R. Santiago, “The Houses of Lakandula, Matandá and Solimán (1571–1898): Genealogy and Group Identity,” *Philippine Quarterly of Culture and Society* 18, no. 1 (March 1990), 39–73, footnote 18 (printed p. 71), identifies the transmission of a purported 1665 genealogical memorandum prepared for Don Juan Macapagal, great-grandson of Lakandula. According to Santiago, the text was first published by Isabelo de los Reyes, *El Folk-Lore Filipino*, vol. II (1889), pp. 219–221, and translated by William Henry Scott, “Isabelo de los Reyes, Father of Philippine Folklore,” in *Cracks in the Parchment Curtain*, pp. 260–262. Santiago uses Scott's translation and cites Archivo General de Indias, Filipinas 43, docs. 113–114, and Filipinas 348, ff. 76–78v.

Sources: Santiago's article https://www.jstor.org/stable/29791998 (footnote 18 is readable at https://www.scribd.com/document/925170845/Ancestry ); de los Reyes 1889 bibliographic record https://openlibrary.org/books/OL25530796M/El_folk-lore_Filipino and scanned volume https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:El_folk-lore_Filipino_(IA_aep1355.0001.001.umich.edu).pdf .

**Provenance:** purported 1665 family memorandum (original not examined) -> de los Reyes 1889 publication -> Scott English translation -> Santiago 1990 quotation. Neither the 1665 original nor de los Reyes's pp. 219–221 were directly inspected in this pass. The archival references may be related records; their precise contents remain unverified.

The narrative claims that Lakandula of Tondo collected Chinese anchorage fees, withheld sails and rudders, and controlled purchases. It is a retrospective family-privilege account, not eyewitness evidence for Ache's childhood. The separate 1570 Spanish report of Chinese merchants complaining about seized gear concerns rulers of Maynila. Do not conflate the two polities, periods, events, or motives. Plasencia's 1589 description of some Tagalog chiefs controlling fisheries and river-market privileges is a more direct comparative account but still cannot establish the novel's particular landing charges.

**Fiction treatment:** Preserve Hamid's provisional unloading and the blue poles as inventions. For Chapter 4, distinguish control of a berth, rights asserted over traders, actual collection, and allegiance by households. No immediate prose rewrite is justified by this source lead.

**Next verification:** Read the 1889 printed Spanish transcription and locate the 1665 instrument and 1667 Macapagal merit/encomienda petitions, catalogued as AGI INDIFERENTE,121,N.81 and FILIPINAS,43,N.27. Compare the manuscript to the later published translations. Do not claim independent Chinese merchant testimony has been located.
