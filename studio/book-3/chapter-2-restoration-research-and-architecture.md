# Book III Chapter 2: The terms of restoration

**Packet:** RA-B3C2-ARCH-01. **State:** Research and six-scene planning, not novel canon or published historical fact. **Read against:** published Book II Chapter 10, published Book III Chapter 1 `src/content/chapters/book-3-chapter-1.md`, and the earlier handoff `studio/book-3/chapter-2-handoff-causal-gates.md` in draft research PR #69. **Core promise:** Maynila's restoration is completed by Chapter 2's end; Chapter 3 owns the settlement with Tondo. Preserve 30 core chapters and Book III bonus 11–12.

## What the published story actually establishes

- Ache arrived on **Harun's privately owned trading vessel**, with cloth and resin to sell, a limited Bruneian letter, Isan's language assistance and Sulad's voluntary company. Harun can depart after selling. There is no armed expedition, ship transfer, promised troops or automatic right to land.
- The landing woman and keeper granted **three nights' paid lodging in a tool room**. Ache paid with a carved box and thread from his own gifts. Lodging confers no authority over the landing. He may work for additional pay.
- Amaya, who has kept the household operating, says Ache's mother ordered the hall maintained for work and shelter before she left. Amaya retains a **piece of the old red post** as material evidence. Of the three witnesses to the instruction, one is dead and another went inland. Ache's mother's present whereabouts remain unresolved.
- Banu carried Ache's question to the far bank and returned word that **a man speaking for a boat-owning house** would come. Banu explicitly disclaims endorsement of Ache. No named Tondo ruler has yet made a personal commitment.
- Sulad returned a borrowed net weight to the old worker's sons and found paid work at the nets. He will tell what he knows; he has not agreed to fight or accept office.
- Chapter 1 ends with Ache planning to seek paid work, ask Amaya for witnesses and await the far-bank representative. The next chapter must begin with that concrete position, not with a prepared army or a wedding.

## Historical evidence boundaries

**Documented or locatable:** Rodrigo de Aganduru Móriz, *Historia general de las islas occidentales*, vol. 1 (Madrid: M. Ginesta, 1882), is catalogued as CODOIN vol. 78; its exact Spanish text at printed pp. 59–60 has **not** been collated. The Google Books catalogue identifies the edition but cannot substantiate a particular passage. Luis C. Dery's 1992 NAST study (printed p. 617) presents a *hypothesis* that Matanda, Lakandula and Sulayman's father were brothers. This is neither direct testimony about the 1520s nor evidence of a particular Lakandula military encounter.

**Inference only:** Political claims in the Manila Bay region could be negotiated through kin, traders, boat owners and household obligations. Specific early-1520s offices, military formations, exact restoration date, location of disputed landing, age and identity of a Tondo ruler, and any marriage sequence remain unverified.

**Invented for the novel:** Amaya, Banu, Harun, the red post fragment, named household repairs, landing-fee arrangements, the hearing, an alliance of independent crews and any tactical struggle in this chapter. They may be plausible reconstructions but cannot be narrated as established history in notes.

**Forbidden shortcuts:** No army appears because an outline says 'expedition'; no marriage is retroactively inserted; no named Lakandula is assigned to an undocumented battle; no Bruneian royal letter overrides local consent; no taking Harun's ship or cargo; no miraculous three-day conquest.

## Dramatic design

**Theme:** A returned claimant cannot own the work of those who kept his home alive.

**Ache's need:** Win jurisdiction over the principal Maynila landing and household, while acknowledging the people who maintained both. His inherited claim is one argument among several.

**Amaya's choice:** Speak truthfully about the mother's instruction and repairs, even where it limits Ache. She must be able to refuse him without being punished or transformed into a convenient ally.

**Sulad's choice:** Earn his living and evaluate Ache's conduct before committing to a limited task. He is not a hereditary retainer.

**Isan's choice:** Translate and negotiate on his own terms. No one treats his work as ownership or perpetual service.

**Harun's choice:** Complete his trade and depart when profitable. A later loan, charter or introduction would require an explicit price and acceptance; his refusal must remain viable.

**Far-bank representative's choice:** Protect a real boat-owning house's landing rights, labor and access. He may bargain or refuse. Do not use 'Lakandula' as a shorthand for all Tondo interests.

**The absent mother:** Her instruction is contested in meaning, not forged into an all-purpose deed. Do not disclose her location casually.

## Six scenes with irreversible consequences

1. **The Piece of Red Wood:** A hearing under the landing shelter, with Amaya, the keeper, household workers and a far-bank representative. Ache publicly withdraws his demand to expel occupants. A witness distinguishes the mother's order to maintain the hall from a transfer of ownership. Outcome: a **conditional right to present a claim and organize maintenance**, not recognized rule.
2. **The Boats That May Refuse:** Owners decide which landings and freight they will support. Ache offers repair labor, limited fees and dispute hearing; an owner refuses. Sulad takes a specific paid role only after hearing the terms. Harun states his departure window. Outcome: a **small, bounded coalition** with named material commitments.
3. **Between Two Monsoons** (working title, check actual seasonal plausibility): **Explicit weeks or months pass.** The coalition repairs a channel marker, roof and mooring, purchases food, pays workers and loses a supporter. A household resists Ache's dues. Outcome: enough local capacity for a defensible landing, at a visible social cost. Avoid exact season labels until weather/chronology checked.
4. **The Narrow Landing:** A rival boat-owning interest blocks an access channel or landing. Ache attempts a measured movement using only boats and people already earned. He misjudges the civilian effect: a family's stored goods are damaged or a fishing crew loses access. Outcome: tactical opening, moral debt and a political demand for redress.
5. **The Price of a Door:** Ache can press the advantage by destroying or seizing a rival's livelihood, but refuses. He negotiates reparations and limited reciprocal passage, at the cost of his own coalition's patience. Outcome: a credible path to recognition, not total domination.
6. **Maynila Open Again:** A witnessed settlement returns **principal landing access, household jurisdiction and recognized right to hear local disputes** to Ache, with workers' use and repair claims protected. The far bank retains its boats and its right to contest future levies. Ache receives authority from local acceptance and enforceable arrangements, not the foreign letter. End with the injured household's unresolved grievance; Chapter 3 handles Tondo's wider settlement.
