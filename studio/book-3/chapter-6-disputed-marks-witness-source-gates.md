## Canon ledger: what is actually in dispute

The keeper cut **one long mark for one paid journey**, **two short marks for two baskets**, and **explicitly said**: “Nothing here promises another journey.” The small-boat owner was present and touched each short cut, but may not have heard or processed that final sentence amid the boat's movement. Ache heard it and failed to ask the owner to repeat his understanding. The cargo owner says she paid for one journey. The small-boat owner's crewman says his employer instructed him to return for a second journey, interpreting the two short marks as permission for two trips. The owner himself is **absent at Chapter 5's end**. The crewman was keeping the bow clear, so his hearing was partial; Ache and the keeper both saw the owner touch the marks and assumed comprehension. The owner may have misunderstood without lying; the keeper may have recorded the intended agreement accurately without communicating it adequately. The original strip must remain intact as contested evidence.

The step is a **repaired upper landing**, usable only for limited separately carried goods with a line at the **old upper post**. Raya demands a new inspection after high water; the sounding stake is not a mooring post; the lower berth is unfinished. Dala controls the route beside her fishing space and has an unsettled broken frame/lost-catch claim. Raya's household covering is still owed, as is **one cloth from Ache to Amaya**. The basket maker has been paid for three accepted baskets, a fourth was returned, and a short trial binding for Dala has **not yet been made or accepted**. Madan's timber is **already paid**. Do not turn any of these into a generic harbor tax or falsely mark a debt discharged.

Sera and Lana are independent merchants with separate transactions. The woman who paid for the disputed boat journey is the **unnamed cargo owner in Chapter 5's closing scene**; avoid identifying her as Sera or Lana without text support. The boat owner and his crewman have distinct interests; the crewman cannot bind his employer to new terms or be punished for an instruction he reports honestly.

## Evidence tiers and the crucial literacy distinction

**Early written debt evidence, distant in time and form:** The Laguna Copperplate Inscription, dated to 900 CE, records a debt clearance in a Malay-language inscription with Indic script. Clavé and Griffiths (2022) analyze its Luzon/Java/maritime context and the dangers of projecting a simple homogeneous “Malay World.” It demonstrates that a written record of obligation existed in the wider region centuries before Ache's fictional landing. It does **not** document early-1520s Maynila's tally-strip system, harbor procedure, public hearings, scribal office, or this family of characters. Do not claim that precolonial Luzon had no writing or that all agreements were necessarily oral. Equally, do not import the inscription's official form as Ache's ready-made administrative practice.

Source: Elsa Clavé and Arlo Griffiths, “The Laguna Copperplate Inscription: Tenth-Century Luzon, Java, and the Malay World,” *Philippine Studies: Historical and Ethnographic Viewpoints* 70:2 (2022), https://archium.ateneo.edu/phstudies/vol70/iss2/2/ ; Antoon Postma, “The Laguna Copper-Plate Inscription: Text and Commentary,” *Philippine Studies* 40:2 (1992), https://archium.ateneo.edu/phstudies/vol40/iss2/3/ . These references establish the published scholarship; no verbatim ancient text is quoted here.

**Later Tagalog writing, even further removed from this fictional procedure:** Two land contracts in a 1629 Manila dossier are written in baybayin, with two more in romanized Tagalog, discussed by Regalado Trota José in *Spanish Pacific, 1521–1815* (2020), pp. 91–114, https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/spanish-pacific-15211815/don-luis-castilla-offers-to-sell-land-in-manila-1629/D1BE244B891CEF9E8DF4BCA76E3ADDCD . The documents are colonial-era and cannot establish the script, profession, contract terms or literacy distribution of Ache's early-sixteenth-century landing. **Editorial correction:** “Avoid introducing an unearned alphabetic/scribal office” is a narrative gate, not a claim that writing did not exist.

**Later social testimony, not a transferable law code:** Juan de Plasencia's *Customs of the Tagalogs*, dated 21 October 1589, describes distinctions in rank, service, property and debt in a missionary account recorded decades after the story's period and under colonial pressure. Consult the Blair/Robertson translation in *The Philippine Islands*, vol. 7, https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/The_Philippine_Islands%2C_1493%E2%80%931898/Volume_7/Documents_of_1589 . This cannot justify forcing Dala, Raya, the crewman or any household into a standard labor category; no precise `aliping namamahay` classification, sale of dependents or transfer of a human being belongs in this chapter.

**Contemporary regional commerce, not a local docket:** Tomé Pires's *Suma Oriental* (composed 1512–1515; Cortesão edition, 1944) records Luzon–Borneo–Malacca trade information. A University of the Philippines *Asian Studies* discussion cites Pires on Luzon foodstuffs, wax and honey and commerce through Borneo, https://asj.upd.edu.ph/mediabox/archive/Vol15%2CApr-Aug-Dec1977.pdf . This supports a plausible trading setting and perishable cargo stakes; it does not identify Ache's boat owner, a specific shipment, tally convention or local judgment. Original Portuguese has not been collated here.

## Six scene gates and distinct physical turns

1. **A load already prepared.** The young crewman returns with a second load arranged by his employer. His pay for waiting is threatened; a buyer expects the cargo. Raya has not cleared the step after the high water. Ache must decline an immediate second landing **without declaring the owner a cheat**. End with a specific cargo owner facing a measurable delay.
2. **Owner returns, hearing differs.** The owner remembers touching two short cuts and hearing “two”; the keeper and cargo owner recall “two baskets.” A witness working at the bow could not hear the entire exchange. Ache catches himself favoring the keeper because he needs the landing to appear orderly. An offer to pay the crewman's wait becomes a material choice, not a lecture.
3. **What a mark can and cannot hold.** Test distinct signs for goods, journeys, time, and the path, using a loose strip or separate pieces while preserving the original. Read back orally; each participant repeats in their own words. A trial agreement fails because **Dala was not consulted**. Avoid declaring a standardized script, public court, or permanent tax office.
4. **The unrelated claim.** Dala sees her damaged frame claim displayed beside the new tally and hears someone infer that a future fiber binding has settled her account. She shows the unmade binding and unpaid catch. The keeper visibly corrects the interpretation **without scratching away the earlier record**. Ache must spend his own time or goods rather than taking Amaya's stores.
5. **A procedure tested under pressure.** Raya physically tests the step, Dala agrees or refuses path access, the owner sets transport terms, a witness hears the oral readback. A worker refuses an imposed service obligation. Delay costs a sale or a paid day's work; no clean institutional victory.
6. **Limited resolution, credible appeal.** The second trip was **never authorized** by the first strip. A separately negotiated new trip may be accepted if safe and paid, or the owner may use the far bank. Keep the crewman's lost time visible. The final test is someone who asks whether the same hearing applies when Ache himself owes cloth to Amaya. This leads into Chapter 7 without conjuring a council, dynasty or army.

## Character and literary QA

Ache speaks in first person, sees only what he is physically present to witness, and misreads motives when his own authority is at stake. The keeper protects the original tally but concedes that correct cuts did not ensure shared meaning. The owner has real costs, not villainous convenience; the crewman has wages, pride and the possibility of refusing the next assignment. Raya is responsible for safety, Dala for her fishing space, Amaya for household property, and the basket maker for her materials. The scene should contain rope, mud, weight, weather, wages and bodily work rather than six variations of a public hearing. Do not import anachronistic “appeal court,” “contract,” “tax code,” “registry,” or “administration” into character dialogue. These can be analytical Studio terms only.
