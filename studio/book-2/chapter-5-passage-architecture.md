# Book II Chapter 5 — Passage architecture (development, not canon)

**Packet:** RA-B2C5-PLAN-01. **Provisional title:** *The Price of Passage*; *Pirate Life* remains the canonical outline's working name until integrated prose review. **POV:** Ache, first person, limited knowledge. **State:** six-scene causal plan, no reader-facing manuscript. **Sources:** see `chapter-5-sulu-period-and-passage-research.md` on this research branch. The described voyage, negotiations, characters' agreements and danger are fictional.

## Book-level function

Chapter 4 ended with Nasir missing, Hamzah's vessel seized, the cargo owners awaiting separate accounts, and Jamil withdrawing Ache's right to carry passage orders. Chapter 5 must **not** reward Ache with a new ship or command merely for being ashamed. Its central contest is over who can grant passage, who bears risk, and who can legitimately use force. The Sulu/Mindanao sphere must have its own decision-makers, not exist as a training ground lent to a Bruneian prince. A limited agreement, earned through listening and material sacrifice, can prepare the organized campaign of Chapter 6 without already granting its command.

**Opening material facts:** Nasir's whereabouts are unknown, his sister has rice but no answer, Bakar's boat and crew remain his to direct, Safi independently asks bank communities for information, Hamzah has debts and a missing vessel, Jamil has not restored Ache's authority. **Prohibited shortcut:** finding Nasir alive or dead by coincidence merely to free the plot. The search can produce an independently verified lead, or remain open; any discovery must be supported by a credible witness chain.

## Six scenes and escalating decisions

1. **The Bank That Answered Nothing (Brunei).** Ache accompanies Bakar only if Bakar consents to take him as an observer. Searchers compare riverbank testimony, distinguish debris from proof, and carry back an unsatisfying account to Nasir's sister. Ache wants to turn a rumor into a direction; a working pilot refuses to risk another crew on it. A visiting merchant's request for a careful cargo witness introduces a possible eastward passage, not a rescue mission. **Decision:** Ache must report uncertainty exactly, even when it costs him his one chance to appear useful. **End turn:** the merchant will take a recorder, not an officer.

2. **The Owner of the Fare (Brunei departure).** Merchant, pilot and crew separately specify loads, compensation, stopping conditions and who can decide to put ashore. Ache cannot promise Jamil's protection or passage for Nasir's searchers. The boat owner takes a genuine risk in accepting him; a crew member may refuse. **Decision:** Ache accepts a subordinate role and a defined liability for his own food/space. **End turn:** one claimant disputes an item in the manifest, threatening the departure and exposing the limits of his marks.

3. **A Shore with Several Voices (fictional Sulu-area landing; exact harbor TBD).** A negotiated landing reveals overlapping obligations to hosts, cargo owners and pilots. Introduce Jalal as a local noble with a real constituency and imperfect authority, not as a guaranteed prince-ally or lover. He can be charming to Ache and careless toward workers; his decision cannot bind all crews. **Decision:** Ache refuses to translate one man's permission into general consent. **End turn:** two parties claim the same cargo or passage payment, and a small armed escort approaches. No ethnic identity or named raiding institution is assigned without evidence.

4. **What They Call Taking.** An opposing party seeks compensation for a prior seizure and threatens to hold the disputed cargo. Ache recognizes the similarity to Hamzah's losses but cannot determine the truth by rhetoric. Witnesses differ over whether the cargo was a debt payment, a gift, or a theft. A worker or boat owner refuses to be sacrificed for a noble settlement. **Decision:** Ache must choose between quick coercion, which he is not empowered to order, and delaying departure to hear competing claims. **End turn:** the delay imposes a measurable cost on his own hosts.

5. **The Unpaid Share.** A settlement requires actual redistribution of goods, labor, or a guarantee with named guarantors; a clever speech is insufficient. Jalal's kin/crew set a boundary; Ache's family name does not pay for it. A partial bargain protects people immediately but creates a legitimate political enemy and a debt that will matter later. **Decision:** Ache sacrifices a personal advantage or reputation rather than treating another crew's risk as his own to spend. **End turn:** the opposing party accepts the immediate terms without accepting Ache's version of events.

6. **The Price of Passage.** Ache returns or sends an accountable report through a traceable carrier, distinguishing what he witnessed from what others told him. A limited merchant recommendation or invitation is plausible; independent naval command is not. Nasir's search and Hamzah's claims remain open and explicitly carried into later chapters. Jalal may decline future military aid while remaining interested in Ache's judgment. **Final image:** a physical record of a passage whose last mark belongs to someone else, contrasting Ache's earlier solitary count-stick. **Handoff:** Chapter 6 must earn any wider coalition and command on page, not inherit it from this chapter's tentative agreement.

## Character and dramatic QA

- **Ache:** agency through accurate testimony, restraint and personal cost; still capable of pride and overinterpretation. He cannot solve each scene by apologizing or speaking well.
- **Nasir's sister:** demands traceable action and retains her own interests; not a device to absolve Ache.
- **Jamil:** retains command in Brunei, his mistaken assessment and restitution duties; cannot simply commission a replacement mission to restore Ache's standing.
- **Bakar, Safi and working crews:** independent authority over their labor, safety and boats; the narrator cannot turn them into expendable followers.
- **Jalal:** fictional, independently answerable to Sulu kin and workers; title/precise family relationship provisional. No romance until adult ages and non-kinship are established. A refusal must change the plot.
- **Laila:** if present, choose an independent political or material commitment that Ache cannot reverse. No automatic romantic reward.
- **Mulaw:** prior alliance was never guaranteed; do not silently imply he is already Ache's loyal Sulu subordinate.

## Evidence discipline and red lines

**Attested/report:** Pigafetta's 1521 travel account mentions Zolo/Taghima pearls, Mindanao cinnamon exchange and an explicitly secondhand Burné–Zolo pearl-seizure story; Majul discusses political functions of genealogies; modern research separates early Sulu polities from much later organized raiding systems. **Inference:** overlapping maritime trade, diplomacy and coercion make a disputed passage plausible. **Invented:** named crews, compensation rules, Jalal's role, specific harbor, events and resolution. No exact route, calendar date, fleet size, uniform ship type, institutional rank, or Sulu/Brunei administrative hierarchy without new primary evidence. Do not call Ache's fictional activities documented 'piracy.'
