# Book II, Chapter 2 — A place on the list (working scene architecture)

**Status:** Studio plan with six completed draft scenes; no prose publication, canon change, or chapter checkpoint movement. **Prepared:** 25 October 2026 PHT. **Working title:** *A Place on the List*. Existing reader-facing outline title *The Sultan's Court* remains unchanged until prose justifies a change.

## Handoff that must be earned

Book I Chapter 10 has **already** brought Ache before Siripada. Do not stage a second first audience. Book II Chapter 1 has already spent several mornings at a receiving house. Ache refused to invent testimony in a damaged-ceramics dispute, lost a merchant's *possible* Maynila contact, and earned only the right to direct claimants to the receiving-house supervisor. Idris's original carrier wage is still owed by that merchant, although Idris earned separate pay for later work. Sulad works independently along the bank. Ache's witnessed message to his mother has **not** been dispatched; Sulad's separately requested words have not yet been added before the witnesses. No ship or captain is promised.

**Chapter 2's distinct problem:** Ache must learn that even a request for a messenger or a place on a vessel can commit labor, cargo space, and political credit belonging to other people. This chapter moves from a private commercial dispute to competing obligations across Brunei's maritime households; it must not replay five scenes of a broken object or make every adult an instructor.

## Dramatic question, stakes, and irreversible change

**Question:** Can Ache ask for help with Maynila without speaking as if Bruneian boats and workers are his to offer? His immediate objective is a credible way to get news to his mother, not yet an army or command.

**Pressure:** A vessel preparing for a legitimate local obligation has limited room and hands. Ache sees an opportunity to attach his message. Safi is responsible for a competing commitment and suspects Ache will receive special treatment. Jamil is assessing who actually understands a vessel's readiness. A boatman's decision to sail cannot be overridden by an appealing speech.

**Cost:** Ache's premature assurance to a petitioner produces a real expectation he cannot fulfill. He must correct it publicly, losing status and possibly the best available route for the message. The worker/boat owner must retain a genuine right to refuse. **End:** Ache receives a narrow assignment to observe and report actual vessel readiness for Jamil, not a berth, crew, naval title, or promise of Maynila aid. This is the concrete handoff to Chapter 3's training.

## Six-scene sequence (provisional, revise before prose)

### 1. The request at the steps — a promise he did not make

Ache comes from the receiving house to follow up his still-unsent message. Someone tells him that a vessel from a western trading connection may soon call, but its route, master, and willingness to carry words remain unknown. A petitioner with an urgent local shipment mistakes Ache's kinship for authority to reserve space. Ache answers too generously instead of saying he has no right to decide. **Turn:** the petitioner repeats his words as a commitment; Ache cannot recall them privately. Do not make this the merchant's withdrawn contact from Chapter 1 by coincidence.

### 2. What a boat can carry — capacity is already spoken for

Jamil takes Ache to inspect an actual vessel before considering requests. A damaged spar or covering, a limited number of experienced hands, and already-accepted goods matter more than its appearance from shore. Safi knows which commitments have already been made because he asked the people doing the work. Ache assumes that an empty-looking space is available; a crew member demonstrates why it is not. **Turn:** Ache realizes his promise could displace another person's paid work. Show material facts through handling and dialogue, not a classroom lecture. Specific fittings and vocabulary require a separate technical source check.

### 3. Safi's fair win — no ceremonial contest

Before Jamil and a small group of people directly affected, Ache and Safi each propose a feasible loading/crew sequence. Safi's plan preserves the earlier commitment; Ache's quietly depends on an unasked-for extra pair of hands. The person whose labor Ache counted says no. Safi wins **on the merits**, without humiliating the worker or needing Siripada's favoritism. **Turn:** Ache can acknowledge the error or appeal to blood. He initially tries to explain himself and makes the embarrassment worse. Avoid turning Safi into a caricature or a friend by the end.

### 4. The correction — public cost, limited rescue

The petitioner from Scene 1 arrives expecting the place Ache seemed to offer. Safi prevents the expectation from turning into an actual displaced load, but he insists Ache correct his own words. Ache does so where they were repeated. The petitioner loses time and is entitled to be angry; no convenient alternate berth appears. Jamil sees that Safi protected the existing obligation. **Turn:** Ache's apology does not repair the petitioner's missed opportunity or buy immediate respect.

### 5. A separate choice — Laila and Hasan enter as people

Laila is occupied with a commitment of her own (for example, securing provisions owed to a household), and refuses to let Ache treat her access as a private route around the people who said no. Give her a specific reason and a consequence independent of his attraction. Hasan is consulted over how a promise was understood across speakers; he can clarify the uncertainty but cannot pronounce what everyone intended or cure the lost time. **Turn:** Ache must distinguish being heard from being entitled to an answer. **Age gate:** no romance or adult sexual chemistry until both characters' ages and power relations are established; keep Laila distinct from the unnamed historical Bruneian cousin-wife.

### 6. The smaller assignment — useful without a vessel

Jamil offers Ache a supervised task: document what a vessel and its crew actually need before another commitment is made. Ache accepts the unglamorous work without representing himself as a commander. Safi retains the more consequential responsibility and is not required to congratulate him. Siripada may confirm the arrangement but does not bestow an army. Ache returns to the unresolved message and asks what he may legitimately request next. Sulad is working elsewhere and does not become his assistant by default. **End image:** an unfinished count of available hands or repair materials, with a space deliberately left blank until someone answers.

## Character movement

- **Ache:** begins with limited responsibility earned at the receiving house; overextends a promise; suffers a public correction; ends with a narrower but real assignment and a concrete obligation to verify before speaking. His pride should persist.
- **Safi:** wants Bruneian obligations honored before a displaced relative's private request; wins fairly, and helps prevent damage partly to protect his own reputation and the crew. His dislike of Ache remains credible.
- **Jamil:** evaluates actual labor and seaworthiness rather than speeches. He may be unfair to a familiar veteran; do not portray him as infallible. His exact historical-sounding title remains fictional until separately sourced.
- **Laila:** has a resource or household obligation that would matter if Ache never appeared. She sets a boundary with a practical cost.
- **Hasan:** useful interpreter of uncertain language, but cannot replace witnesses or make an unknown route certain. Do not turn him into a sermon voice.
- **Sulad:** independent work, independent message, and the right to refuse Ache's plans remain live.

## Historical research gate — attested setting vs invention

**Attested regional context:** Roderich Ptak, “The Northern Trade Route to the Spice Islands: South China Sea–Sulu Zone–North Moluccas (14th to early 16th century),” *Archipel* 43 (1992), discusses Brunei merchants' northern network, Luzon connections, and its limits. https://www.persee.fr/doc/arch_0044-8613_1992_num_43_1_2804

**Material context:** Matussin bin Omar, *Archaeological Excavations in Protohistoric Brunei* (ANU, 1978), documents imported ceramics and trade-linked material distributions; it does **not** establish this invented hearing, load allocation, or individual vessels. https://openresearch-repository.anu.edu.au/items/dae0290e-5f8f-450c-b00e-198297fea895

**Broad comparative context only:** Dadi Darmadi, “Al-Islām wa al-Malāyū wa al-Siyādah fī al-Muḥīṭ,” *Studia Islamika* 22(1) (2015), discusses Brunei's early modern religious, political, and trading environment; its general account is not proof of any specific court procedure. https://studiaislamika.ppimcensis.or.id/index.php/studia-islamika/article/view/1390

**Invented until independently evidenced:** Safi, Jamil, Hasan, Laila, the petitioner, the loading decision, the promise, the exact court/household protocol, and the scene's dialogue. Do not import later Brunei administrative structures or titles uncritically into Ache's period. Do not use Brunei pitis coinage as a dated prop without a period-specific source. The exact Aganduru CODOIN 78 pp. 59–60 remains uninspected; Siripada/Bolkiah identity is unresolved. A later account of Brunei cannot establish what this specific group did.

## Six-scene manuscript QA gate (26 October 2026)

- **Physical and chronological continuity:** Scenes 1–4 follow the same two food baskets from waiting to explicit refusal; in Scene 5 their owners take them away and the tally boy records refusal; Scene 6 starts after that boat has left. Jamil's second torn covering is distinct from the repaired first covering. The single observed worker and two unfilled hand-spaces in Scene 6 are consistent. Do not reintroduce the baskets or count absent workers as committed.
- **Pacing risk before public assembly:** Four successive scenes turn on the same unaccepted cargo, and Hasan and Jamil both revisit the limits of Ache's words in Scenes 5–6. Review the entire reading copy for duplicated question-and-answer beats and repeated moral explanation, especially Scenes 3–6. Preserve the different consequences: Safi's fair loading decision, the petitioner's lost time, Laila's actual provisioning debt, and Ache's narrowly supervised task. A passing build is not a literary clearance.
- **Character and evidence locks:** The petitioner refuses a convenient rescue; the carrier receives payment for the last sack but this does not settle Idris's unrelated earlier wage; Safi retains accepted-load authority; Jamil can misjudge workers; Laila owes replacement food; Hasan cannot invent witnesses. Sulad has not consented to an appointment or dictated his words; no Maynila courier is confirmed. Keep fictional household procedures distinct from attested regional trade and material evidence.
- **Title recommendation, not publication:** *A Place on the List* now has a concrete double meaning in the separate tally marks and Ache's unfilled spaces. *The Sultan's Court* implies a new royal audience that the chapter deliberately avoids. Reconsider the reader-facing title and preserve the old title as an alias only when assembling the public chapter; do not alter published navigation or the forward checkpoint here.

## Material-culture evidence audit — 26 October 2026

**Scope and source access:** Checked the published bibliographic record for William Henry Scott, “Boat-Building and Seamanship in Classic Philippine Society,” *Philippine Studies* 30(3) (1982), https://archium.ateneo.edu/phstudies/vol30/iss3/3 ; the Australian National University repository abstract for Matussin bin Omar, *Archaeological Excavations in Protohistoric Brunei* (1978), https://openresearch-repository.anu.edu.au/items/dae0290e-5f8f-450c-b00e-198297fea895 ; and Roderich Ptak, “The Northern Trade Route to the Spice Islands,” *Archipel* 43 (1992), pp. 27–56, especially the searchable introduction on p. 27, https://www.persee.fr/doc/arch_0044-8613_1992_num_43_1_2804 . **Do not claim full-text or page-by-page inspection of Scott or Matussin.** The Ptak web transcription warns that its OCR is uncorrected.

- **Boat peg, swollen hole, propped hull (Scene 6):** Scott is a relevant *comparative Philippine* boatbuilding reference, not evidence that this particular Bruneian repair or fastening occurred. The prose wisely leaves the boat type unnamed. Keep the peg and threatened split as a plausible fictional material problem, but **withhold a source-backed historical note about this exact repair** until Scott's relevant original pages and Brunei/Borneo-specific construction evidence are checked. Do not silently convert the small boat into a documented balangay or caracoa.
- **Coverings, cords, poles, labor allocation (Scenes 1–4, 6):** Physical handling is narratively credible as reconstruction, but none of the inspected sources establishes the particular covering repair, crew count, wage agreement, loading order or two-person hull support. Treat each as invented logistics; no precise dated Bruneian technique or formal job title has been verified.
- **Tally boy's marked board and Ache's offcut (Scenes 1 and 6):** These are useful visual devices distinguishing an observed worker from unconfirmed hands. They are **fictional recordkeeping**, not evidence for a documented sixteenth-century Bruneian registry or receiving-house accounting system. Retain their ordinary physical description; do not add anachronistic bureaucratic labels or claim an attested administrative form.
- **Food bundles, household provisioning, imported goods (Scenes 1 and 5):** Matussin's repository abstract supports a broad archaeology of locally produced pottery and imported ceramics at Kupang and Sungai Lumut, with exchange inferred from assemblage patterns. It does **not** attest Laila's kitchen, portions, a cook's reimbursement rule, a named receiving house or these specific food deliveries. Those remain fiction. No new pottery claim is required in Chapter 2.
- **Maynila correspondence and westward trade (Scenes 1, 5, 6):** Ptak's regional-route reconstruction supports a connected maritime world, **not** an available courier, a named master, a specific westbound itinerary, or Siripada's control over any vessel. The message remains undelivered; the trader remains unconfirmed. Avoid treating network-level evidence as evidence of Ache's personal itinerary.

**Decision:** No demonstrated anachronism requiring a prose rewrite from this source pass; **historical/material-culture clearance remains partial**, not complete.
