## Historical basis and limits

- **Archaeological material:** Matussin bin Omar, *Archaeological excavations in protohistoric Brunei* (Australian National University, 1978), documents local earthenware and imported Song–Ming ceramics in Brunei and trade-related distribution patterns. This supports the presence of ceramic vessels in the wider historical setting, **not** the invented unloading scene, ownership dispute, or a specific receiving-house practice. https://openresearch-repository.anu.edu.au/items/dae0290e-5f8f-450c-b00e-198297fea895
- **Kota Batu trade context:** The SOAS lecture summary, *The Islamic Sultanate of Brunei: Past and Present Culture*, notes the shift of regional activity toward Kota Batu and archaeological ceramic finds. It cannot date a particular fictional household or prescribe its rules. https://www.soas.ac.uk/about/event/islamic-sultanate-brunei-past-and-present-culture
- **Near-contemporary cargo comparison:** Brunei shipwreck materials include Chinese, Thai and Vietnamese ceramics dated approximately late fifteenth to early sixteenth century; the reported assemblage makes imported ceramics plausible cargo, but cannot prove any particular vessel, merchant, or liability rule in Ache's story. https://www.xinhuanet.com/english/asiapacific/2018-11/16/c_137612111.htm
- **Unresolved primary-source boundary:** Aganduru's exact Spanish CODOIN 78 pp. 59–60 remains unverified. Do not attribute the receiving-house scene, royal family relationships, or trade practices to Aganduru. Pigafetta's 1521 Brunei account is later than this fictional scene and cannot directly validate it.

## Scene architecture and QA gates

**Scene 1, *The Unbroken Knot*:** After several mornings of work, Ache observes a merchant disputing the condition of a covered basket of imported bowls and threatening a younger carrier's payment. Ache saw only the last stage of unloading. The receiving-house woman insists on separating what each witness saw from what the merchant wants recorded. Daran's earlier encounter with Ache sharpens the temptation to defend a worker by claiming more than Ache knows. The merchant has contacts who might eventually carry news toward Maynila, but offers no passage or delivery.

**Later movement:** Sulad obtains relevant information through independent work without being turned into Ache's investigator. Competing accounts remain credible. Ache's eventual testimony must be bounded, cost him a patron's favor, and affect the worker's material claim. Any authority he gains is limited and earned. No royal decree, named historical office, guaranteed courier, instant favor from Siripada, or second counting-error lesson.
