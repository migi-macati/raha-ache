## Evidence located and what it can support

1. **Rodriguez et al., “Sea Nomads, Sultans, and Raiders: History and Ethnogenesis in the Sulu Archipelago, Philippines,” *Journal of Maritime Archaeology* 20 (2025), 973–1000.** Open-access peer-reviewed synthesis: https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11457-025-09481-3 . Its historical sections distinguish the emergence of Sulu's sultanate (roughly 1400–1500) from the Iranun-Balangingi age (1760–1848). It discusses Sama/Tausug differentiation, political hierarchy, seaborne livelihoods, and Sulu's links with Borneo. **Use:** broad periodization and hypotheses for further checking. **Do not use:** the later specialized slave-raiding economy, nineteenth-century ships, or ethnic hierarchies as unchanging early-sixteenth-century facts. The article is a retrospective interdisciplinary synthesis, not a contemporary eyewitness account.

2. **Cesar Adib Majul, “An Analysis of the 'Genealogy of Sulu',” *Archipel* 22 (1981), 167–182.** https://www.persee.fr/doc/arch_0044-8613_1981_num_22_1_1677 . Majul emphasizes that tarsilas transmit ancestry and help legitimize political claims; they were copied and maintained for political purposes. **Use:** treat dynastic genealogy as both a historical source and a political act. **Do not use:** every succession, personal name, event or date in a genealogy as contemporary independently confirmed fact. Original tarsila wording and chronology require separate examination.

3. **William Henry Scott, “Boat-Building and Seamanship in Classic Philippine Society,” *Philippine Studies* 30(3) (1982), 335–376.** Publisher record: https://archium.ateneo.edu/phstudies/vol30/iss3/3/ . The separate National Museum monograph (1981, 38 pp.) has distinct bibliographic status. **Use:** comparative Philippine maritime practices only after relevant pages are authenticated. **Do not use:** Visayan or later Spanish-era vessel terms as proof of Bruneian or Sulu-specific early-1500s practice.

4. **James Francis Warren, *Trade, Raid, Slave: The Socio-Economic Patterns of the Sulu Zone, 1770–1898* (1975 dissertation).** https://openresearch-repository.anu.edu.au/items/6f3690ed-581e-4cc1-8ad5-14048541ca32 . **Use:** understand a much later historical system and guard against false analogies. **Do not use:** eighteenth–nineteenth-century commodity demands, captive markets or raiding institutions as direct evidence for Ache's era. Likewise, Warren's article on Sino-Sulu trade concerns the late eighteenth and nineteenth centuries, despite mentioning the 1417 mission.

5. **Darwin J. Absari, “Sulu-China History from Pre-Islamic to Spanish Era,” *Chinese Studies Journal* 17 (2023), 227–242.** https://www.chinesestudiesjournal.org/volumes/volume-17-2023/sulu-china-history-from-pre-islamic-to-spanish-era . **Use:** potential guide to early Chinese textual evidence and the 1417 embassy. **Status:** bibliographic/abstract lead only; the underlying article and Ming passages have not been inspected here. No exact quotation or chronological claim from the Chinese original is authorized yet.

## Immediate historical questions

- Which Brunei–Sulu–Mindanao routes and political relationships are supportable for the novel's actual year, and which are inferred from later genealogies or Spanish reports?
- What authority could an individual merchant, datu, boat owner or ship's crew plausibly exercise? Do not assume a unified Bruneian naval chain of command extends to Sulu.
- What would Ache have to offer to secure passage after losing Jamil's trust? He cannot receive a fleet merely because the outline requires a comeback.
- Which forms of maritime coercion, ransom, tribute, debt or protection are documented for this period, and who describes them? The category “pirate” must be treated as contested and time-bound, not as a self-evident occupational identity.
- How will the unresolved search for Nasir and obligations to Hamzah's merchants continue to matter? Do not turn Chapter 4's losses into disposable motivation.
- If Jalal is introduced, what independent social position, crew relationships and political obligations constrain him? Any attraction or romantic tension requires both characters to be adults and able to refuse.

## Provisional narrative bridge, not yet approved plot

Ache could be permitted to accompany a merchant or diplomatic party **as a recorder or working passenger**, without authority to change passage terms. The voyage would expose competing claims to safe passage, cargo and protection. A local leader might ask for help Ache cannot give on Jamil's behalf. A clever intervention could secure a limited practical outcome while creating a new debt or antagonism. Any later leadership must arise from negotiated trust, not inherited Bruneian privilege. This is a fictional possibility requiring further source and continuity review, not a documented historical episode.

## Title and literary risk

*Pirate Life* is a useful outline shorthand but risks predetermining who is legitimate and importing a later colonial category. Review after the chapter's actual conflict is clear. Possible exploratory directions: *Whose Water*, *The Terms of Passage*, or an indirect title rooted in a physical object or decision. None is approved for publication.
