## Source and title gates

The existing Chapter 9 outline title *After the Iron Ships* is misleading if read literally: the 1521 expedition vessels had wooden hulls, despite their metal weapons and fittings. Its alternate outline label *Triumphant Return and Wedding* pre-decides a victory and romantic reward that the Chapter 8 ending does not support. Review reader-facing title after drafting. Provisional *The List at Low Water* refers to the harbor's changing tide and the contested records of people, goods and obligations; do not force the metaphor into dialogue.

Pigafetta's 29 July 1521 narrative says the Luzon prince/captain was released after João Carvalho accepted gold without the crew's consent. Francisco Albo also reports the release but gives a different vessel tally; the anonymous Genoese-pilot transcription differs in the sequence and hostage exchanges. None establishes Ache's exact post-release itinerary, the fate of each local captive, a documented recovery meeting, a named post-release wedding or a particular crew reorganization. Source comparisons are in draft research PR #58. The novel's Chapter 8 exchange, women held aboard, incomplete payment accounting and local harbor testimony are fictional reconciliation, not an independently verified local record.

**Chronology and politics:** Do not assert that the European fleet was immediately defeated or that Ache regained a historical command the next day. Do not import later Spanish colonial military offices, ships or court language. Ache's family connection to Luzon and Brunei service does not authorize him to dispose of independent boat owners, captives or goods. If the plot reaches Maynila, establish the voyage and obligations on page; Chapter 10 owns the Book II main-conflict choice and resolution.

## Opening evidence and obligations

- Ache has been released and has given a partial, explicitly bounded account. His working knife was returned; his arm was twisted and hurts. The ruler has withheld authority over agreed routes pending separate inquiries. Jamil is neither exonerating nor abandoning him.
- Safi and the watch boat owner rescued an injured man and a dead man, withdrew under the owner's condition, and returned alive. The owner must inspect his vessel before sailing again. Safi has his own judgment and loyalties.
- Ruma's repaired boat stayed at the inner landing. She can receive wounded if water and cloth are supplied, but she will not put them on her damaged hull. Tahir's boat grounded after Ache's proposed maneuver; his nephew's hand was injured. Tahir and Hasan's clerk are maintaining independent records.
- The Laoe-linked junk's master, his crew, a splinted injured sailor and a boy were being moved when Ache departed the foreign ship; their arrival ashore is **not yet verified**. An older woman sought her son who swam away, identified by a scar on his left shoulder; no rescue confirmed.
- A woman in blue and a younger woman with an injured ear were still captive when Ache left; the older woman may or may not have reached shore. The woman in blue gave names and a river location for people who might look for them. Do not silently release, kill or forget these people.
- Ache saw gold and boats exchange signals but does not know the payer, amount or actual terms. A merchant's claim of involuntary loss competes with a claim of voluntary contribution. This is an active political dispute, not a solved ransom account.
- Nasir remains missing after Book II Chapter 4; his sister has independently financed inquiries. Dima's mother and Laoe's disputed goods and casualties remain unresolved. Ache ends Chapter 8 going to Nasir's sister, not to a celebration.

## Six-scene causal plan

1. **The List at Low Water.** Ache meets Nasir's sister before any court celebration. She rejects another vague assurance and asks him to identify which witnesses he can actually reach. The harbor's separate boat, passenger, injury and missing-person lists disagree. End: a returning boat brings new information that changes one category, but not all.
2. **The Hull That Returned.** The Laoe-linked junk's fate is established by direct testimony, not rumor. Its master chooses whether to trust Ache after the brother lost at Laoe; the boy and injured sailor have independent needs. The older woman's son remains a specific inquiry. End: a repair or burial obligation consumes the resources Ache had imagined using to sail.
3. **The Gold Without a Giver.** A merchant, court representative and boat owners dispute payment and the release's meaning. Ache gives only what he witnessed. The foreign ship's missing men and local captives are not traded as abstract counters. End: Ache's name has become a liability to people who did not consent to pay for him.
4. **Safi's Conditions.** Safi and Ruma offer practical assistance on terms that exclude another impulsive expedition. Jamil requires evidence of voluntary crew commitments, a credible cargo ledger and a procedure for missing people. Ache must decide which responsibilities to relinquish. End: the first prospective crew declines him, forcing a smaller, more honest plan.
5. **A Person Who Can Leave.** A private relationship, whether friendship, kinship or adult attraction already earned by prior chapters, is tested by the cost of Ache's absence. The other person has their own work, loyalties and freedom to refuse him. No automatic wedding or romantic absolution. End: a credible offer of departure is made without guaranteeing that those left behind are safe.
6. **The Tide After.** Ache presents a limited operational plan to owners and court: who can sail, with what cargo, on whose terms, and who remains to pursue unresolved captives and claims. Jamil and the ruler may authorize a narrow role; their approval does not restore every lost trust. End: Chapter 10 must resolve the Book II main conflict through Ache's free decision about Luzon versus Brunei, rather than a foregone triumph.
