## Source comparison: do not collapse these into one event transcript

**Antonio Pigafetta, entry dated 29 July 1521:** In the English translation at the Philippine Diary Project, Pigafetta says more than one hundred praus approached in three groups, the Europeans feared being trapped between them and junks at anchor, fired on the junks, captured four and killed many. A captured junk carried a **son of the ruler of Luzon** who was Brunei's captain-general and had returned from the reported sack of Laoe. The source does **not name him Ache**. Pigafetta says João Carvalho released that captain and the junks for gold without the others' consent. Pigafetta also reports the Europeans kept sixteen important men and three women, while Carvalho took the women for himself. Source: https://philippinediaryproject.com/1521/07/29/29th-of-july-1521/ . These are claims in a European witness's narrative, not an independent local record of motives or negotiated terms.

**Francisco Albo, bracketed [21 June–29 July 1521] log entry:** Albo reports shoals, the need for a local pilot, trade and peace, then **260** armed canoes and capture of **one junk** carrying a son of the ruler of Luzon; the captain released him without others' counsel. The date range is an editor's bracket, not an exact day asserted in the log. Albo's brief passage gives no verified gold amount, named local captives or full negotiation transcript. Source: https://philippinediaryproject.com/1521/07/29/21-june-29-july-1521/ ; provenance note: https://philippinediaryproject.com/about-the-philippine-diary-project/about-the-diaries/about-francisco-albo/ .

**Anonymous Genoese-pilot account, online English transcription, original edition uncollated:** The transcription says five junks appeared at anchor, then two hundred praus approached. The foreigners seized **one junk with twenty-seven men**, which reportedly sank in a squall that night. **On the next morning**, they captured a **different, larger junk** carrying a son of the ruler of Lucam/Luzon and **ninety men**. The account describes negotiation over seven Europeans on shore: two returned, five did not, thirty captives were sent back, and fourteen men plus three women were carried away. It names Espinosa as captain of Victoria during the Brunei reception. This sequence and prisoner accounting conflict materially with Pigafetta and Albo. The accessible English transcription claims derivation from a Genoese pilot's paper-book and a 1524 Portuguese transmission, but manuscript/edition/translator lineage remains **unverified**. Source: https://www.historycentral.com/documents/Magellan.html (Brunei passage). Do not treat this transcription as equal textual certainty to a checked critical edition.

## Carry-forward continuity and character agency

- Ache has conditional responsibility for agreed cargo and message routes toward Luzon; he is **not** owner of the harbor fleet. His pride after Laoe has been complicated by disputed goods, wounded civilians, missing Nasir, Dima's missing mother and the cost of his own decisions.
- Ruma's seam was repaired but she kept her vessel at the inner landing. Tahir's boat grounded after a maneuver Ache proposed; his nephew's hand was injured. Tahir's wrapped message was returned to Hasan's clerk. Banu shifted goods by land and did not join the dangerous passage.
- Safi's watch boat rescued one injured man and one dead man from the water and then withdrew under its owner's stated condition. Ache climbed onto the endangered junk to help lower another injured man. Safi's boat was last seen moving toward shore; do not presume a death or abandonment.
- On the junk, a boy and injured man remain behind a low cover, an older woman awaits news of her son who jumped into the water, and the master has a brother missing after Laoe. These are fictional characters with independent choices. Ache cannot command them or claim to have saved them.
- Chapter 7 ends with ropes/hooks at the rail and feet above; **no actual boarding/capture, captor name, bargain, or release has yet occurred**. Chapter 8 must not jump over the injured man's fate or the separated crew to reach the famous historical encounter.
- No automatic restoration of Ache's command after release. Jamil, Safi, Ruma, Tahir and the ruler will judge losses independently. His personal survival must not resolve Nasir's absence or Laoe's outstanding claims.

## Six-scene causal architecture (provisional)

1. **The First Hand.** The boarding completes. Ache is disarmed and separated from the junk's master. He chooses to keep the injured man visible rather than assert princely status. A captor misunderstands the gesture. End: Ache is moved away from those he came to help.
2. **A Name for a Price.** Captors and intermediaries seek his identity through imperfect languages. A person from the junk recognizes his Laoe role, which raises his bargaining value and creates resentment among other prisoners. End: his name becomes useful to men who do not know him.
3. **The People Left Aboard.** Ache sees that women, sailors, wounded men and high-status prisoners face different fates. He cannot count them reliably or compel their release. End: a proposed exchange makes the missing Europeans on shore part of the negotiation.
4. **What Gold Could Carry.** Negotiation occurs through carriers and signals, without a fabricated exact gold sum or direct quotation. The captain/pilot's motives remain ambiguous. Ache must decide whether to seek his own release when others may remain captive. End: his freedom becomes possible at a cost he cannot distribute fairly.
5. **The Boat Sent Back.** Ache leaves the foreign vessel under the chosen Pigafetta-compatible fictional sequence. Other prisoners' fates remain uncertain; some may be retained. No sweeping mercy narrative. End: he returns to a harbor where the attack is still unfolding in testimony and rumor.
6. **The Name That Returned.** Jamil, Safi, Tahir and the ruler demand separate accounts. Ache can report only what he saw; he cannot guarantee the injured man's fate or the disposition of captives. A limited, painful settlement closes the captivity arc. End: his survival creates a new political problem for Chapter 9, rather than instantly restoring command.
