## Narrative problem

Ache expects Jamil to reward the care he took with the repair tally. Jamil instead gives him a small, real movement of two working boats through a tide-affected channel. Ache can see the shorter water but has not learned whose labor, skill, or risk makes a route possible. Safi's task is legitimate and better supported; he has an independent interest in preventing royal favoritism. This is supervised seamanship and command apprenticeship **invented for the novel**, not an attested Bruneian naval academy, fleet drill, rank examination, or standard signal code.

The decisive gain is not a spectacular maneuver: Ache identifies a danger, allows an experienced hand to overrule his first instruction, and successfully coordinates a change of course without disguising the cost. Jamil gives him another limited duty, not command of a squadron. The chapter ends with a small, seductive overconfidence that makes Chapter 4's failure possible.

## Six-scene causal chain

1. **The boat already moving.** Ache arrives early at the landing with the marked offcut. The unrepaired boat remains waiting for its owner. Jamil takes Ache to two craft preparing to carry supplies and people beyond a constricted reach. Safi has confirmed his crew's agreement. Ache learns that an empty boat's draft and a loaded one's are different, and that being permitted to observe is not authority to order a crew. Opening action should have movement, tension and distinct people, not another receiving-house argument.
2. **The boat under load.** Now aboard Ruma's jar boat as an observer, Ache discovers how a loaded hull responds to the tidal reach. The poleman and owner have their own route, time and return obligations; Safi's timber boat has different handling limits. Ache learns to watch the spacing between the vessels before imagining he can direct either one. His access comes from Jamil, but no one has agreed to accept his orders. Avoid another receiving-house bargain scene: Scene 1 has already established the crew's terms and consent.
3. **The return course.** Beyond the bend the crews deliver their loads. Jamil, meeting the boats there, offers Ache a tightly supervised task on the return: agree a common passage and spacing with both steersmen before the tide changes. Safi objects to a plan that would make his boat wait in unsafe water, and he has evidence from the outward passage. Ache must use what he saw of the loaded hulls rather than repeat his tempting inside shortcut from Scene 2. This begins actual coordination, not another landing tally.
4. **Words between hulls.** Boats separate. A shout is heard ambiguously; Hasan's interpretation of a term or instruction is useful but imperfect, and he must admit uncertainty. Crew negotiate a clear, locally intelligible signal (voice, raised pole, or visible gesture) rather than a standardized modern naval semaphore. Ache must choose whether to admit he did not understand the first instruction.
5. **The cost of a turn.** A load shifts or a line snags at a piling. Ache gives up speed to prevent injury and protects another boat's passage, with real consequences for the boat owner, Safi, and the workers. Do not stage an unearned heroic rescue. The people bearing the cost decide what payment or repair they will accept.
6. **After the water falls.** Jamil distinguishes a correct decision from a lucky one and grants Ache one narrowly bounded supervised assignment. Safi has demonstrably done something well and does not forgive Ache simply for learning. A conversation or absence recalls Sulad's independent work and the unsent Maynila message. Ache starts mistaking one sound judgment for readiness to command more: Chapter 4 will test that error.

## Continuity and character gates

- **Ache:** approx. 16–17 in Book I; do not assert a precise Book II age without chronology. His ambition and vulnerability appear in choices, not a lecture about humility.
- **Jamil:** fictional mentor with genuine skill and the capacity to be wrong. His honorific is a development choice, not evidence of a documented sixteenth-century office-holder named Jamil.
- **Safi:** has his own assignment and crew, a real victory on merit, and a defensible objection to Ache's family advantage.
- **Hasan:** fictional teacher/interpreter from Fansur/Barus; do not assume he knows every local boatman's speech. Language difference should affect a decision, not provide a tidy aphorism.
- **Sulad:** working separately along the bank. Ache cannot promise his participation or add his words to the Maynila message without him.
- **Laila:** has independent provisioning/political obligations; do not introduce her solely to admire Ache.
- **The prior damaged boat:** owner has not produced the replacement peg or accepted pay for extra hands at Chapter 2 close. Any resolution requires a scene, not an aside.
- **No fleet yet:** Pigafetta's 1521 captain-general description and Aganduru's later fleet number refer to a later phase, not proof that Ache already commands ships in Chapter 3.
- **Chapter 4 owns a substantive defeat.** Chapter 3 may involve a frightening near miss and cost, but should end with bounded success, not duplicate the later major failure.
