# Book II Chapter 10 — the return decision: source gates, missing-cast audit and six-scene architecture

**Packet:** RA-B2C10-RESEARCH-01 / RA-B2C10-PLAN-01. **State:** Studio research and provisional architecture, not reader-facing prose. **Main checkpoint at creation:** Book II Chapter 9 *The List at Low Water* merged PR #63, SHA `9fd11524aa5581f55945ad9ba1375094db5f46e2`, deployed in GitHub Pages run `40486270166`; checkpoint commit `55926e64b369eb8ed4de8f31e75b1102b31b1449`. **Architecture:** 30 core chapters across three books plus Book III bonus Chapters 11–12. **POV:** Ache first person.

## Non-negotiable narrative boundary

Book II's central conflict must resolve by Chapter 10. Ache must make and act on a free choice between the life he has built in Brunei and the attempt to return to Luzon. The ending should commit a real, owner-approved expedition toward Luzon; Book III Chapter 1 *The Bay That Forgot Him* retains the actual homecoming and the shock that Maynila did not stand still. **Do not retake Maynila in Book II.** The return is a costly political and logistical decision, not a reward for surviving captivity.

Chapter 9 ends with Ache authorized only to carry questions between families and Hasan's clerk. Jamil has not restored command. A possible passage owner is expected after the next market, but no passage, boat, crew, cargo or Brunei military backing has been pledged. Nasir remains missing; a paid inquiry has departed toward the traps without Ache aboard. The woman in blue, the younger woman with the injured ear, the junk's missing sailor and the older woman's son remain unresolved. The merchant's gold claim and the status of foreign visitors ashore are disputed. Ruma may trade upriver; Safi and the watch-boat owner have not offered their vessel. These cannot silently resolve between chapters.

**Command progression required on page:** practical work with the family questions and harbor records; independent owner agreements; sufficient provisions and a plausible route; Jamil's evidence-based assessment; the ruler's politically limited authorization; only then an actual sailing. Avoid a grand fleet materializing after one audience. A small force with explicit voluntary commitments may be more credible than an unspecified armada, provided it satisfies the Book II resolution and Book III handoff.

## Historical evidence and uncertainty

Pigafetta's July 1521 Brunei encounter and Francisco Albo's navigational account differ in local flotilla and captured-junk counts. The Genoese-pilot account offers another contested sequence. The novel has already chosen a fictional reconciliation in Chapters 7–9. The texts do not document Ache's exact post-captivity timetable, a personally commanded northbound fleet, named officers aboard it, ransom quantity, a wedding on his release, or his private departure dialogue. Do not assign any of these to a historical witness. The Spanish Aganduru Moriz passage in CODOIN 78 pp. 59–60 remains **uninspected**. Secondary identification of Ladya Siripada with Sultan Bolkiah remains possible rather than established; do not collapse those identities. A Brunei-backed return is a narrative premise requiring explicit fiction/source boundaries, not a dated itinerary to invent.

The outline's `sources: [aganduru-moriz, pigafetta]` does not itself authenticate the source claims. Any published notes should follow the separate collapsed historical-notes contract and remain outside default Read Aloud.

## Critical cast-presence audit: new editorial risk

Direct inspection of **published Book II Chapters 1–9** on 27 October 2026 found:

- **Sulad:** appears in Chapters 1–3 (12, 6 and 3 textual mentions respectively), but **zero mentions in Chapters 4–9**. Chapter 3's ending explicitly places him farther downriver with working men and says his own witness account has not yet been heard. The gap may be compatible with independent work but has not been narrated. Chapter 10's outline assumes he is available as Ache's oldest companion. Do not summon him from nowhere as a loyal follower.
- **Laila:** appears in Chapter 2 (14 mentions) in a concrete food-provisioning dispute, with her own store, credit obligations and decisions; **zero mentions in Chapters 3–9**. The Chapter 10 outline relies on her as an emotionally significant Bruneian attachment. Their relationship has not earned an automatic wedding or an established reciprocal adult romance. Her independent household obligations provide a credible route back into the plot, but a retrospective narrative bridge is required before treating her as a decisive romantic stake.
- **Safi, Ruma, Tahir and Jamil:** remain active through Chapters 7–9 and have developed independent obligations. They must not become a chorus that unanimously approves departure.
- **Nasir's sister and the older woman:** have no duty to forgive Ache or resolve their searches to free him. Their paid inquiries and witness questions must have credible handoff arrangements if he sails.

**Retrospective repair proposal, separate scoped PR(s), no silent retcon:** Inspect Chapters 3–9 and find the smallest causal places to establish Sulad's continuing downriver work and why his testimony/return was delayed; locate Laila's food-accounting obligations during the harbor crisis, her choice to stay at work and her knowledge of Ache's capture/release. Prefer one or two short character-driven scenes or mentions with an actual decision, rather than retrospective exposition inserted only into Chapter 10. Review exact existing prose and preserve chronology before editing. Do not rewrite the entire published chapter or alter its central outcomes. The gap is an editorial risk, not evidence that either character died, abandoned Ache or changed allegiance.

## Six-scene causal architecture (provisional)

1. **The Man Who Went Downriver.** A real result from the paid inquiry or a separate message arrives. Ache is working under Hasan's bounded terms when Sulad's independent whereabouts and unsubmitted testimony are established. Sulad's time away must have work, cost and a choice of his own. He questions whether returning to Luzon means protecting people or claiming them. A missing-person inquiry is handed to a named person, not abandoned.
2. **The Woman Who Kept the Stores.** Laila's provisioning accounts and household commitments surface in the disputed gold/food economy. She may disagree with Ache's use of her help and refuses to be counted as available cargo for his voyage. If affection develops, establish both parties' adulthood and reciprocal choice through lived interaction, not a court declaration. A plausible Brunei future becomes attractive on its own terms.
3. **The Ruler's Two Offers.** Siripada and Jamil offer a meaningful path to remain in Brunei, with work, political protection and duties that are not humiliation. A northbound venture is also possible only with owners' voluntary commitments and explicit limits. Siripada has political interests beyond his grandson; a grant must cost him something and need not mean an unlimited fleet.
4. **The Owners of the Sails.** Show work at boats and warehouses, not another extended audience: hulls, crews, shares, rice, water, navigation, season and return obligations. Some owners decline. One or more make bounded offers for their own reasons. Ache earns authority over **agreed work** through credible accounting and decisions, with a defined procedure if a crew withdraws. The merchant's disputed gold cannot fund the venture by fiat.
5. **What Cannot Be Carried.** Ache makes the personal choice and speaks separately with Sulad, Laila, Safi, Ruma and the families whose inquiries remain open as relevant. At least one relationship changes irreversibly. No wedding as reward, no assumed consent from any adult partner, no compelled loyalty from Sulad. The ruler's offer to stay remains genuine. A concrete cost is paid, not merely announced.
6. **The Water North.** The authorized, actually provisioned vessels depart under named owners and a clear destination. The final order commits the Book II choice. A final image can hold the memory of the people still missing, while Book III retains Maynila's politics, legitimacy and restoration. End with motion and uncertainty rather than a triumphal speech.

## Quality gates

The six scenes must have different dramatic actions, physical locations and pressures; avoid repeated hearings and repeated formulations of "I cannot command." Give Ache a demonstrable competence that changes someone else's practical decision, not just a new vocabulary of humility. Preserve flawed behavior: he can want prestige and make a wrong assumption even after learning caution. Let the ruler, Jamil, Laila, Sulad, Safi, Ruma and owners disagree for credible material reasons. If an adult attraction matters, let it complicate the choice rather than certify a moral transformation. Review the chapter title after drafting; current outline title *Luzon on the Horizon* remains provisional, while *Admirable Admiral* risks triumphalism.
