## Pacing diagnosis and decision

**Editorial judgment:** 15,467 raw words before the principal survival crisis and approach to Brunei is disproportionate for a chapter whose primary payoff is that crisis. The recurring pattern of negotiating one leg, explaining that no further passage is guaranteed, and demonstrating Sulad's separate consent has become repetitive even though each transaction is internally credible. Do **not** draft more routine bargaining scenes merely to accumulate realism. Preserve causality but compress repeated explanations during reader-facing integration.

Audit for consolidation, without discarding already written source files: Scenes 1A–2 (mat delivery), 3A–4 (sick sailor and jar-boat preparation), 5–7 (jar-boat contract, departure, delivery), and 8–9 (Daya's parcels, larger carrier and departure). Favor specific irreversible decisions, material consequences, and Ache's changing judgment over additional demonstrations of the same rule. This is an editorial recommendation, **not** an authorization to silently erase character or compensation continuity. Reassess the reader-facing title *The Last Storm* after the crisis exists; avoid promising a spectacle before the manuscript earns it.
