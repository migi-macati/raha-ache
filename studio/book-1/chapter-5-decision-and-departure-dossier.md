# Book I, Chapter 5 — decision, costs, and departure dossier

**Research/planning pass:** 12 October 2026 (Asia/Manila). **Status:** Studio-only; no public prose or chapter checkpoint advancement. **Current functional title:** *Fight or Flight*. **Reader-facing title:** undecided.

## Canonical handoff and narrative question

Chapter 3 ends with Ache seeing the Tondo clerk's marks beside crews and learning that a record useful for finding a missing sailor could also help people claiming labor. Chapter 4 (*The Second Crossing*, merged as PR #30) stages Sima's contested second voyage: she paid the organizer for the first transfer; the organizer's sister's son says the crew itself remains unpaid. The regent limits the meaning of the tally boards, permits Tondo crews to work at Maynila, and sends **her steward**, rather than Ache, to ask what it would take to retain reliable crews on Maynila's shore. A merchant independently plans to bring baskets to Tondo's landing the following morning, without any crew yet booked. Ache cannot simply replace Tondo labor: Sulad and his uncle are repairing fishing stakes and nets. Sima negotiates her own two-basket shipment.

**Chapter 5's dramatic question:** What can Ache actually ask other people to risk to defend Maynila, and what will he sacrifice when they refuse? The strategic departure for Brunei must be a choice made here; the physical voyage belongs to Chapter 6. Do not treat the regent's compromise as cowardice, or Ache's correct diagnosis of commercial dependence as sufficient justification for conscription.

## Evidence ledger: what can be said, and what remains invented

1. **Near-contemporary regional commercial context.** Tomé Pires, *Suma Oriental* (written 1512–1515; Cortesão ed./trans., 1944, vol. I, pp. 133–134), describes the Luções in trade involving Borneo and Malacca, and commodities including foodstuffs, wax, honey and gold. Pires gathered commercial information in Malacca; his statements do not identify Ache, his household, a specific Manila landing or the date of this invented chapter. His broad characterization of Luzon government is not a warrant to erase the novel's separately sourced royal kinship tradition. Repo record: `src/content/sources/tome-pires-suma-oriental.md`. 2. **Scholarly route reconstruction.** Roderich Ptak, “The Northern Trade Route to the Spice Islands,” *Archipel* 43 (1992), pp. 27–56, DOI 10.3406/arch.1992.2804, describes a Borneo-coast corridor that might call at Brunei and a Luzon-to-Mindoro/Calamianes corridor entering the Sulu Sea. These are interconnected **trade-route possibilities**, not a documented direct Manila–Brunei sailing or Ache's personal itinerary. The accessible introduction was rechecked 12 October 2026: https://www.persee.fr/doc/arch_0044-8613_1992_num_43_1_2804 .

He was not an eyewitness to Ache's adolescence. The original Spanish CODOIN 78 pp. 59–60 and its informant chain remain unverified. 5. **Novel canon, explicitly invented:** Sima, Sulad, Bulkan, the organizer, unpaid rower, tally boards, merchant's next-day decision, steward's negotiation, fishing-stake repair, household recruitment, any threat or bargaining demand, and private mother/son dialogue. Do not style fictional bargaining as a recorded customary-law rule or as a documented early-sixteenth-century tax.

## Scene architecture: six causally distinct turns

**1. The cost appears before the argument (morning; public landing).** Ache sees the merchant's baskets being taken *toward* Tondo's landing, or prepared for that trip; the organizer has **not** promised a crew. The regent's steward goes to hear terms for supplying rowers to Maynila. This is a measurable loss of initiative, not proof that Maynila's trade has collapsed. A younger rower asks the organizer for his share of yesterday's wages; he receives an evasive answer, not a tidy settlement. Ache cannot fix the labor grievance by claiming authority over either man. **Scene objective:** discover what an actual defensive alternative would require. **Reversal:** Ache realizes his mother's clerk can tally vessels but cannot create willing rowers.

**2. A plan with a price (Bulkan or another experienced boat captain).** Ache proposes a small show of force or a voluntary Maynila-run crew service to keep merchants from shifting landings. Bulkan asks concrete questions: which boat is seaworthy, who repairs damaged gear, who feeds the men while they wait, whose catch or cargo they forgo, who authorizes force if a Tondo crew resists. Bulkan is neither a caricatured hawk nor an omniscient mentor: he may prefer a visible armed patrol despite the risk because he knows how quickly a reputation weakens. **Decision test:** Ache must choose between a coercive gesture, a costly voluntary alternative, and accepting the present terms. **No tactical instruction or anachronistic standing navy**; this is character conflict.

**3. A household refuses (fishing bank, not court).** Ache asks a household connected to Sulad's uncle whether they can lend a working boat and hands for several days. The damaged nets and stakes from Chapters 1 and 4 make the cost visible; their family needs the next catch. The household's refusal is not a sermon. It may offer one limited service in exchange for food or compensation Ache cannot yet guarantee. Sulad refuses to let Ache promise his uncle's labor in his name. **Irreversible consequence:** the proposed immediate resistance cannot credibly happen without imposing the cost on people Ache claims to protect. Avoid repeating Chapter 4's “you spoke for me” dialogue beat verbatim.

**4. A private confrontation with the regent (household stores or tally room).** The mother learns Ache sought rowers, perhaps because a household petitions her for clarity, not because of a conveniently omnipresent spy. She has asked the steward to negotiate for Maynila crews and may have considered provisions for volunteers; this does not make her plan guaranteed to work. Ache points to real long-term danger in letting Tondo become the easier hiring shore. She does not deny it. He wants permission to take a risk she cannot require other households to bear. She refuses to lend the household's authority to a premature armed stand. **Emotional injury:** she has trusted the steward with the very negotiation Ache believed should be his; she fears losing her son to pride and is also frightened of losing the landing. They do not fully reconcile.

**Scene 4 causal handoff and conflict test (editorial refinement).** The fishing household's conditional offer creates a concrete question: who may pledge provisions from the regent's stores? Ache goes to ask for a limited allotment rather than arriving solely to denounce Tondo. The regent already knows the steward is negotiating terms, but has no reason to know every private conversation Ache has had; she learns of Sulad's offer directly from Ache. She can consider feeding a single paid messenger while refusing to provision an armed watch, which makes her judgment consequential rather than uniformly obstructive. Ache must admit that he offered stores before asking. Let her ask which household would lose its share if she released food. Let him answer with a name or acknowledge he does not know. Neither wins the argument: her steward's negotiation might still make Tondo indispensable, while Ache's proposed alternative lacks consent and funding. End with a tangible, limited refusal or condition, not a generalized speech about leadership. The steward's report, if it returns in this chapter, must contain negotiated terms rather than a conveniently settled agreement.

**5. Brunei becomes a possible route, not an instant rescue.** Reconnect, through a credible rendezvous or messenger, with the **unnamed Malay-speaking captain encountered upriver in Chapter 3**; his vessel's present location and availability are not established. He has traveled through Brunei, seen its court, and dealt with people connected to its household, but claimed no personal intimacy with Siripada. He corrected Ache's limited Malay and warned that a grandson is not a fleet. He can describe an upcoming southbound commercial movement only if his actual knowledge and route are established in-scene, or point Ache toward a *possible* intermediary linked to Brunei. Do not conjure a direct royal ship or guarantee safe passage. The captain demands tangible terms: work aboard, provisions, a recognized sponsor, or the uncertainty of joining at another port. Ache understands that being the regent's son does not compel a foreign crew to take him. The decision to seek his maternal kin can draw on Aganduru's later narrative while the exact messenger, timing, and logistics remain fiction. **Suspense:** the same networks that can carry Ache's appeal can report his intentions to Tondo or his mother.

**6. Sulad's condition and Ache's choice (night, repaired boat or household courtyard).** Sulad is not automatically recruited. His condition should cost Ache something measurable: he must secure a replacement worker or food for Sulad's family, promise not to order Sulad into another household's quarrel, or accept that Sulad may refuse if his obligations cannot be met. Avoid a sweeping modern declaration of equality. Ache privately resolves to seek support in Brunei rather than launch an immediate attack. His mother may discover or permit the journey in Chapter 6; this chapter must not pre-decide the historical uncertainty. **Ending image candidate:** the paddle Ache had expected to take remains beside the unfinished net while a different boat is made ready. The physical departure remains for Chapter 6.

## Arc ledger and continuity

- **Ache:** begins knowing how to identify administrative capture; ends accepting that he cannot force others to defend his preferred solution. He chooses an uncertain appeal abroad, still partly motivated by pride. The choice should be costly, not presented as inevitable wisdom.
- **Regent:** her compromise has real political risk. She tries to keep trade moving and may be wrong about the bargaining leverage she is conceding. Her exclusion of Ache from negotiations is both practical and personally damaging.
- **Sulad:** preserves household duty, independent belief and the protective cord from Chapter 3. Any tenderness or adolescent attraction is Ache's uncertain internal perception, not evidence of reciprocation. No sexualized scene. He is allowed to refuse travel.
- **Bulkan:** practical experience and a potential preference for visible force can clash with both Ache and the regent. His participation in the actual Chapter 6 voyage is undecided; do not silently convert him into a guaranteed companion.
- **Sima and organizer's nephew:** continue living beyond Ache's attention. The first voyage's unpaid-wage claim remains open unless independently earned. Do not reuse Sima as another moral instructor or invent that she has become Ache's ally.
- **Tondo ruler:** remains unidentified in this adolescent-era plot; do not reinsert Lakandula as a named youthful cousin. The Tondo clerk is conscientious but his board could be exploited.
- **Chapter 6 handoff:** the journey begins only after a vessel, crew authority, food/water, season, route candidate, and terms are plausible. A later offer of gold/permission from the regent, if chosen, must be dramatized and marked as a reconstruction from Aganduru, not a proven event.

## Reader suspense and scene-quality tests

The reader may expect Ache to reach Brunei eventually; uncertainty should center on **who bears the immediate cost**, whether Sulad goes, whether the regent cooperates, and which local ties Ache loses. Each scene must change a material option, not merely supply another person who corrects Ache. Avoid six consecutive lessons, convenient overheard confessions, simplistic “Tondo bad/Maynila good,” and prophecy-like dialogue. Keep river work tactile and spatially intelligible. First-person voice should notice what Ache misunderstands as well as what he correctly perceives.

**Title review:** *Fight or Flight* and Chapter 6's *Flight* are repetitive working labels. Candidate reader titles grounded in planned action: *The Empty Place*, *The Price of Oars*, or *The Unfinished Net*. None is approved until the final image and chapter structure are written; retain functional title in Studio for now.
