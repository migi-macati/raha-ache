# Book I, Chapter 4 — cargo shift and political consequence

Reviewed: 10 October 2026, 13:55 Asia/Manila. Studio-only editorial decision. Forward checkpoint remains Chapter 4. No public publication authorized.

## Exact current-state finding

The working prose currently says a merchant asks the Tondo organizer to reserve two rowers for the following morning; the organizer says he will answer in Tondo; the merchant nods. This is an inquiry, not a booking. The next exchange has Ache say “Tomorrow's work is going to Tondo,” which overstates the visible event. The mother's defense of Tondo labor access is credible, but Chapter 4's outlined irreversible reduction in Maynila's bargaining power remains weakly dramatized. The Chapter 4–5 handoff note also inaccurately calls this a completed booking.

## Recommended scene revision (fictional, pending manuscript edit)

Replace the merchant's nod and narrator's abstract inference with a clear commercial choice **without falsely showing a completed labor contract**:

> The organizer said he would answer him in Tondo.
>
> “Then I will take my cargo to your landing at first light,” the merchant said. “I cannot leave it here waiting for your answer.”
>
> The organizer nodded. He had not promised the men. The merchant had already decided where his goods would wait for them.

This is a prospective decision to move the merchant's cargo, not proof that the rowers have been hired. It is reversible in principle but immediately transfers the place of negotiation and staging. Ache's next line should say **“Tomorrow's cargo is going to Tondo”**, not “Tomorrow's work is going to Tondo.” The mother's response can change “the cargo may follow” to **“more cargo will follow”**: she is choosing to preserve shared access to prevent further diversion, not simply submitting to an abstract threat.

## Ending and Chapter 5 handoff

Cut the two adjacent retrospective explanations beginning “I had thought that if I could prove the Tondo men were wrong” and “Now I had heard a woman tell me she would stand where her household could work.” Sima's independent two-basket bargain, the working Tondo crew and the clerk's unanswered question carry that information more naturally. Consider cutting “The clerk was waiting for someone important to tell him what to do” immediately before Ache says “Ask the men.” Preserve Sima making the clerk wait as the final image.

This revision gives Chapter 5 a concrete problem: moving a merchant's cargo back would require dependable Maynila crews and food, neither of which Ache can command into existence. It does **not** establish a military invasion, secret Tondo conspiracy, a historically attested tariff, or a particular kin identity for the Tondo ruler. The Chapter 5 departure must remain a choice under pressure, not a predetermined historical speech.

## Evidence, character and title gate

All merchant dialogue, labor arrangements, cargo transfer and political decision are novel invention. Plasencia (1589) is later comparative context for varied service obligations; the Pandanan shipwreck supports regional trade, not the exact fictional cargo procedure. Aganduru's original Ache departure wording remains unverified. Sima is not a pawn in the merchant's decision. Ache's mother keeps her own policy judgment. Sulad's fishing work remains independent. The missing sailor stays unresolved.
