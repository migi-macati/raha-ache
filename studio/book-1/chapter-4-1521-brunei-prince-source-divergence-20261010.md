# Book I Chapter 4–6 research: conflicting accounts of the Luzon prince at Brunei in 1521

**Research pass:** 10 October 2026, 06:58–07:xx Asia/Manila  
**Status:** Studio source comparison; not a public Novel/Universe claim or chapter revision.  
**Forward target:** Book I Chapter 4; checkpoint remains unchanged.  
**Retrospective chapters revised:** none.

## Directly inspected translated historical texts

1. **Antonio Pigafetta, account of the 1521 Brunei encounter**, in Lord Stanley of Alderley, ed./trans., *The First Voyage Round the World* (Hakluyt Society, 1874), Pigafetta narrative, Brunei section. Full transcription: https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/The_First_Voyage_Round_the_World/Pigafetta%27s_Account_of_Magellan%27s_Voyage . Pigafetta describes a captured **son of the ruler of Luzon** commanding junks for Brunei, returning from a campaign against **Laoe** whose inhabitants had preferred the ruler of Java. He says **Juan Carvalho** released this commander without consulting the expedition in return for gold. The passage **does not contain** a first-person speech about the prince's mother sending him away, nor does it name Ache. The expedition's interpretation of the campaign, its political loyalties and Carvalho's motive is itself a partisan outsider account, not unmediated Bruneian testimony.

2. **Gaspar Correa, *Lendas da India*, tome II, chapter XIV**, translated/excerpted in the **same** 1874 Hakluyt volume, section 'Account of the Mutiny in Port St. Julian, and Gaspar Correa's Account of the Voyage': https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/The_First_Voyage_Round_the_World/Account_of_the_Mutiny_in_Port_St._Julian,_and_Gaspar_Correa%27s_Account_of_the_Voyage . In the Brunei passage Correa instead describes the captured **son of the King of Luzon** as arriving with junks **to marry a daughter of the King of Borneo**, carrying wedding gold and jewels. Correa likewise reports an illicit release in return for valuables. This is a **later chronicler's narrative**, not a second independently authenticated interview with the prince. The 1874 editor explicitly flags an error elsewhere in Correa's retelling of Magellan's death. The marriage story therefore remains an **attested account**, not a verified event.

3. **Austin Craig, ed., *Gems of Philippine Oratory* (1924), pp. 14–15**: catalog https://openlibrary.org/books/OL1068268M/Gems_of_Philippine_oratory ; indexed digitization https://kahimyang.com/resources/books/gems-of-philippine-oratory-austin-craig.pdf . Craig prints a dramatic first-person speech attributed to 'Mahomet-ben-Suleiman,' with a claim that his mother sent him to Borneo because she could not hold Maynila against a Tondo cousin. The prior pass located this wording; this pass confirmed the edition and a searchable page-15 extract, **not** the full original-page image or its verbatim correspondence to Aganduru. Craig's text must not be quoted as a recorded 1521 speech without independent authentication.

4. **Ian Christopher Alfonso (2016)**, 'Revisiting the Provenance of the Very Strange 1589 Last Will and Testament of Don Fernando Malang Balagtas,' *Historical Bulletin* 50, 31–61, especially note 69: https://pssc.org.ph/wp-content/pssc-archives/Historical%20Bulletin/PHA%20HB%202016.pdf . Alfonso cites Craig for the reproduced English speech and explicitly questions the undisclosed source of the speech attributed to Aganduru. His tentative cousin identification is not settled genealogy.

**Original-language gate:** Aganduru Móriz's seventeenth-century Spanish passage and Correa's original Portuguese passage have **not** been checked against their 1874/1924 intermediaries. The exact historical identity of the captured Luzon prince and the relative reliability of the campaign, marriage and childhood-departure explanations require further scrutiny.

## Conflict and evidence classification

- **Shared attested core:** an expedition account places a Luzon ruler's son with ships at Brunei in 1521 and describes capture and a questionable release. This is not proof of every narrative detail or of the modern identification with Ache.
- **Divergence:** Pigafetta foregrounds **military command and a recent campaign**; Correa foregrounds a **royal marriage mission**. These need not be mutually exclusive in fiction, but neither should be fused into a single established historical itinerary.
- **Unverified retrospective story:** Craig/Alfonso's mother-sent-him narrative lacks an authenticated verbatim primary source. The Tondo cousin remains unnamed in that attributed speech; Malangsi/Lakandula identification is contested.
- **Creative possibilities, NOT adopted canon:** Brunei court factions might frame the same voyage as military service, marriage diplomacy or political survival; a character may sincerely misinterpret why another traveled. Avoid a plot twist that merely reveals 'all versions were true' without evidence-informed cause and consequence.

## Chapter-specific decisions

**Chapter 4:** Do not add an explanatory 1521 flash-forward or a historical certainty to the cargo dispute. The mother/son strategic disagreement already prepares a departure conflict. Preserve the economic cost of Maynila's reliance on Tondo; resolve Sulad's contradictory 'you told them to wait' accusation and test pacing separately.

**Chapter 5:** The outline currently specifies secret departure. The attributed Craig account instead says the mother sent Ache. Plan a decision with genuinely competing motivations and a possible later change in her consent, without treating Craig's speech as proof.

**Chapter 6:** If the mother aids departure, show one limited practical action whose political cost is legible; no miraculous ship, guaranteed Brunei reception or retrospective voice-over explaining the historical record.

**Later books:** Keep Brunei court marriage and campaign strands as separate research questions. Pigafetta's Laoe/Java account and Correa's marriage account could shape conflicts, but require independent corroboration before being narrated as fact.
