## What Chapter 7 actually leaves in Ache's hands

- **Money:** Ache spends his last small silver payment on a pole-handler. That man performs exactly the agreed work and keeps the payment. Do not let Ache produce unexplained coin or gold in Chapter 8. The Chapter 6 fare already consumed the gold and Sulad's contribution; the first-voyage payment bought no onward leg.
- **Food:** The fish earned through labor in Chapter 6 has been eaten. At dawn in Chapter 7 Ache asks whether there is anything to eat, and Sulad says not unless he saved something. They have no breakfast yet. The buyer is counting baskets when Ache offers to work; whether the buyer accepts, what work he specifies and what food he offers must be dramatized, not assumed.
- **Water and vessels:** The pair have their own water jar, used in the Chapter 7 lodging and landing scenes. Sulad refills it from a smaller vessel by the baskets on the following morning with the buyer's consent. Preserve this modest supply; don't convert it into enough provisions for a sea leg. The Chapter 7 manuscript has an **unresolved jar-location inconsistency** around Sulad's movement between the roof and landing. Do not silently certify it fixed.
- **Shelter:** The woman grants one night's space under her eaves in exchange for water-carrying and roof-binding. She has a child and a sister's absent belongings to protect. She invites them to ask again, not to stay indefinitely. She owns no obligation to store visiting cargo or supply food.
- **Basket labor:** Ache and Sulad shift the buyer's baskets under a witnessed, time-bounded mooring arrangement. Sulad volunteers three baskets and refuses an open-ended promise. Ache returns the baskets before leaving; the buyer adjusts the last one. Do not resurrect the older missing-return defect. **Open QA:** a late conversation calls these the fisherman's baskets, despite earlier possession being assigned to the buyer.
- **Mulaw and Nima:** Mulaw names himself only at dawn, does not offer passage, and leaves with Nima's sister's cloth aboard. The cloth never transfers to the buyer or the lodging woman. Mulaw's direction and return are unpromised. Do not treat him as an appointed sponsor, captain for Ache, or historical figure.
- **Sulad:** He remains free to return home, refuse new work, and withhold agreement to further travel. His choice to carry three baskets cannot become consent to a passage debt.
- **Place and route:** The landing is deliberately unnamed. No voyage length, island, weather window, named port or Brunei-bound boat has been established. A buyer's information about arrivals is only a lead.

## A distinct Chapter 8 conflict

Chapter 7 already dramatizes the moral and physical consequences of Ache promising someone else's hands and another person's ground. **Do not stage the same mistake again as the main Chapter 8 reversal.** Put pressure on *timing, credit and the impossibility of making a stranger's voyage schedule fit Ache's need*.

A plausible sequence: Ache asks the buyer for paid work and for information; the buyer distinguishes the two. A vessel with a credible commercial obligation refuses passengers because its cargo, provisions, or next landing are already committed. Ache can propose his own future service, but the counterparty asks how to enforce payment if the boat sails without him or he is unable to work. Sulad separately states his own terms. The negotiated outcome, if any, must specify the first leg only, which provisions each person supplies, the latest decision time, and the consequence of delay. If no agreement is yet possible, end the chapter with a concrete expiring opportunity rather than another lesson about asking permission.
