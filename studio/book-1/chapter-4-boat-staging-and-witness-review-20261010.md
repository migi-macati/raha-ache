# Book I, Chapter 4 — boat staging and witness continuity review

## Findings requiring correction before publication

1. **Departure of the disputed crew:** The organizer tells the rowers to take another assignment. Two follow him, while his sister's son remains holding the bowline. The young man then lets the boat swing free and follows them. As written, the empty boat appears to drift away without an identified person aboard to steer it. Stage two rowers boarding, the nephew casting off and boarding, and one rower pushing away under control; the organizer can follow the boat along the bank. Avoid implying an unattended boat is abandoned in the current.

2. **Hearing witnesses:** The organizer returns explicitly **without his crew**, yet the next paragraph has two rowers testify about provisioning and the disputed promise. Either bring specific rowers back as witnesses or identify two independent landing workers who saw the food prepared and heard the conditional inquiry. Do not let a witness appear without an arrival.

3. **Paddle action:** “Another wedged a paddle against the landing” is physically vague, especially while the current is already pulling the stern away. Prefer a controlled draw stroke to bring the stern closer, with the line keeping the bow in place. Exact tidal direction and riverbed geometry remain unverified; do not invent a precise current speed or tide.

## Pacing and dramatic assessment

The scene presently gives Ache successive corrections from the clerk, Sima, his mother, Sulad and Sima again. Their independent objections are credible, but this rhythm risks becoming a succession of lessons. The manuscript already has a counterweight worth preserving: Ache correctly identifies the danger that crew marks can be repurposed, and a merchant's independent decision to arrange tomorrow's labor through Tondo confirms the broader political risk. Keep the mother genuinely fallible and Sima commercially capable. Do not make the organizer a conspirator to vindicate Ache.

The strongest next-scene consequence is not a speech about Maynila losing control. It is a future booking quietly moving to Tondo because the organizer's network is easier to use. Chapter 5 must show how Ache interprets this result, how his mother weighs actual provisioning against sovereignty, and why their disagreement becomes a costly choice.
