# Book I, Chapter 6 — Scene 4A: The Weight of the Net

*Studio working prose, first half of Scene 4. Ache narrates. Sulad has not accepted passage; no vessel is booked. Fictional fishing household and dialogue.*

When I reached the fishing bank, Sulad was standing in the water with the net around his knees.

His uncle had pulled one end onto the sand. Each time Sulad lifted the other, the wet knots dragged against his legs and caught on the stones. A small fish flapped inside a fold. He bent, freed it and dropped it into the basket at his side.

I waited until he looked up.

“Have you found your captain?” he asked.

“No.”

“Then you have found me at a good time.”

He hauled the net another arm's length. I took hold of the edge nearest me. It was heavier than it looked. When I pulled, I brought a handful of mud onto the clean part his uncle had already spread.

“Not there,” Sulad said.

I let go. He shook the mud free with two quick movements.

“You could have told me.”

“I did.”

His uncle watched us from the bank. He had a narrow piece of wood in his hand and was working a knot loose with its point. Beside him lay a torn section of net, its edges tied apart so he could see which strands still held.

“Your mother has sent for me?” the uncle asked.

“No. I came to speak with Sulad.”

“Then speak. He can hear while he works.”

I told them about the trader and the space he might have for a passenger. I said the man would not name his first landing until he knew where he could sell his cargo. I said I had asked my mother for help and she wanted a carrier, provisions and a way to send word before she would part with anything.

“You have no place on the boat,” Sulad's uncle said.

“No.”

“And you came to ask for my nephew.”

“I came to tell him what I know.”

“You know very little.”

I opened my mouth. Sulad gave the net a sharp tug and the fish basket overturned at my feet. Three fish slid toward the water. I dropped to my knees and caught two. The third was gone before I reached it.

Sulad set down his end of the net. He looked at the empty place in the basket, then at me.

“Those were for tonight,” he said.

“I'll replace it.”

“With what?”

I had silver left, though less than before I paid the messenger's brother. I took it out.

His uncle held up the wooden point. “Put that away. The fish is gone. If you want to help, keep the basket upright.”

I pushed the basket farther up the bank and stayed beside it while they finished bringing in the net. It took longer than I had expected. Once the last weight was on the sand, Sulad crouched by the torn section and began passing new fiber through the old mesh.

His uncle turned to me. “He has spoken to me.”

I looked at Sulad.

“He spoke before you came,” the uncle said. “He says he may go with you. He says he has not decided.”

“That's true.”

“He also says you asked him to decide for himself.”

“I did.”

“Good. Then you will not answer for him now.”
