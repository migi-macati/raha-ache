# Book I, Chapter 9 — Northern trade-route evidence and itinerary gate

**Research status:** Secondary-source reading; editorial planning only. **Checked:** 19 October 2026 (Asia/Manila). This note does not establish the location of Ache's unnamed landing or publish Chapter 9.

## Source inspected

Roderich Ptak, “The Northern Trade Route to the Spice Islands: South China Sea – Sulu Zone – North Moluccas (14th to early 16th century),” *Archipel* 43 (1992), 27–56, DOI: 10.3406/arch.1992.2804. Open scholarly text: https://www.persee.fr/doc/arch_0044-8613_1992_num_43_1_2804 .

The article's opening (printed p. 27, directly inspected) distinguishes a Java route and a Borneo route toward the Moluccas. Ptak proposes that ships on the latter could follow Borneo's coast northeastward, **perhaps** calling at Brunei, and enter the Sulu Sea via Balabac Strait. He separately describes ships traveling from China through Luzon, then south via Mindoro or the Calamianes toward the Sulu Sea. His conditional language matters: these are reconstructed trade networks, **not** a manifest for Ache's boat, an established stop at Brunei, or proof that any particular leg was available to passengers. The article's full route argument and references beyond the accessible opening have **not** been independently page-checked in this pass.

Related period source already checked in the Chapter 8 port-trust source gate: Tomé Pires, *Suma Oriental* (1512–1515), Cortesão English edition (1944), vol. I, printed pp. 133–134, reports regional Luções–Borneo commerce. That does not establish a single Luzon-to-Brunei passenger service or its sailing time. William Henry Scott, “Boat-Building and Seamanship in Classic Philippine Society,” *Philippine Studies* 30(3) (1982), 335–376, is an appropriate vessel/seamanship comparator; specific craft and handling claims must be checked in its text before adoption.

## Plot decisions this evidence permits

- A **multi-leg**, commercially contingent route toward Brunei is historically plausible at network scale. It remains a **fictional itinerary** until the story specifies Ache's landing, each counterparty, each cargo obligation, and what makes the next leg possible.
- Chapter 8's mat carrier offers only one conditional leg to another **unnamed** landing. Her vessel must not quietly become a Brunei-bound trader; Chapter 9 first needs the departure, cargo delivery, earned food and a new bargain.
- Do not insert Balabac, Mindoro, the Calamianes, Palawan, Sulu, or Brunei as the first destination merely because a scholarly network traversed them. Select an itinerary after checking geography, season, vessel type, elapsed time and the characters' actual information.
- If the storm is the Chapter 9 climax, establish the open-water exposure, crew roles, provisions, weather observations and competing cargo/rescue duties **before** it arrives. Do not invent a pursuing vessel to accelerate the plot.
- Sulad's consent renews at each leg; Bulkan cannot appear without an independently established route. Ache's leadership must grow from enforceable choices with consequences, not from authority over another person's boat.
