## Title decision
**Preferred reader-facing title: The Second Crossing** (provisional).

The disputed second transfer is a literal crossing whose terms neither party understands the same way. Ache also crosses a political boundary when he speaks for Sima without her consent. The second meaning is earned by her refusal and the closing decision to ask rowers before recording their names.

Other possibilities: **The Uncounted** (centers rowers but makes the bookkeeping motif overly explicit); **What the River Keeps** (evocative but risks repeating maritime metaphors across the trilogy); **Encroachment** (accurate Studio working label, too abstract for a published title).

Retain **Encroachment** as the functional outline title until the public prose is ready. Do not change chapter metadata, navigation, or links yet. On publication, review the final scene again and propagate the selected title through canonical chapter metadata and navigation without altering the shared Read Aloud template.

## Editorial issues found in the second-pass Studio manuscript
- The opening establishes Sima's baskets **on shore**, but the explanatory paragraph still says the first movement was **from a small boat**. Clarify that the baskets move from the landing **by** small boat to the larger vessel.
- After the organizer's nephew follows the other rowers, the standalone sentence **“She did not.”** lacks its former antecedent. Replace with Sima's observable choice not to call the crew back.
- The endnote still calls the manuscript **first-pass prose** even though it is a second-pass draft. Correct the status in Studio; never expose this editorial gate in public Novel.
- The Chapter 3 ending and next-morning Chapter 4 opening are now consistent. The rower service dispute and the physical movement of boats still require a final mechanics review.

## Historical and narrative boundary
Sima, the crew dispute, paired tally boards, and both meanings of the title are fictional. Plasencia's 1589 testimony is later comparative evidence for differentiated rowing service, not direct proof of the novel's invented c.1500 bargain. No new primary-source verification was completed in this title pass.
