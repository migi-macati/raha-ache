## Finding

The published opening five chapters are substantially shorter than the subsequent chapters. Approximate narrative word counts, excluding frontmatter and collapsed historical notes, are: Chapter 1 **1,849** (2 scenes); Chapter 2 **3,155** (4); Chapter 3 **2,688** (5); Chapter 4 **2,753** (5); Chapter 5 **4,180** (6). Book I Chapter 6 is approximately **10,715** words, and Book II's ten chapters are approximately **10,500–15,300** words each. The discrepancy is evidence of a change in drafting scale, **not proof** that an opening chapter needs padding. Chapter 1 may benefit from its fast, comic-to-consequential turn; preserve that advantage unless a close reading identifies a missing dramatic step.

An exact 18-word overlap scan of narrative text, excluding historical-note `<details>` sections, found no cross-chapter repeats **within** each of Books I, II and III. This is a limited lexical check, not an assessment of repeated situations, diction or emotional beats. Do not use a word-count target as a revision criterion.

## Chapter-specific editorial tests

**Chapter 1, the wager and the damaged landing.** The race, broken goods, injured rower and mother's first hearing occur in only two scenes. Test whether the injured rower's work and the fisherman's torn net have enough physical presence before Ache offers to pay from stores he has not checked. A revision should deepen the immediate cost of his mistake and Sulad's independent response without adding a second accident or delaying the brisk opening. Preserve the ending in which Sulad forgets to claim the rice cakes.

**Chapter 2, the hearing and mother's private reckoning.** Four scenes move from restitution to disputed river payments, Ache's remark about his father, his mother's private apology and the records. Test whether the labor and property claims are sufficiently distinct in the reader's memory, and whether the mother's decisions have credible material limits. Her apology should retain its uncomfortable specificity; do not convert it into an exposition speech about Maynila's institutions. Keep the first-light fishing-stakes promise and its later partial fulfillment in Chapters 3–4.

**Chapter 3, Ula's upriver journey and the unnamed captain.** Five compact scenes cover the riverside gathering, an imperfect speech, the larger trading vessel, Sulad's discomfort and the Tondo clerk. Test whether Ula's reasons for bringing Ache, the gathering's ordinary work and the captain's commercial constraints remain visible beyond Ache's curiosity. The captain is **unnamed** in published canon; do not import the unmerged Nakhoda Hamid identification. Ula has offered a conditional inquiry, not guaranteed passage or a settled Brunei relationship. Preserve Sulad's separate motives and the ending's unresolved access to crew marks.

**Chapter 4, Sima's disputed second crossing.** Five scenes make Sima's household, the organizer, the crew and the two clerks central. Test whether the first transfer's payment and the rowers' still-unpaid wages can be distinguished without an explanatory aside. The regent's order concerns the **second** voyage; it does not retrospectively settle the first. Sima must retain the power to reject Ache's interpretation of her agreement. The fishing-stakes labor should remain physical work, not a symbolic shortcut. Do not resolve the crew's choice in the clerk's voice.

**Chapter 5, the boat that stays.** Six scenes already give Bulkan, Sulad's uncle, Sulad, the mother and Ula separate grounds to refuse or condition Ache's request. Test whether each refusal changes Ache's next concrete action, and whether the rower's unpaid wages remain a distinct obligation. Preserve the conditional maternal allowance, the unconfirmed messenger and captain, and Sulad's undecided participation. No departure occurs here: Chapter 6 owns the actual passage.

## Revision sequence and acceptance criteria

Start with a close reading of Chapters 1–2 alongside Chapters 3–6, focusing on causal transitions and whether ordinary workers have identifiable interests independent of Ache. Record exact passages that fail a test before drafting. If no concrete omission is found, leave the chapter unchanged. For each warranted correction, revise the smallest scene on a clean main-based branch, compare its original and revised beats, and preserve established chronology, promises, character identities and outcomes.

A prose expansion earns its place only if it adds consequential action, differentiated perception, historically plausible material constraints or character agency. Do not add scenes to reach six, inflate word counts, make Ache prematurely competent, manufacture romance, or imply historical institutions beyond the evidence. Keep first-person Ache voice except where an already established POV transfer applies. Review titles after any substantial revision, favoring layered concrete imagery over explanatory labels.

Before proposing public release, check the revised scene against its preceding and following published chapters; compare named characters for exact **and near-duplicate** names (the Iman/Imran collision and subsequent Latif correction in Book II Chapter 4 demonstrate why); separate source-backed setting from fiction; inspect the historical-notes contract; run the repository build and semantic-link checks; and require exact-head PR CI plus GitHub Pages deployment evidence. Artifact inspection cannot establish rendered live verification or actual Read Aloud playback.
