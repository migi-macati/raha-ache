## A: published control (237 words, 1274 characters)

The foreigner took the board and made a new mark beside the old line. I asked the interpreter what he had written.

“He says the boat is released.”

“Does it say to whom?”

The interpreter hesitated. “It says the claim was refused.”

“Whose claim?”

He did not answer immediately. The foreigner had already turned away. The interpreter called him back and asked a question I could not follow. The foreigner pointed at me, then at the woman. He said my title with an ending that was not ours.

The interpreter came back to us. “He has written that the old chief of Maynila objected.”

The woman stared at the board. “I objected.”

“I know,” I said.

“But his words will stay.”

The interpreter held the board so she could see it. There was a narrow space below the line. He asked the foreigner for the reed pen, but the man refused to hand it over. After a moment he added something himself, two small groups of marks, and showed the board again.

“What now?” I asked.

“The woman says the boat is hers.”

“Did you tell him her name?”

“I told him. I cannot make him write it as I said it.”

The woman gave her name a second time, slowly, as if the marks might listen. The interpreter repeated it. The foreigner said a version that left out part of it. He put the board away.

## What B changes

B compresses only the second register exchange, reducing it by **58 words / 308 characters**. It retains the mistaken credit to the old chief, the woman's direct correction, her warning that the official words will endure, the interpreter's inability to force accurate transcription, the foreigner's refusal to hand over the pen, and the poignant image of the woman saying her name as if the marks might listen. B preserves the surrounding scene verbatim, including the woman's later rebuke to Ache, the young carrier's paid help, the unrecovered husband and Ache's trembling hand. It does **not** move the canoe incident or change Awi's first-person handoff.

**Risks to test:** The clipped “The claim was refused” could obscure which claim was refused; the shorter handoff could flatten the interpreter's hesitation or make the foreigner's written credit feel less deliberate. The more direct sequencing may reduce the scene's sense of bureaucratic delay, which is itself part of the woman's injury. B shortens roughly 0.6% of the whole chapter and cannot by itself resolve the larger first-three-section pacing exposure.

## Decision record: scene-level comparison and coda payoff (2 November 2026, 08:00 PHT)

**Control retained; B rejected on observable craft/causality grounds, not on a claimed reader test.** Reread the complete published canoe episode, the Awi roof/illness sequence, and the post-death return of the canoe owner. In A, the interpreter first reports the foreigner's claim that the boat is released, hesitates when asked *to whom*, then physically calls back the foreigner, who points at Ache and the woman while misnaming Ache's title. That hesitation and second exchange make the false attribution a visible action by the record-maker, not merely an information summary. B's adjacent `The boat is released` / `The claim was refused` reads as an unexplained contradiction, and its clipped response removes the interpreter's uncertainty and the foreigner's pointing. The reduction of 58 words (under 0.5% of the chapter) has no credible path to correcting the much larger pre-fever pacing exposure.

The woman's own `I objected` and `But his words will stay` remain central; the subsequent physical failure of Ache's trembling hand and the woman's rebuke that he spoke after she answered establish his declining authority. In the final Awi POV, after Ache dies, the same woman deliberately gives Awi her name, waits until Awi repeats it accurately, and affirms the canoe can be hired on her own terms. That payoff is stronger when the earlier failed recording remains legible and resistant to Ache's attempted intervention. Awi's bowl ownership, roof repair, temporary lodging and independent witness remain separate agency tests; do not turn her into a posthumous executor of Ache's commands.
