## Controlled A/B test

The unresolved question is whether the losses change Ache's final options and relationships enough to earn a trilogy climax.

**B: character-led structural proposal, not replacement prose.**

1. In III.9, bring the contested watcher charge and diverted boat into one decision: Ache must publicly choose between claiming authority to retain the trade and admitting he cannot guarantee safe passage or levy an unagreed charge. **Preserve** the existing fish-sale loss in III.9 scene 11–12, the missed Tesa work and Amaya's unpaid cloth. Test whether the trade diversion and misrepresented mark can cause an additional *change of choice* rather than inventing another loss. Do not manufacture a historical political office or command.
2. Have Amaya, Dala or Raya react from an independently established stake, rather than delivering another correction of Ache's ethics. One relationship should be changed in a way that cannot be reset by a new hearing.
3. In III.10, make Ache's decision address that loss, not merely restate his willingness to carry baskets. His sacrifice should foreclose a personally desired status, privilege or immediate reunion; avoid a convenient resolution of his mother's fate.
4. Keep the old stones, the limited repaired step, Harun's merchant terms, independent landing authorities and the single-passenger ending if the latter remains the strongest image. Preserve the water-level safety distinction.
5. A maternal-thread payoff must be evidence-bounded: a credible limited report or a demonstrably failed search with an emotional consequence. A new message cannot conveniently certify death or reunion.

### Revised diagnosis after scene inventory

**Corrected earlier overstatement:** III.9 and III.10 *already* depict concrete lost fish sales, missed paid work, trade diversion, extra carrier expense, a real engineering failure and the continued cloth debt. It would be false to call the chapters consequence-free. The narrower P1 hypothesis is **dissociation between those losses and Ache's decisive personal transformation**: the commercial losses chiefly fall on Sela and other owners, while Ache ends with a familiar promise to work. Test whether a stronger choice can be earned from these existing costs without manufacturing a new disaster.

**Most defensible compression candidates, not yet prose edits:** III.9 scenes 3–4 (setup/hearing), 6–7 (measure/sourcing), 9–10 (rumor/correction); III.10 scenes 3–4 (separate complaint and labor), 11–12 (step test/ground conditions), 16–17 (two accounting hearings). These are *candidates*, not automatic deletions: each currently contains information and independent agency. A viable revision must preserve witnesses, owner-specific limits, causality and dates.

Keep the public manuscript unchanged until the alternative demonstrates a more powerful climax without weakening the existing moral and historical specificity.

### B1 alternative text (first-person Ache)

Sela laid the stained leaf wrapping beside the keeper's knife. The brown patch had spread through the fold where the fish had rested.

"The boat took half a basket," she said. "The buyer paid for that half. The rest went by the upper path and arrived after he left. I paid the carrier for the walk. I sold the fish later for less."

"Did you see the buyer turn it away?" the keeper asked.

"The carrier told me. I saw him come back with it. I sold it afterward."

The keeper set one strip beneath the leaf and another beside it. She would not put the unseen refusal on the same strip as the money Sela had counted.

I had carried the first half across Raya's new timber. I remembered the pull of the basket as the water rose. The rest of the fish had not crossed. No mark could make the buyer wait.

Laman came in without Hiran. He said she had taken work with another owner.

"She warned me the water was rising," he said. "I paid her the waiting measure and the journey we agreed. I saw the first basket come aboard. I did not see the other one reach the buyer."

Sela nodded. They had each lost something, and neither asked the keeper to charge the bank for it.

Raya put a wet stone beside the two strips.

"This is what came loose under the lower bearing," she said. "I packed larger stones there. Yesterday, at low water, the step held a carried basket. When the water reached the mark, I closed it. I will inspect it when someone asks to use it. If I am away, no one can say I have opened it."

"Could another maker inspect it?" Laman asked.

"If that person knows the work and wishes to answer for it."

Dala stood at the doorway with her net weights in one hand. She had come to hear about the ground above the step.

"One basket may wait there after I lift the net," she told Sela. "Ask me on the morning you need it. I may have to set the net again."

Sela touched the stained leaf. "I have a buyer who may take a smaller load next market. I will ask Marun to bring me word before I hire a boat."

"Has he agreed?" the keeper asked.

"Not yet."

The keeper left space for his answer.

The cord owner came carrying a jar from the farther landing. He had brought it for a buyer above the market. The three knots were still in his line, though he had pulled them apart.

"The owners there pay a man to tell them when the dry patch is full," he said. "They know he may tell them to turn back. They pay for the news."

"Then they know what they have bought," Sela said.

"I could have offered the same service here."

"You offered a measure from every boat," Laman said. "I never hired your watcher."

"I know."

The clay-weight trader had been listening from the doorway. He asked Dala whether two jars could wait on her path at the next market.

"Not on my net ground."

He picked up his jar. "Then I will take them by the other landing. I have already asked for news there."

The cord owner moved aside to let him pass. He had won that trade. He did not ask anyone to call him the winner.

Laman said he might carry Sela's next load if no one had hired him first. He would ask Hiran about the pole work when he knew the journey. Dala went back to her net. Raya took the wet stone away before it marked the keeper's mat.

"Does anyone still ask me to record a charge for every boat?" the keeper said.

No one did.

Sela folded the stained leaf. I remembered the grain she had paid me for carrying one basket the market before. I had taken it because I did the work. She had paid others too, and still lost two sales. Amaya had counted grain from Tesa's carrying work toward the cloth I owed her. I could not take that payment back. The keeper could remember what happened. She could not make it cheaper.

### B1 comparison and decision gate

- **Causal retention:** Sela's recorded price loss and reported buyer refusal remain distinct; Laman/Hiran's paid labor remains separate; Raya's low-water inspection, Dala's net path, Marun's unaccepted offer, the clay-weight trader's departure and the rejected universal fee survive.
- **Deliberate addition:** Ache explicitly separates the **III.9 wage Sela paid him** from the **Tesa wages Amaya accepted**. These are different payments; the first draft conflated them and was corrected before publication. This remains reflection, not a new irreversible decision; test for repetitive moral explanation.
- **Deliberate cuts:** Repeated enumeration of every owner's terms and the keeper's secondary summary. Risk: less texture of the keeper's recordkeeping and reduced independent space for Laman and Sela. Restore only if the shortened passage loses clarity or voice.
- **Provisional result:** A controlled compression sample exists; no proof yet that it improves reader engagement or earns the trilogy ending. The larger climax and maternal payoff remain unresolved.


## 2 November 2026: B1 agency and trilogy-ending cross-check

**Control re-read:** Published III.9 scene 14 and III.10 scenes 1–5 and 15–18, plus bonus III.11–12 endings, against main `e002e40941fb15728bdd6fa93af90646708ae661`. B1 remains an **unpublished Studio specimen**. No reader evidence supports its adoption.

**Decision: REJECT B1 as written; preserve published III.10 scenes 16–17.** B1 shortens the two hearings, but its gain is primarily lexical. It loses three causally distinct acts: (1) Sela's explicit account of a buyer who will pay for a *smaller* next load and her still-unmade hiring decision; (2) Laman's separate completed oil journey, with Hiran working for another owner and reserving the right to give her own account; and (3) the cord owner's direct question to Sela about whether she will use his farther landing again, which gives the rival a negotiated future rather than an abstract defeat. B1 also adds Ache's explicit accounting moral at the end, repeating a lesson already dramatized by the losses and the keeper's marks. These are concrete regressions against the agency, commercial-causality and subtext gates. **Do not apply B1 or revive the previously rejected III.10 hearing-compression PR unchanged.**

**The actual climax:** III.9 ends with Amaya publicly naming the unpaid cloth, the rival owner securing enough traffic to operate elsewhere, and Ache accepting that he cannot open another person's step. In III.10 Ache *keeps Tesa's paid work* even when Raya needs a lift, earns rice Amaya accepts toward the cloth, and witnesses a half-load cross before rising water forces closure. Sela's later lower sale price and extra carrier cost cannot be undone by the successful crossing. The final return to Tesa's awning keeps the cloth debt unpaid; Amaya independently rejects an inferior weave. The single passenger landing on old stones is a modest, genuine success. This is a coherent bounded climax, although whether its emotional scale satisfies a trilogy remains a **P1 reader-impact hypothesis**, not a verified defect.

**Bonus continuity:** III.11 shows the costs of foreign military intrusion and missing people without magically solving the local claims; III.12 transfers the last first-person perspective to a household witness after Ache's death and treats his mother's dented copper bowl as an independently witnessed gift. A proposed new maternal message or definitive maternal fate in III.10 would conflict with that deliberately open line. The late POV transfer is part of the bonus architecture, not a violation of Ache's primary first-person voice.

**Next narrower experiment:** Test *one* change in Ache's personal reaction at the III.10 scene-15 loss or scene-18 Tesa exchange, against the existing control, only if it reveals a previously unexpressed desire or forfeiture through behavior. Do not add another speech about consent, a new casualty, a sudden cloth payoff, or a manufactured refusal by Amaya. Compare III.9 scene 14, III.10 scenes 7–8/15/18, and III.11's opening in sequence.
