## Question and control

The chapter makes Ache pay a real cost for leaving Burné: he rejects Laila's paid receiving work, accepts Harun's conditional route, writes his name on the fare terms, and spends money saved for his own landing to replace the crew's broken water jar. These actions are causally necessary and should survive. The P1 hypothesis is narrower: repeated verbal restatements of independent command and consent in the *final* palace exchange may weaken the tension of Ache finally signing his name.

**Control, immediately before Siripada approves the bargain:** Siripada asks separately who leads the party, who commands Harun's vessel, what happens if Harun will not enter Maynila, and who will speak ashore. Jamil then repeats Isan's interpreting limit and Sulad's working-place limit. Ache answers, “I know.” The same chapter has already dramatized these limits through Harun, Sulad and the other owners. The control does protect each person's agency and makes the political stakes explicit.

## Variant A: compress the final palace interrogation

**Replace only the exchange beginning “Who leads your party?” and ending at Ache's “I know.” Keep the preceding trade-goods terms and the following paragraph beginning “Siripada held out his hand.”**

> “Who leads your party?” Siripada asked.
>
> “I do.”
>
> Jamil turned Harun's board toward him. “Harun commands the vessel. Sulad has agreed to work his passage. Isan will interpret what he understands. Neither has pledged to fight for you.”
>
> Siripada touched the place where my mark was still missing. “If Harun refuses the river?”
>
> “We find a landing he accepts. Or we turn back.”
>
> “And who speaks for you when you reach the shore?”
>
> “I do. Sulad may speak if he wishes. Isan can translate what he knows.”

**Potential gain:** The ruler's finger lands on Ache's unsigned obligation; an existing physical object supplies tension in place of another abstract explanation. Harun's veto and Sulad/Isan's limits remain explicit. Ache owns the risk in first person.

**Potential regression:** The control gives Siripada and Jamil more separate authority and more carefully distinguishes Isan's agreed interpreting scope from what he is able to understand. Variant A could falsely broaden Isan's obligation. Before promotion, reread the actual Isan bargain and confirm that the compressed words do not enlarge it. If they do, reject or revise A; shorter dialogue alone is not success.

## Variant B: reduce the final thematic restatement

**Control:** After Harun orders departure, Ache says, “I had not given the order to move Harun's boat. I had chosen to be on it when he did.” Later, the final paragraph states that Ache had once believed he knew what belonged to him and now returns with a letter asking to be heard and people who agreed to travel only so far.

**Test:** Delete only the two-sentence “I had not given the order” passage. Leave the action, the letter and the closing image unchanged. The reader has just watched Harun give the order, and the later paragraph supplies Ache's changed understanding.

**Potential gain:** The narrative trusts the already dramatized decision, avoiding a double moral conclusion immediately before the final emotional image.

**Potential regression:** The removed sentence is a precise distillation of Ache's agency without control over another man's boat, arguably the book's earned resolution. If the ending becomes emotionally diffuse or the contrast with Book I's boatlessness loses force, retain the published control. Do not combine A and B automatically.

## Adjacent-chapter and character gates

Read II.9's unresolved Nasir inquiry and III.1's opening offshore arrival together with II.10's final departure. Preserve Laila's refusal to organize her life around Ache, her independent work, Nasir's sister's ownership of the inquiry, Sulad's self-negotiated berth, Harun's command and the actual water-jar payment. Do not add a fleet, a royal grant, a maternal reply or new casualties. The ruler's letter authorizes a request to be heard, not a claim to Maynila.

**A/B acceptance:** (1) every contractual scope is accurate to the preceding negotiations; (2) the farewell still has the emotional cost of a credible Burné life left behind; (3) the signing and jar payment remain two different concrete commitments; (4) an uninterrupted read produces a clearer climax without flattening Siripada/Jamil or losing the book's final transformation; (5) a separate reader's preference, if ever obtained, is recorded as actual evidence, never invented. Reject either variant if its gain cannot be demonstrated.
