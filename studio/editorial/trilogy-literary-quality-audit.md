## Goal and release gates

**Aspirational target:** exceptional international-standard historical fiction across the complete trilogy. No internal numeric score, completed checklist, publication milestone or source-note count establishes a 10/10 outcome. Compare technique with *Wolf Hall* (political psychology), *Shōgun* (sustained narrative momentum) and *A Wizard of Earthsea* (economy and earned transformation); do not imitate their voices or claim award competitiveness.

The six gated phases are:

- [ ] **1. Complete manuscript diagnosis.** Read all 32 chapters consecutively; map each scene's conflict, desire, turning point, irreversible consequence, emotional change, and purpose. Record chapter-specific evidence and score ten dimensions only after the full read.
- [ ] **2. Structural revision.** Fix demonstrated P0 arc/climax defects and P1 repeated conflicts, low suspense, weak reversals and unearned outcomes. Preserve the 10+10+10 core structure and two bonus chapters.
- [ ] **3. Character and emotional revision.** Audit Ache's ambition, contradictions, attachments and transformation; independently track Sulad, Ache's mother, Amaya and other major characters. Test danger, desire, humor, intimacy, betrayal, grief, triumph and surprise where appropriate to the actual story.
- [ ] **4. Prose and historical revision.** Improve voice, rhythm, imagery, subtext and compression; verify period-specific claims and label retrospective testimony, reconstruction and invention. Do not expand merely to equalize chapter lengths.
- [ ] **5. Independent validation.** Solicit actual, consented reader/editor/historian feedback where available; record who read what, completion, confusion, engagement, disagreements and revisions. Never fabricate beta readers.
- [ ] **6. Definitive-edition QA.** Reread the revised trilogy end-to-end; resolve continuity and historical issues; test deployed navigation, mobile readability and audible Read Aloud. Distinguish successful HTML/CI from rendered-live verification.

**Ten assessment dimensions:** originality; historical authenticity/world; prose and distinctive voice; character psychology; plot/suspense; pacing/structure; dialogue/relationships; themes/subtext; emotional impact; cumulative endings and lasting effect. A high average cannot hide a critical failure. For each dimension record textual evidence, strongest counterexample, severity, corrective experiment and result. Scores remain **UNASSESSED** until full coverage and calibrated review; independent validation is a separate field.

**Severity and workflow:** P0 = broken structural arc, causality or climactic payoff; P1 = repeated dramatic engine, weak stakes, thin character agency or emotional consequence; P2 = prose, dialogue, compression; P3 = local polish and metadata. No prose rewrite without an identified issue, a smallest viable scene-level change, a continuity test against adjacent chapters, and a scoped PR with exact-head passing CI before merge. Track before/after literary trade-offs; revert regression. Keep source-note work in `studio/editorial/chapter-historical-notes-packet.md`.

## Completed consecutive close reading: Book I Chapters 1–6 (6/32)

### I.1 — *The Boy Prince*

**Verified causal arc:** Ache and Sulad wager on a quick river turn; a cargo boat breaks loose; Ache attempts rescue but ignores Sulad's warning to lower the sail fully; the rescue saves the child while damaging boats, fishing gear, a porter's passage and a rower's hand. Ache promises restitution without knowing what he owns. His mother orders a hearing; the chapter ends with Ache asking the fisherman to show him the torn net and Sulad not claiming the rice cakes.

**Strength to protect:** Fast comic opening turns into genuine bodily and economic harm. The child's rescue is real and cannot simply be erased by the harm; Sulad's fear and the mother's fear create emotional counterweights. The final unclaimed wager has earned resonance.

**P1 hypothesis RA-LIT-01:** Ache's mother supplies an explicit explanation of political responsibility immediately after the accident (the 'separate river' exchange). Read the later trilogy for recurrence of authority figures delivering thematic corrections after each mistake. **Acceptance:** retain her fear and the concrete restitution hearing, but test whether Ache's choices and relationships can carry more of the lesson without recurring declarative dialogue. Do not rewrite yet.

### I.2 — *The Paddle Against the Wall*

**Verified causal arc:** The hearing gives separate claims to the fisherman, porter, cargo owner and injured rower's sister. Ache pledges his own ornament and labor, while the injured rower's lost livelihood remains unsettled. Tondo's messenger offers a one-season shared tally arrangement; Ache challenges its future political implications. A private exchange with his mother complicates his idealization of his dead father. Ledger inspection shows uncertain collectors and crew marks; Ache resolves to investigate who gives orders in Maynila's name.

**Strength to protect:** The sister places her brother's paddle against the wall and refuses a premature settlement. Her unanswered question about how long the hand will heal gives the scene a human cost that no tally can settle. The mother's public mistake about Ache's father and subsequent private apology complicate her authority. Tondo's proposal is attractive to merchants for intelligible reasons.

**P1 hypothesis RA-LIT-02:** The chapter repeatedly explains political trade-offs through mother/son questions and aphoristic answers ('Which boundaries?' and the ensuing sequence). Test whether the conflict is dramatized strongly enough by the merchant, messenger and injured family, and whether the mother has a privately costly decision beyond tutoring Ache. **Acceptance:** an editor can state each party's incompatible desire without relying on explanatory dialogue; retain the mother's apology and the unresolved injury.

### I.3 — *A Grandson Is Not a Fleet*

**Verified causal arc:** After partial restitution and continued tally inspection, Ache accompanies Ula upriver rather than confronting Tondo collectors. Ula's gathering reveals religious practice and everyday maritime preparation; an unnamed Malay-speaking captain punctures Ache's assumption that kinship guarantees passage or command. Sulad refuses to surrender his mother's protective cord for Ache. Back in Maynila, a Tondo clerk explains that crew marks can help locate missing people but also expose rowers to coercive claimants. Ache asks who may inspect the board; the clerk has not answered at the ending.

**Strength to protect:** The captain's limited knowledge and practical concern with food/prices make Brunei tangible without promising a fleet. Sulad's cord has independent emotional stakes. The clerk's dual-use record is a genuine dilemma, not a simple villain's instrument. The opening's partial restitution and Tondo's operational arrival preserve continuity from I.2.

**P1 hypothesis RA-LIT-03:** Ula repeatedly articulates the thematic lesson for Ache ('the river ... drowns everyone equally'; the warning about ruling an empty shore). Combined with I.1–2, this may produce a predictable tutor/correction pattern. **Acceptance:** across the next seven chapters, record how often Ache learns through another person's speech versus independent, surprising or irreversible consequences; revise only if repetition is substantiated. Preserve Ula's humor and spiritual independence.

**P1 continuity/character gate RA-LIT-04:** The clerk's unanswered question about access to crew marks is a specific political and personal promise. Track whether subsequent chapters develop the danger of searchable labor records rather than merely repeating that records can be misused. Track Sulad's cord as a relationship stake, not only a symbol of tolerance. **Acceptance:** map exact later callbacks and consequences; no invented resolution.

**P2 prose gate RA-LIT-05:** Opening chapters are brisk and voice-driven, with recurring sharp question-answer dialogue. Check the full trilogy for overreliance on short pedagogical exchanges, repeated physical motifs (cords, baskets, knots, tallies, jars), and uniformly modest closing gestures. **Acceptance:** identify actual repetition and show a stronger alternative before changing any sentence; retain meaningful motif recurrence.

**No confirmed P0 defect yet.** The first three findings are hypotheses; the additional close read below identifies a more concrete P1 repetition issue. This is not permission to inflate the opening chapters. A reader should be able to disagree with each proposed revision based on the text.

### I.4 — *The Second Crossing*

**Verified causal arc:** The Tondo clerk answers the access-to-crew-marks question left open in I.3: he says he would ask a claimant why the information was needed, even if the claimant were a raha. Sima disputes a supposed second voyage with a Tondo organizer; Ache prematurely speaks for her and loses her trust. The organizer's sister's son reveals that wages from the *first* voyage remain unpaid even though Sima paid the organizer. Ache's mother limits what the shared boards can prove and sends the steward to investigate crew terms. Sima negotiates another boat without ceding her voice to Ache.

**Strength to protect:** The clerk's response follows the previous chapter's precise question, Sima's rebuke is an earned humiliation, and the organizer has plausible economic grievances. The organizer's young relative exposes an important distinction between payment to an intermediary and payment to workers. The unresolved wages have consequences in I.5–6.

**P1 diagnostic RA-LIT-06 (confirmed across I.4–6):** A succession of authority figures and workers repeatedly explains that Ache cannot promise another person's work, property, wages or consent. The underlying ethic is sound; by I.6 it risks becoming a predictable dramatic engine rather than a changing conflict. **Acceptance:** map each repetition and retain only those that introduce a new cost, choice, relationship or reversal. Protect Sima's explicit rebuke and the unpaid-wage distinction. Do not delete lines solely because they use similar vocabulary.

### I.5 — *The Boat That Stayed*

**Verified causal arc:** A merchant shifts work to Tondo while the organizer's nephew still has no wages. Ache asks Bulkan for guards, Sulad's uncle for a boat, his mother for stores and permission, Ula for a messenger, and Sulad for companionship. Each has independent obligations and offers less than Ache wants. The mother permits inquiry but no guaranteed voyage; Ula has no confirmed carrier or captain; Sulad has not consented; the steward returns with Tondo's proposed crew terms.

**Strength to protect:** The young rower challenges Ache's ability to notice an unpaid worker. Bulkan refuses to become a political guard, Sulad's uncle cannot sacrifice fishing days, Ula will not convert an inquiry into a summons, and Sulad asks whether Ache wants his company or his obedience. The fish shared at the end is a modest emotional turn, and the final boatlessness is causally honest.

**P1 diagnostic RA-LIT-07:** The chapter contains five related request/qualification/refusal sequences. Their accumulation gives each supporting character agency but may flatten suspense because the reader can predict the answer before Ache asks. **Acceptance:** compare the five scenes' unique dramatic information and emotional stakes; consider compression or a more surprising counteraction where the manuscript permits it, without fabricating a guaranteed captain, changing the conditional maternal permission or erasing independent consent.

### I.6 — *No Boat Owed Us*

**Verified causal arc:** Ula returns Ache's unagreed silver; an intermediary cannot locate the unnamed captain but directs him to a different trader. The trader offers only a conditional first-leg fare. Ache's mother separates her own gold from household grain and other people's labor. The unpaid rower obtains separately paid new work without extinguishing his earlier debt. Sulad negotiates directly, obtains his uncle's understanding, chooses to go, and contributes his own silver. The two buy passage, depart, and arrive at a first landing without shelter or onward transport; they earn food moving rejected cargo.

**Strength to protect:** The mother's two gold pieces and crooked stitches give departure a personal cost. Sulad's decision is made to his uncle before Ache hears it; his own money and refusal to be both fare-paying passenger and unpaid crew are meaningful agency. The first landing denies Ache any triumphant arrival; the buyer's damaged basket creates a new economic problem.

**P1 diagnostic RA-LIT-08:** At roughly 10,715 lexical words, I.6 is longer than I.1–5 combined in several pairings and revisits the 'do not promise what you do not own' principle through Ula, the intermediary, the trader, Ache's mother, Sulad, Bulkan and a boatman at the destination. The first-leg booking, departure and arrival all matter; however, the number of separate interrogations may weaken their cumulative emotional force. **Acceptance:** make a scene/beat map and mark each repeated negotiation's *new* information or consequence; trial-compress redundant turns while preserving the mother's gold, Sulad's autonomous choice, actual departure, and first-shore vulnerability. Assess the revised sequence against the unedited version before any public prose PR.

**Continuity checks passed within I.1–6:** The fisherman's damaged stakes receive labor in I.3–4; the injured rower's food and open claim are acknowledged in I.2–3; the Tondo crew-mark question from I.3 is answered in I.4; the first-voyage rower's unpaid wages survive I.4–6 despite his new paid task; the captain from I.3 remains unnamed/unfound; I.5 promises no boat and I.6 purchases only the first leg; Sulad's consent is earned, not assumed. These are local textual checks, not a whole-trilogy continuity certification.

**First confirmed cross-chapter editorial risk:** the same consent/property/authority teaching pattern repeats through I.4–6, even as stakes shift. This warrants a structural experiment after the remaining Book I chapters have been read. Do not immediately rewrite or cut: I.6 also contains the strongest early emotional payoff and the first physical departure.

### I.7 — *The Length of a Rope*

**Verified causal arc:** After first-leg arrival, Ache and Sulad earn one night's shelter by carrying water and tying a roof. At the landing, Mulaw's crew needs a post while Nima protects his absent sister's wet cloth and a fisherman needs his net-drying ground. Ache spends their remaining silver hiring a pole-handler before securing permission for any place to put the cloth; the paid man does his agreed work, but the problem remains. Ache repeatedly assumes Sulad will carry baskets. A narrower agreement eventually lets the boat tie briefly and repair its covering without unloading the cloth. Sulad chooses to carry three baskets, and Mulaw leaves after giving only his name and advice, no onward berth.

**Strengths to protect:** The lost silver has a concrete, unrecoverable cost; Nima's sister's property and the fisherman's net remain independent interests; the narrow mooring agreement is operationally credible; Sulad's three-basket limit and uncertainty about staying are real choices. Mulaw's name does not equal an alliance or booked passage.

**Confirmed P1 RA-LIT-09, escalating RA-LIT-06–08:** Six scene sections, many returning to the same wet-cloth/post dispute, restate that Ache cannot promise another person's space, cargo or hands. The sheltering woman's objection, Nima's objection, the fisherman's objection and Sulad's objection are individually credible, but several consecutive exchanges reiterate the same correction after Ache has acknowledged it. The book risks training the reader to expect a pedagogical refusal rather than a surprising reversal. **Acceptance:** produce a beat map distinguishing new information, actual cost and change in relationship for each return to the landing; trial-compress or restructure only redundant turns, preserving the silver loss, Nima's sister, the fisherman's claim, the agreed repair and Sulad's three freely chosen baskets. Check whether a reader can predict the later dialogue from the first refusal.

### I.8 — *The Unfinished Edge*

**Verified causal arc:** Ache moves six baskets for breakfast; a jar carrier cannot accommodate two additional people and does not accept speculative future payment. Ache carries three sound empty jars for a meal while the owner herself handles a cracked fourth. Sulad separately learns mat repair and secures a possible paid apprenticeship. The woman who housed them must reclaim the eaves when her sister returns; they lose shelter that night. The mat-mender's customer offers conditional passage to the next landing for loading and unloading mats, with food earned separately. Sulad explicitly chooses only one more landing, despite the opportunity to remain and learn a trade.

**Strengths to protect:** The returning sister is a genuine external displacement, not a moral lesson; Sulad's independent livelihood creates an emotionally credible alternative to Ache's journey; his one-landing consent matters more because he has something to decline. The conditional mat-boat agreement advances the route without inventing a guaranteed Brunei connection.

**Confirmed P1 RA-LIT-10:** The jar-carrier refusal, the jar-owner's payment negotiation, the roof's limited permission and the mat-carrier's conditional terms echo I.6–7's repeated contract/consent lesson. Nearly all movement toward the next destination occurs late, after extensive repetitions of bargaining. **Acceptance:** compare scene-level narrative return for the basket, jar and mat sequences; retain the sister's arrival and Sulad's vocational alternative, but test whether the early exchanges can be compressed or whether a materially different obstacle should replace repetition. Avoid flattening historically plausible logistics into a frictionless voyage.

**Character gate RA-LIT-11:** Sulad's mat-mending competence and ability to remain ashore are a stronger independent arc than merely rebuking Ache. Trace whether later books allow him to develop skills, loyalties and consequences beyond companion work. **Acceptance:** record subsequent paid work, autonomous decisions and any actual sacrifice of his own opportunity.

### I.9 — *Seven on the Board*

**Verified causal arc:** The mat carrier takes both men one landing and pays for agreed work. At the next boat, a sick crewman must be housed at the leader's expense; Ache proves useful but traps a new line beneath the jar rack, delaying departure. The leader reports both his success and error. On a larger vessel, Daya's single cloth lot is split into two separately tracked parcels; Ache learns to watch coverings rather than improvise repairs. A later wax-and-fish crossing encounters rain and a trapped crewman's wrist. In the rescue, one of eight wax pieces is lost. The receiver records seven accepted, one missing; Sulad empties their drinking jar to bail the boat, and the two exchange both food packets for clean water.

**Strengths to protect:** This chapter finally introduces material consequences beyond refused permission: a delay caused by Ache, a real injury, lost wax and the immediate food/water trade-off. The wax owner's truthful account avoids simplistic blame; Daya's lot-versus-parcels distinction is a concrete tension between bookkeeping and ownership. The storm puts labor knowledge under bodily pressure.

**P1 RA-LIT-12:** The first several sections still repeat instruction on rope, load ownership, and the limited scope of each passage. The injury/wax loss and food-for-water consequence deliver the book's clearest mid-voyage dramatic escalation but arrive relatively late. **Acceptance:** map how long readers wait for the consequential storm and whether earlier negotiations can be condensed without weakening the causal logic of the rescue or the skill Ache brings to it. Protect the unresolved young crewman's wrist and the owner's uncompensated eighth wax piece; check for genuine downstream effects, not a reset to the next contract.

### I.10 — *Before the First Load*

**Verified causal arc:** The anchorage's fish seller will repeat Ache's name to a cloth buyer only if he arrives. Ache and Sulad work aboard that buyer's Brunei-bound carrier, arriving with no letter, army or promised introduction. A receiving official verifies only what Sulad can personally attest. Siripada receives Ache and acknowledges knowledge of his mother but declines to send force on Ache's incomplete account of Maynila. Ache accepts work at a receiving platform, mistakenly accuses Daran of losing a bundle he had simply not seen placed beyond a post, publicly corrects himself and gains a specific job. His message to his mother is witnessed but **has no named carrier and has not been sent**; Sulad separately chooses landing work and asks that any message from him be his own. The chapter ends as Ache counts the first bundle off a boat.

**Strengths to protect:** The physical Brunei arrival and guarded audience fulfill the geographic objective without a fantasy rescue fleet. Siripada's knowledge of Ache's mother gives a personal hint without making him omniscient. Daran has grounds to resent the public accusation; Ache's correction costs pride. Sulad's autonomy survives the arrival. The message's precise unsent state creates a useful Book II promise.

**P1 structural gate RA-LIT-13:** The core Book I **journey-to-Brunei** conflict reaches a clear resolution, while the opening **Maynila/Tondo political threat** is expressly deferred. This may be an effective first-book ending if the trilogy promises an apprenticeship and return; it may feel dramatically underpowered if the reader expects a decisive political confrontation in Book I. The final receiving-platform knot echoes the opening chapter's lessons in observation and responsibility but also risks repeating the trilogy's dominant ethical lesson rather than delivering a distinct climax. **Acceptance:** inspect the published book-level promise and Book II opening, then test with independent readers whether the arrival, audience, denied force and new role amount to a satisfying Book I climax. Do not invent a victory, army or premature message delivery.

**P1 voice gate RA-LIT-14:** The recurring 'That is not what I asked' interrogation occurs again with the fish seller, cloth buyer and Siripada, after many comparable question/answer corrections in I.1–9. **Acceptance:** quantify and read these turns in context across Book I; keep unique interrogations that reveal character or alter stakes, and test alternatives to repeated verbal instruction. Preserve Siripada's guarded questioning and the distinction between Ache's hearsay and witnessed facts.

### Book I synthesis and Phase 2 experiment gate

**Confirmed across I.4–10:** Consent, evidence, ownership and limited obligations are deliberately recurring themes. In I.7–8 especially, multiple scenes use the same dramatic procedure: Ache requests a resource, another person qualifies the request, Ache learns the limit, and the journey continues after a smaller bargain. This produces credible material culture and supporting-character agency but a sustained risk of low surprise and delayed escalation. I.9 demonstrates a more effective variant when a bodily injury, missing commodity and sacrificed food alter the protagonists' situation. I.10 supplies an earned arrival but no political rescue. The initial concern from I.1–6 is **strengthened**, not yet a license to rewrite unexamined later books.

**Proposed first controlled revision experiment, not yet authorized prose:** Draft two alternate beat maps for I.7–8 and compare with the current text: (A) compress repeated negotiations while preserving every actual cost and independent choice; (B) change the order or dramatic emphasis to foreground the silver loss, sister's return, Sulad's viable trade and next-boat opportunity. Assess what each scene contributes to suspense, character, historical texture and continuity. Prefer a reversible Studio experiment before any public chapter edit. A successful revision must preserve Nima's absent sister, Mulaw's limited offer, the host's sister's return, Sulad's separate earnings and conditional next-leg choice, the I.9 departure, and all source boundaries.

**Book I continuity promises to track in Book II:** Siripada's help is not a pledged force; the mother's message is only witnessed, not dispatched; Sulad's personal message is requested but not yet added or sent; Daran's public accusation has been corrected but their working relationship is nascent; Mulaw is a passing boat leader rather than a confirmed patron; I.9's injured crewman and missing wax have no proven restitution. The original Spanish Aganduru passage remains uncollated. These are factual text states, not guarantees that later books resolve them.

### II.1 — *The Other End of the Cord*

**Six-scene causal map:** (1) At Siripada's receiving platform a merchant blames a young carrier for a cracked bowl found inside a covered basket; Ache refuses to assert that he saw the break. The merchant hints at a possible Maynila contact. (2) The packer discloses an earlier movement of the basket aboard; the carrier demands the originally agreed wage, and the merchant conditions the possible introduction on Ache's testimony. (3) A second basket is opened; the merchant leaves the first and its cracked bowl until the next tide, with the carrier still unpaid. (4) Rising water forces Ache and Daran to move the disputed basket indoors before witnesses; the carrier leaves temporarily to seek other work. (5) The merchant returns, retrieves the basket, again offers less than the agreed wage, and departs when the carrier refuses. Ache finally asks the carrier's name: Idris. (6) Idris independently negotiates and receives payment for a separate basket-moving job, while the first debt remains open; Ache is assigned to keep a rear passage clear. Sulad works independently on a platform and reminds Ache that his personal message has still not been added before witnesses.

**Strengths to preserve:** The merchant's contingent promise of a Maynila contact tempts Ache to sacrifice the limits of his testimony; this is a genuinely personal conflict. Idris's refusal of reduced pay and later independent bargain have economic and psychological credibility. The rising water makes the disputed basket's custody physically consequential. Daran's response to the earlier false accusation carries Book I's correction forward. Ache asking Idris's name only at the end can land emotionally if the preceding material earns the delay.

**Confirmed P1 RA-LIT-15:** A single cracked-bowl dispute dominates five of six long scenes, repeatedly returning to the same witness-limit and wage question. The merchant's attempts to extract blame and the woman's/keeper's refusal recur with little change in leverage until the late reduced-wage offer. The new water hazard and Idris's separate paid work are distinct developments, but a reader may correctly predict most intermediate answers. **Acceptance:** map each revisit by changed evidence, bargaining power, cost or relationship; test a shorter alternative that preserves the disputed cause, the conditional Maynila-contact temptation, custody through the rising tide, Idris's *unpaid* original wage, and his separately *paid* new job. Do not invent proof of when the bowl cracked or imply the debt was settled.

**Character gate RA-LIT-16:** Idris is named only after the merchant has departed. Test whether the withheld name deepens Ache's realization or delays the reader's investment unnecessarily. Preserve Idris's right to reject an unfair settlement and leave; he should not exist solely to teach Ache a lesson.

### II.2 — *A Place on the List*

**Six-scene causal map:** (1) Ache again checks the witnessed but uncarried message and learns that Sulad must give his own words in person. He prematurely assures a food supplier that two baskets have a place aboard an upper-bend boat. (2) Jamil and Safi inspect the boat's already accepted cargo and repair needs; the promised space does not exist without displacing prior loads. (3) Safi devises a feasible load sequence, while Ache proposes unpaid extra hands and invokes Siripada; Jamil adopts Safi's plan. (4) The food owner refuses the baskets for lack of a receiving party at the destination; the woman's delay and spoiling food are real losses, and a separate carrier secures payment before lifting the last accepted sack. (5) The supplier leaves without delivery; Laila personally negotiates extra meals for two workers from her own store, and Hasan refuses to convert Ache's private intention into a publicly witnessed promise. (6) Jamil gives Ache a narrow task recording an unrepaired boat's actual workers and missing peg; Safi receives responsibility for accepted loads. The mother's message remains without a carrier, and Sulad has not spoken his addition before witnesses.

**Strengths to preserve:** The woman's time-sensitive food creates a consequence that cannot be corrected by revising a tally; the boy's separate marks make the misunderstanding visible. Safi earns his task through demonstrated competence, creating a credible competitive foil. 3. **Soliman seeks to defend the river mouth.** Ache urges a reconnaissance rather than a premature attack; the younger ruler sends consenting watchers.
4. **Watchers return with partial evidence.** They see a large vessel and apparent gun ports, hear a local-language speaker, but cannot count the fleet or identify him.
5. **Mehomete's first-person transfer reveals divided loyalties.** His brother wants to see his family; Mehomete serves foreigners for wages, passage home and a belief that accurate interpretation may prevent violence.
6. **The officer's peace offer carries a hidden demand.** Mehomete challenges the ambiguity between safety, friendship and control over Maynila's guns.
7. **Mehomete's brother goes ashore separately.** He refuses foreign rowers and any claim to represent all households; the boatman is paid for a bounded journey.
8. **The brother does not promptly return.** The officer expects an answer on a ship's timetable; Mehomete cannot promise a household's decision.
9. **Ache hears of a local-language messenger and a Chinese steering dispute.** A captain claims his gear was held over an unpaid lighter charge; the absent original bargain is contested.
10. **Lian refuses to become the involuntary custodian of the steering piece.** Her shed and son are at risk; the captain's man cannot simply take the wood without implicating her.
11. **Pablo, a Japanese resident, rejects easy religious inference.** Knowing the foreigners' prayers does not reveal their intentions; he keeps repairing a basket for a buyer.
12. **The steering piece is moved to a lighter with competing witnesses.** Lian regains her shed, but the debt is delayed, not settled. The messenger asks for a hearing at the gate.
13. **The armed commander enters under a peace offer.** Soliman agrees to hear him; an embrace mistakes Ache's seniority for authority. Armed men remain near the landing.
14. **A blood compact and provision/tribute language remain ambiguous.** Ache asks how households can promise food not grown; the commander gives no precise answer.
15. **Rumors of northern boats and conflicting gun orders escalate.** Ache cannot reconstruct the first shot from sound and smoke; Soliman must act while the guns fire.
16. **Foreign soldiers storm and burn houses.** Ache sees a soldier lift a brand to a storehouse; he helps Lian and her child flee, but loses sight of other people. His knowledge of ownership cannot stop armed destruction.
17. **After the fire, a clerk reduces contested houses to names.** A woman brings two strips proving separate claims, but the clerk wants a list, not a hearing.
18. **Lakandula and Soliman face coerced new terms.** Each can speak only for particular people and boats; no joint sovereign settlement is established.
19. **The commander demands renewed friendship under occupation.** A soldier pays for water only after the owner challenges him; the leaders leave without guaranteeing a fair complaints process.
20. **A rower reports the northern-channel battle.** He cannot identify the leader or confirm two missing companions; Soliman requests names rather than an invented death count.
21. **A woman searches for her husband; wounded men need shelter.** Her canoe returns without him; a householder offers a shed with explicit food and water needs.
22. **Lakandula's messenger carries distinct missing-person accounts.** A foreign officer seeks a list of fighters that might wrongly include people who supplied food or rescued wounded.
23. **The canoe is recovered without its missing user.** The owner hires carriers and refuses an invented recovery; the final image preserves her independent claim amid foreign posts. **Bonus function:** historically anchored loss of political autonomy and lives, not the core book's postponed political victory.

**Strengths:** The move from the mundane landing to military coercion gives the procedural theme its most forceful counterexample: clear witness testimony cannot prevent fire. Mehomete's competing family and interpreter obligations introduce a genuinely divided intermediary. Soliman has urgent motives independent of Ache. The woman's missing husband and recovered canoe resist tidy consolation.

**Risks:** The first fourteen scenes still repeat who heard whom, whose cargo, who pays and what can be promised, delaying the dramatic arrival of violence. The foreign steering-piece dispute is plausible texture but occupies several scenes during a time-critical threat. Mehomete's phrasing and Ache's phrasing often share the same explanatory ethical cadence. Ache's physical age and fear enter too late relative to the scene count. The chapter's uncertain first shot is historically responsible; its drama should not be weakened by unnecessary procedural recitals.

### III.12 — *The Roof After Rain*: 16-scene death and aftermath map

1. **The canoe owner confronts a foreign written seizure.** The clerk credits Ache's objection instead of the woman's claim; she secures release but her missing husband remains unknown. Ache's failing strength is visible.
2. **Awi faces a leaking roof and Ache's unauthorized room promise.** Her own bowl, his mother's dented bowl, a displaced family and a scarce maker create incompatible needs; she insists on choosing whom to house.
3. **Awi hires a roof inspection; a priest offers baptismal instruction.** Ache challenges what renunciation and conversion would require, refusing to call the first conversation baptism. Awi maintains the maker's paid work.
4. **The repaired roof survives its first rain as Ache becomes ill.** He is dizzy, feverish and unable to keep a promised visit.
5. **Awi arranges payment for a boatman and medical help.** She refuses to make the creditor wait for Ache's pride; her labor carries the consequence.
6. **Soliman brings new foreign property questions.** Ache cannot attend; Awi speaks for her own rooms, and Soliman begins asking owners directly.
7. **Awi offers a displaced family three nights and hires a healer.** The household's future becomes a live choice made without Ache.
8. **The healer finds illness without a guaranteed cure.** Ache requests the priest again but still wants to understand the words.
9. **Ache chooses baptism after translated questions.** He distinguishes religious confession from an officer's coercion and keeps some doubt; the event is not evidence of historically recoverable private belief.
10. **Ache offers his mother's dented bowl to Awi with a witness.** He explicitly releases any demand for future care or preservation; she accepts and owns the choice to sell, use or keep it.
11. **Ache's final dusk is marked by fatigue and the household continuing to work.** He cannot summon a final universal command.
12. **Awi assumes first-person narration.** Ache stops asking about tasks; she manages the family and calls for those he asked to see.
13. **Ache dies with Soliman and the priest present.** The healer confirms death; the market continues outside. Awi's grief is restrained but specific.
14. **A relative disputes the copper bowl.** Awi produces her witness and refuses to surrender the gift merely because a male heir is alleged.
15. **Burial rites are negotiated among different believers.** The canoe owner still lacks news of her husband and asks Awi to carry her name accurately.
16. **Awi continues housing decisions and uses the dented bowl.** The child's ordinary washing and the roof holding provide a modest, concrete aftermath. **Ending:** Awi's own authority and the household's continuity, not Ache's posthumous command.

**Strengths:** Awi's voice has a credible material vantage point, practical agency and consequences; the bowl transfer is one of the trilogy's clearest earned emotional objects. Ache's dependence reverses his youthful assumption that he can direct a household. The baptism scene separates recorded external action from unverifiable private certainty, and Awi's aftermath resists a sentimental last speech.

**Risks:** The priest, interpreter, roof maker and Awi often deliver another sequence of precise consent questions that recalls the earlier landing lessons. The mother is evoked in memory and in the bowl, but the story never resolves the Chapter III.1 promise that Amaya could seek news of her. The death and bowl inheritance are powerful enough to justify a shorter lead-in; test whether the first three very long scenes delay the physical decline. Do not turn Awi into Ache's moral lecturer or pretend the historical sources document the invented household details.
